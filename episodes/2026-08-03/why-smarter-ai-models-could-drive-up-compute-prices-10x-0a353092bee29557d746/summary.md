# Why smarter AI models could drive up compute prices 10x

- Podcast: Dwarkesh Podcast
- Published: 2026-08-03
- Source: https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/why-compute-might-get-10x-more-expensive-video
- Relevance: 5/5

In the matching essay linked from the episode metadata, Patel argues that AI revenue and monetizable capability may grow much faster than compute supply, bidding scarce frontier capacity sharply higher before manufacturing catches up. He combines a 10x-versus-3x revenue/compute framing with reported premium-capacity prices, a supply-growth decomposition, and forecasts of stronger frontier-lab concentration and pressure on low-value applications.

**Why it matters:** The argument reverses the default assumption that better hardware will steadily make AI cheaper. In Patel's near-term scenario, capability growth raises willingness to pay faster than supply expands, shifting value toward scarce compute, efficient frontier models, and incumbent labs while compressing weaker models and low-value applications; several inputs are explicitly speculative and need verification.

## Signals

- **Patel argues that sustaining Anthropic's reported 10x annual revenue growth against roughly 3x annual lab-compute growth would require some combination of higher margins, more expensive compute, and a larger inference share.** _companies_capital_allocation; inference; medium confidence._ He estimates Anthropic could end the year at $100-150 billion of revenue and notes that another 10x would imply $1 trillion the following year, explicitly presenting this as a capability-dependent scenario rather than a base-case fact.
- **Patel says the revenue-to-compute adjustment is already appearing in inference margins and compute allocation, though his latest figures are estimates.** _frontier_labs_models; observation; low confidence._ He says Anthropic's Fable inference margin rose from about 40% in 2025 to probably above 80%, while OpenAI's inference share moved from roughly one quarter of 2024 compute spend to closer to one half or more; he labels the Fable estimate a vibe claim.
- **Patel says the secure, large-scale compute tranche frontier labs need is already priced well above headline spot markets.** _infrastructure_energy; observation; medium confidence._ He cites reports that Google pays SpaceX $900 million per month for 110,000 blended GB200 and GB300 GPUs, about twice spot pricing, while spot itself had risen more than 40% from its February trough.
- **Patel argues that annual AI-compute supply growth near 3x has limited room to accelerate and that leading-edge wafer reallocation will soon saturate.** _semiconductors_compute; inference; medium confidence._ He decomposes growth into about 1.4x from Moore's Law, 1.2x from new fabs constrained by EUV-tool supply through at least 2030, and 1.8x from reallocating leading-edge wafers; he expects AI's N3 share to rise from 60% to 86% by end-2027.
- **Patel forecasts that a human-level software engineer running on an H100-equivalent could support compute rent above $250,000 per year, roughly 15x current spot pricing.** _applications_business_models; forecast; low confidence._ He maps current software-engineer compensation onto one accelerator and argues that specialization and innovation could preserve high marginal labor value despite a large supply of AI workers, while acknowledging the shock may break that analogy.
- **Patel predicts high compute prices would amplify the advantage of the most capable and compute-efficient frontier models.** _companies_capital_allocation; forecast; medium confidence._ At a hypothetical $20 per H100-hour, he argues that weaker models would waste costly tokens, allowing the best model to charge a larger premium and making it harder for revenue-free entrants to acquire compute and catch up.
- **Patel expects sharply higher compute prices to displace low-value AI applications even as high-value labor substitution remains economical.** _applications_business_models; forecast; low confidence._ He uses short-form video generation as an example of demand that could be priced out once scarce accelerators can instead perform work valued near top human labor, while conceding that markets may find ways to economize the scarce input.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Near-term compute prices could rise despite hardware progress if model capability increases willingness to pay faster than physical supply expands.
- Scarce expensive inference can strengthen the margin premium for the most token-efficient frontier model rather than commoditize model providers.
- The leading-edge wafer-allocation lever may approach saturation by end-2027, leaving Moore's Law and fab-tool-constrained construction as the main supply-growth channels.

## Follow-Ups

- Verify Anthropic's revenue, Fable margins, and Google-SpaceX capacity contract against primary company disclosures or counterpart reporting.
- Rebuild the 3x compute-supply decomposition from foundry capacity, EUV shipments, packaging, power, networking, and accelerator-performance data.
- Track whether accelerator rental prices and frontier API margins rise together or whether efficiency gains and new supply absorb capability-driven demand.
