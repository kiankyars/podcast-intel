# Who Feeds the GPUs? Inside AI's Hidden $30B Layer | Renen Hallak, VAST Data

- Podcast: The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck
- Published: 2026-09-24
- Source: https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/Who-Feeds-the-GPUs--Inside-AIs-Hidden-30B-Layer--Renen-Hallak--VAST-Data-e3palb8
- Relevance: 4/5

VAST Data CEO Renen Hallak describes rapid expansion in customers' storage plans, an existing multi-exabyte cluster, strong customer retention, and a profitable software model. The most consequential product development is DataEnclave, intended to bring proprietary models into enterprise-controlled environments while protecting both weights and data. VAST's primary announcement clarifies that it is a preview with shipment planned for Q1 2027. Customer demand, deployment scale, retention, and profitability remain executive-reported; the interview does not quantify revenue, bookings, backlog, or audited margins.

**Why it matters:** The interview strengthens the case for a software supplier benefiting from AI infrastructure growth without owning the underlying compute fleet. It also identifies a potential expansion from storage into model distribution and governance. Capacity plans must convert into funded orders, while DataEnclave must reach shipment and customer deployment before its promised market expansion can be counted as realized business.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- The customer capacity anecdote supports accelerating infrastructure planning, but the additional 2 EB must not be mistaken for a 2 EB total, a completed deployment, or contracted business.
- DataEnclave advances VAST's model-management strategy, but the primary-source Q1 2027 shipment plan moves any production-adoption inference into the future.
- VAST's claimed retention and profitability make the software layer a distinct way to assess AI infrastructure economics; neither stored-data growth nor the host's valuation figure establishes revenue growth.

## Follow-Ups

- Determine how much of the revised 2.5 EB customer plan is funded, contractually committed, delivered, and ultimately utilized; identify customer concentration and cancellation exposure.
- Track DataEnclave shipment against Q1 2027 and obtain named production deployments, supported model licenses, security attestations, performance overhead, and pricing before attributing incremental revenue to it.
- Seek independently supportable revenue, bookings, backlog, cash flow, profitability basis, and gross/net dollar retention, keeping those measures separate from data-volume expansion and valuation.
- Request workload-level evidence for the multi-exabyte cluster and examine how much of the inference-routing, agent identity, and policy-enforcement stack is deployed at paying customers.
