# Jimmy Iovine on AI: “The Great Artists Will Show the World How to Use It”

- Podcast: TBPN
- Published: 2026-09-02
- Source: https://share.transistor.fm/s/dcf3a038
- Relevance: 4/5

Jimmy Iovine offers a first-party music-industry strategy view of AI: producers he knows already use it to generate ideas and break creative logjams, while rights negotiations could constrain the catalogs available to licensed models. He argues streaming platforms still lack the artist-to-fan social layer the industry needs, recounts an unsuccessful effort by his Apple Music team to pursue Musical.ly, and says labels should use AI to own products rather than repeat their value-destructive pattern of licensing catalogs to platforms. The episode is strategically useful but mostly qualitative: it supplies no model benchmarks, adoption metrics, transaction records, or concrete product economics.

**Why it matters:** The interview connects three issues that are often considered separately: training-data rights can affect both AI-music economics and model quality; professional adoption may begin as expert workflow augmentation rather than artist replacement; and control of distribution and fan relationships determines who captures the value. Iovine's account suggests labels have an opportunity to own part of the AI application layer, but their public-company incentives and history of accepting near-term licensing checks may push them toward repeating the streaming-era mistake.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Licensed access to music catalogs may be both a cost and a capability constraint for AI-music products, not merely a legal-compliance issue.
- Iovine's network-level observation points to professional AI-music adoption beginning with producer augmentation and ideation rather than one-click replacement of expert creators.
- Streaming's strategic weakness may include its failure to own the artist-to-fan relationship, not only its payout structure.
- Apple's institutional aversion to social products appears to have outweighed an internal attempt to add Musical.ly's social layer to Apple Music.
- Labels have an AI-era vertical-integration option, but the incentives that led them to favor streaming licensing checks could keep them from capturing it.
