# The Programmable Data Center — Evan Conrad, SF Compute

- Podcast: Latent Space
- Published: 2026-10-20
- Source: https://www.latent.space/p/sfcompute2
- Relevance: 4/5

Evan Conrad describes SF Compute's move from GPU subleasing into operating clusters and settling trades in usable compute. The most useful evidence is an early resale metric, the constraints preventing GPU-hours from becoming fully interchangeable, and self-service InfiniBand partitions with narrower capabilities than the interview's 'on the fly' phrasing suggests. His financing and market-neutrality arguments are strategic theses, not proof that competing venues cannot succeed.

**Why it matters:** A compute exchange's value depends on whether customers can recover unused commitments and receive equivalent, reliable capacity. That makes storage, networking, control-plane operations and contractual interruption rights part of market design. Investors and infrastructure buyers should distinguish signed contract value, actual trading liquidity and repeatable customer savings, and evaluate which physical constraints the software truly removes.

## Signals

- **Conrad reports meaningful resale recovery from a still-small order book: customers recover about 25% of spend, while only 5% of SF Compute capacity is on the book.** [05:45; 06:21] _companies_capital_allocation; observation; medium confidence._ The 5% figure is his interview snapshot. SF Compute's September 9 [realized-discount announcement](https://sfcompute.com/news/realized-discount) also reports average resale proceeds equal to 25% of on-platform compute purchases. It does not publish cohort size or a distribution of outcomes. This is recovery of unused capacity costs, not a universal reduction in the price of an equivalent workload or evidence of deep market-wide liquidity.
- **Conrad says customers can program their own InfiniBand partitions; current documentation confirms self-service control but requires instance recreation to change membership.** [34:30; 35:06] _semiconductors_compute; observation; high confidence._ The September 25 [launch post](https://sfcompute.com/news/elastic-infiniband-partitions) describes isolated networks managed through CLI/API. [Current documentation](https://docs.sfcompute.com/preview/networking/infiniband-partitions) marks the feature public preview, requires instances to share one physical fabric, and says moving an instance to another partition requires terminating and recreating it. This makes cluster configuration programmable; it does not establish interruption-free repartitioning or connectivity across separate fabrics.
- **Conrad says SF Compute matches orders across acceptable configurations rather than waiting for a universal GPU commodity contract.** [08:42; 09:18; 22:31; 23:08] _semiconductors_compute; observation; high confidence._ Buyers can express multiple acceptable properties; Conrad acknowledges that this fragments liquidity and widens spreads. His distinction between an existing query mechanism and newly developing named queries matters. SF Compute's [Abel ledger description](https://sfcompute.com/news/abel-ledger) corroborates the underlying heterogeneity: it represents different clusters separately and tracks ownership over delivery windows. The implication is that workload portability can have financial value, but its size requires measured execution prices.
- **Conrad argues that interruption rights are a priced resource, with storage and inter-site connectivity determining how much flexibility a workload can offer.** [09:53; 10:31; 11:06; 16:29; 17:05] _infrastructure_energy; inference; medium confidence._ He contrasts batch jobs tolerating short notice with live services needing months, and ties recovery to checkpoint storage and transit between clusters. These are workload-dependent examples, not published standard lease terms. The useful inference is that a low GPU-hour quote can conceal substantial restart and service-continuity costs; compare delivered work and recovery time before valuing a preemptible discount.
- **Conrad describes an asset-independent operator with substantial physical responsibilities, rather than a marketplace that only matches buyers and sellers.** [29:10; 29:46; 31:33; 33:20; 34:30; 36:18] _infrastructure_energy; observation; high confidence._ He says SF Compute operates cluster infrastructure without owning the hardware and retains the SLA. Its September 10 [commercial announcement](https://sfcompute.com/news/tcv-q3-2026) confirms that it operates leased clusters and reports two 36-month B300 agreements totaling $245 million. That is contract value, not annual revenue or order-book turnover. His contention that independence prevents conflicts is a business-model thesis; the operational workload still introduces delivery and execution risk.
- **Conrad warns that compute derivatives can be poor hedges if their reference prices are disconnected from capacity buyers can actually obtain.** [23:44; 24:59; 26:12] _companies_capital_allocation; opinion; medium confidence._ His argument favors physical delivery as the foundation for later cash-settled instruments. Treat the proposed index mismatch and manipulation risk as his assessment, not a demonstrated incident. For underwriting, the practical test is whether a benchmark tracks the buyer's specific hardware, location, service conditions and delivery window; a broad GPU index may leave those risks uncovered.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- GPU-market infrastructure deserves to be evaluated as an operating capability: reliable delivery, workload recovery and control-plane authority can determine whether a quoted contract is useful.
- Resale is a plausible way to soften long-term commitment risk, but a company-reported average recovery rate does not establish exit liquidity for every customer, configuration or market condition.
- Programmable networking removes provisioning friction within specific physical boundaries. SF Compute's documented instance-recreation requirement materially narrows the idea of changing InfiniBand partitions on the fly.

## Follow-Ups

- Request the population, period, median and downside distribution behind the 25% recovery metric, plus time-to-sale and realized proceeds for large future reservations. Track whether results persist as more than 5% of capacity reaches the order book.
- Measure effective cost per completed job across interruptible and reserved capacity, including checkpoint writes, inter-cluster transfer, restart time and customer SLA exposure.
- Test InfiniBand partition provisioning and teardown under concurrent workloads; distinguish adding newly created instances from migrating already-running work, and monitor progress beyond public preview.
- Validate Conrad's 19:28 B300 comparison before using it in a model: he contrasts roughly $40 per hour of inference revenue at full utilization with $5–6 rental cost, but supplies no model, throughput, token price or complete operating-cost bridge. His roughly 50% utilization / $25 revenue illustration is also not arithmetically consistent with a simple halving of $40. This is an unverified economics claim, not demonstrated arbitrage.
- Track delivery and revenue recognition against the announced $245 million of 36-month contracts, separately from secondary trading volume; ask how hardware-owner interests, market access and settlement rules are governed.
