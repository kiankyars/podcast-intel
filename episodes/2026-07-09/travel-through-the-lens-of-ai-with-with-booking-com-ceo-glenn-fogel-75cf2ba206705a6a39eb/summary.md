# Travel Through the Lens of AI with with Booking.com CEO Glenn Fogel

- Podcast: No Priors
- Published: 2026-07-09
- Source: 
- Relevance: 4/5

Booking CEO Glenn Fogel frames AI travel agents as a practical operating layer rather than a near-term platform disintermediation threat. The transcript includes concrete adoption, scale, cost, and capital-allocation details around Priceline's Penny and Booking's AI investments.

**Why it matters:** Travel is a useful test case for agentic commerce because it has messy inventory, real-world failure recovery, merchant-of-record complexity, and high customer-service load. Booking's comments show where incumbents believe their supply, service, and transaction scale still matter.

## Signals

- **Fogel rejects the simple thesis that general AI agents will make travel companies obsolete.** [11:34] _applications_business_models; opinion; medium confidence._ He says outsiders underestimated merchant-of-record and travel complexity, and says OpenAI's retreat from in-app commerce made investors reassess the disintermediation risk.
- **Priceline's Penny is already showing agent-commerce adoption signal, but still at small absolute scale.** [19:37] _agents_developer_tools; observation; medium confidence._ The host says Penny adoption doubled each month for the past few months and improved conversion, search speed, cancellation, and customer success; Fogel responds that the absolute numbers are still small.
- **Booking is gating agent rollout on token economics, model routing, ROI, and lifetime value rather than demo quality alone.** [20:30] _applications_business_models; observation; high confidence._ Fogel says Booking handled $186B of travel and over a billion room nights last year, so they need to know the cost per AI-booked trip, which model to use for which task, and whether loyalty improves.
- **AI customer service is already reducing per-contact costs while improving satisfaction, but Booking still plans to preserve human support where customers want it.** [21:47] _applications_business_models; observation; high confidence._ Fogel says cost per customer-service contact is down and customer satisfaction is up, then warns against making everything AI-only when some users still need humans.
- **Booking is reinvesting heavily into AI and platform while continuing aggressive capital returns.** [23:24] _companies_capital_allocation; observation; high confidence._ Fogel says roughly $700M is being invested this year across AI, platform, and other technology areas; he also says Booking bought back about 40% of shares over roughly a dozen years.
- **Booking's marketplace scale remains a defense against thin AI wrappers, but Fogel does not treat it as a permanent moat.** [26:01] _applications_business_models; inference; medium confidence._ The discussion cites 8.6M alternative-accommodation listings and about three-quarters Airbnb's alternative-accommodation size; Fogel says AI-intensive entrants still need supply connectivity and that no moat protects against innovation forever.
- **Fogel treats AI job displacement as a speed-mismatch problem that companies need to address through upskilling.** [34:00] _applications_business_models; opinion; medium confidence._ He says Booking's old 40-language translation jobs disappeared after machine translation, worries new AI displacement and job creation may not happen at the same rate, and says Booking is pushing AI literacy training.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Agentic commerce in travel appears constrained less by UI demos than by inventory, merchant-of-record obligations, failure recovery, token economics, and incumbent marketplace connectivity.
- Large incumbents may adopt AI aggressively while still keeping humans in the loop for customer trust and edge cases.
