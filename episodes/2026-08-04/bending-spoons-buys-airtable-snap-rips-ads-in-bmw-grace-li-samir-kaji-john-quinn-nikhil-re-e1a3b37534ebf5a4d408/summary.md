- Relevance: 5/5

An unusually dense episode combines Quinn's first-party account of a reported Perplexity appellate win, Cognition's model-routing and enterprise-deployment lessons, a nine-week Pentagon counter-drone marketplace, expanding FCC hardware-security controls, and Intelligence's 5.6-million-person preference-data flywheel. John Quinn also flags cross-default and labor risk beneath the AI data-center buildout.

**Why it matters:** The common thread is that AI advantage is moving beyond raw model quality into deployment structure: choosing models by metric, reorganizing work around agents, capturing preference data through useful products, and managing legal, procurement, infrastructure, and supply-chain constraints. The reported court ruling and FCC developments could directly shape agent access rights and the U.S. hardware market, while Cognition's examples show organizational design becoming a binding adoption bottleneck.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Human-preference products can generate post-training data without paid labeling when comparison and feedback are intrinsic to the end-user workflow.
- Agentic browsing now has a favorable appellate data point: Quinn says the relevant computer access was attributed to the user deploying the agent, not the agent provider.
- AI data-center counterparty risk is more interconnected than a tenant-credit view suggests because projects layer SPVs, securitizations, private credit, and cross-default provisions.
- Enterprise coding-agent adoption may be constrained more by organizational boundaries than capability when working implementations are discarded to preserve existing role handoffs.
- Model routing should optimize for the required error profile rather than a single leaderboard winner, given Cognition's reported OpenAI recall and Anthropic precision split.
- U.S. robotics and drone supply-chain policy is moving from risk discussion to market-shaping covered-list restrictions and lab-level security checks.
