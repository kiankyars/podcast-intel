# Frontier Chips for Frontier AI Labs, with Walter Goodwin, Founder/CEO of Fractile

- Podcast: No Priors
- Published: 2026-10-02
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OpeCP4wCxkA
- Relevance: 4/5

Fractile CEO Walter Goodwin describes a shift away from the company's initial SRAM-based design toward high-bandwidth access to higher-capacity DRAM, motivated especially by long-context inference. He targets a platform ramp in the second half of 2027. He explains why memory bandwidth can change viable model architectures, why a roughly 150-person team spans workload research through physical design and packaging, and why faster design does not remove fabrication, qualification or capital-recovery constraints. The interview offers consequential first-party architectural direction and planning assumptions, but no measured production performance, detailed memory interface, customer deployment or verified cost advantage.

**Why it matters:** The important comparison is sustained inference at the required context length, concurrency and latency, with enough affordable memory for weights and state. A bandwidth advantage alone cannot establish serving economics. Goodwin's account also separates more rapid architectural experiments from physical product replacement: chips still need time to manufacture, ramp and repay their cost. This makes model-hardware fit and deployment readiness as important as a headline tokens-per-second figure.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- The September 17 HBM discussion emphasized bandwidth per dollar and capacity allocation. Goodwin adds a first-party account of redesigning an inference platform around both bandwidth and affordable long-context capacity; this is not evidence that DRAM capacity ceases to matter.
- Faster AI-assisted chip design need not imply rapid economic obsolescence of installed chips. Goodwin's proposed benefit is a larger set of candidate designs and quicker selection, while fabrication, deployment and capital recovery remain constraints.
- Fractile should be assessed against its current described DRAM direction, rather than assuming its initial SRAM approach remains the complete architecture.
