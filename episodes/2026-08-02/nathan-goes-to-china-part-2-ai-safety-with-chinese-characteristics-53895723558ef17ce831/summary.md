# Nathan Goes to China – Part 2: AI Safety with Chinese Characteristics

- Podcast: The Cognitive Revolution
- Published: 2026-08-02
- Source: https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/nathan-goes-to-china-part-2-ai-safety-with-chinese-characteristics/
- Relevance: 5/5

Labenz's field report presents China as materially behind OpenAI and Anthropic on deployed model safeguards but much more engaged in AI safety than the common 'China will never slow down' argument assumes. He describes a fast-growing, university-centered research ecosystem, an internationally oriented Tsinghua safety hub, service-level regulation led by the CAC, and a history of delaying chatbot launches while standards were created. The evidence also exposes important weaknesses: inconsistent company disclosures, less robust open-weight models, and a domestic-control mindset that may not account for risks exported through globally available weights.

**Why it matters:** The episode changes the strategic baseline for US-China AI policy. China's combination of convergent technical research, centralized service oversight, and demonstrated willingness to impose launch costs suggests that bilateral safety coordination is not inherently futile. At the same time, weaker deployed safeguards, limited company transparency, and open-weight externalities remain concrete gaps, so policy should distinguish the Chinese research community, product companies, and state regulator rather than treating them as one uniformly safety-indifferent actor.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- The current US-China model-safeguard gap appears to be driven disproportionately by OpenAI and Anthropic rather than by a uniformly stronger US ecosystem.
- China's AI-safety ecosystem is becoming substantial but is organized mainly through universities and government supervision rather than independent nonprofits or company-authored risk frameworks.
- The claim that any US safety obligation would automatically cede the race to an unwilling-to-regulate China is harder to sustain given China's documented launch delays, review process, and active safety research.
- China's service-level regulatory model may control domestic deployments more effectively than model-only comparisons imply while leaving the global externalities of open-weight releases under-addressed.
