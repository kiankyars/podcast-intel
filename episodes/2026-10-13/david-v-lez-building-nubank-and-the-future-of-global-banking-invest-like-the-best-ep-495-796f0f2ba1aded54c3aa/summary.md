# David Vélez - Building Nubank and the Future of Global Banking - [Invest Like the Best, EP.495]

- Podcast: Invest Like the Best
- Published: 2026-10-13
- Source: https://colossus.com/episode/david-v-goliath/
- Relevance: 4/5

David Vélez describes Nubank's AI transformation through concrete organizational changes: a redesign covering 120 workflows, management layers reduced from ten to five, a dedicated team studying agents as customers, and a requirement that senior managers build software quarterly. He connects proprietary transaction models and open-finance advice to international expansion. His account is stronger evidence of changed operating practices than of a quantified productivity gain: workflow maturity varies, adoption metrics require careful denominators, and the US partner-bank launch differs from Nu Global's international transfer coverage.

**Why it matters:** Nubank is testing AI across a regulated operating business with existing customers, transaction data and distribution. The useful questions are whether redesigned workflows preserve decision quality, whether agents change customer acquisition, and how much of a domestic data advantage transfers into a new market. Vélez supplies specific practices to track, while also acknowledging that organizational simplification itself creates gains. Headcount ratios, model improvements and customer outcomes should therefore be measured separately.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- The most concrete AI change in this interview is organizational: workflow evaluations, fewer management layers and a dedicated agent-distribution team. These are more informative than an unqualified claim that AI makes banks cheaper.
- A large proprietary transaction history can strengthen credit models, but cross-country transfer still depends on local data and validation; software and methodology transfer should not be confused with universal creditworthiness predictions.
- Agent distribution and human trust can coexist. Nubank's dedicated agent team is evidence of preparation for a new channel, not proof that customers have delegated all financial decisions.
- The CEO's specific model-release recollection conflicts with the official date. His broader account still illustrates why earlier failed trials should not substitute for repeated evaluations on the actual workload.

## Follow-Ups

- Track how many of the 120 redesigned workflows are in production, their evaluation criteria, error rates, human-review needs and measured outcomes; separate process simplification from gains attributable to models.
- Watch for products and APIs released by the dedicated agent-segment team, with attributable customer acquisition, deposit movement or loan conversion rather than team formation alone.
- Compare new-market credit performance after local calibration, including matured repayment outcomes, against appropriate incumbent models; distinguish credit decisions from fraud detection.
- Track updated monthly and daily usage of AI Private Banker capabilities, the rollout of a packaged agent and measurable customer savings or refinancing outcomes.
- Follow US customer acquisition and primary-account adoption separately from Nu Global transfer coverage and the future status of Nu's own bank charter.
