# 🔬 An Oscar, Two Asteroids, and the Algorithm in Your sklearn: John Platt on AI for Science

- Podcast: Latent Space
- Published: 2026-09-22
- Source: https://www.latent.space/p/john-platt
- Relevance: 4/5

John Platt describes Google's Empirical Research Assistance (ERA) as a Gemini-driven search over executable scientific models, with humans defining and revising the objective and checking the science. He reports a concrete win: a simple contrail counterfactual model passed the team's tests after its own approaches had been stuck for two years, though the paper was not yet submitted at recording. ERA uses optimistic tree search, roughly ten parallel candidates and shared experiment history; Platt credits a qualitative capability threshold around Gemini 2.5. The limits are equally consequential: hidden holdouts remain essential, simulation costs can be substantial, budget allocation is unresolved, and physical experiments still supply the ground truth. He cautions that weather-prediction progress does not establish comparable progress on nonstationary climate models.

**Why it matters:** The strongest evidence supports scientific coding and model-search assistance with a measurable evaluation loop. It does not establish autonomous scientific validation. For agent builders, the useful design choices are selective exploration, shared history and bounded parallelism. For research-tool economics, token prices alone miss simulation costs and experimental bottlenecks. The practical opportunity is accelerating well-scoped computational work while preserving independent tests and domain judgment.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- ERA's concrete advantage is systematic computational exploration plus human direction; its benchmark optimization should not be treated as independent validation of a scientific discovery.
- Research-agent economics require accounting for simulation, candidate training and data collection as well as inference tokens. The interview supplies no evidence that the combined workflow is cheap.
- Platt's contrail example strengthens the case for useful research assistance, while its internal-test and unsubmitted-paper status limits the strength of the outcome claim.
