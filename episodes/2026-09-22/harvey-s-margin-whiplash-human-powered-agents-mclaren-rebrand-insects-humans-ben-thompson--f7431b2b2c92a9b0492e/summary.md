# Harvey's Margin Whiplash, Human Powered Agents, McLaren Rebrand, Insects > Humans? | Ben Thompson, Gagan Biyani, Alex Ratner, Peter Kalogiannis, Daniel Petkevich

- Podcast: TBPN
- Published: 2026-09-22
- Source: https://share.transistor.fm/s/937dd166
- Relevance: 4/5

Harvey's reported margin swing illustrates how agentic usage can overwhelm seat pricing, while its cofounder says routing, harness changes and post-training restored positive gross margins. Ben Thompson supplies first-party examples of both useful internal agents and unintended subscription actions, then argues that Amazon's physical fulfillment advantage limits commerce disintermediation. Snorkel's Alex Ratner describes why harder, more valuable training data can sustain human-expertise demand; Swarm Aero's Peter Kalogiannis connects autonomy to manufacturing scale and operator shortages.

**Why it matters:** The strongest evidence concerns the work surrounding models: cost control, reliable state, accurate action semantics, specialized data and physical execution. These examples favor evaluating AI businesses on delivered outcomes and unit economics rather than model access alone. Harvey's recovery remains a company assertion relayed by the hosts, Thompson's commerce conclusion is a thesis, and Swarm Aero's scale and cost figures are targets rather than demonstrated production results.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- The negative-margin Harvey headline is incomplete: the hosts also relay a subsequent return to positive gross margin. That narrows the immediate concern to pricing and cost-control execution without establishing durable profitability.
- Thompson's deployed assistant bot and subscription-support incidents strengthen the case that reliable state and precise action semantics are material product requirements, beyond conversational model quality.
- Ratner offers a mechanism for continued expert-data demand under model improvement: harder remaining errors can command greater economic value, although this remains a supplier's thesis.
