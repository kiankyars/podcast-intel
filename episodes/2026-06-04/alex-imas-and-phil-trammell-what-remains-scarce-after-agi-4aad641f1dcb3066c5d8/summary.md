# Alex Imas and Phil Trammell – What remains scarce after AGI?

- Podcast: Dwarkesh Podcast
- Published: 2026-06-04
- Source: https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/alex-imas-phil-trammell
- Relevance: 4/5

Alex Imas and Phil Trammell use economic models to pressure-test what remains scarce after AGI, focusing on labor share, relational goods, demand elasticity, redistribution, indexing AI returns, and developing-country exposure. Their strongest claims are that current AI labor-displacement evidence is weak, a broad demand-collapse recession requires implausible assumptions, and indexing ownership of the AI economy may matter more than retraining alone.

**Why it matters:** The episode gives a useful framework for AI investment and policy: the distribution of gains depends less on generic automation rhetoric and more on demand elasticity, capital access, private-company concentration, and whether AI becomes electricity-like infrastructure or platform-like rent capture.

## Signals

- **Imas argues there is not yet strong evidence of an economy-wide white-collar AI bloodbath.** [00:30:02] _applications_business_models; observation; medium confidence._ He says recent broad labor-market analysis requires squinting to see AI effects, with only below-trend junior developer hiring and possible stronger demand for senior developers.
- **The guests treat unsaturated compute demand as the central variable behind rising capital share.** [00:00:00] _infrastructure_energy; inference; medium confidence._ They discuss H100 rental prices rising despite better technology, using that as evidence that smarter models can raise the opportunity cost of compute.
- **Trammell says developing countries should prioritize indexing AI returns, not only retraining.** [01:01:28] _policy_geopolitics_security; opinion; medium confidence._ He recommends trying to index the AI economy given possible speed of takeoff, while still preserving retraining value in slower or messy-middle scenarios.
- **Imas and Trammell frame universal basic capital as attractive but hard because the right AI assets may be private or concentrated.** [00:25:57] _companies_capital_allocation; inference; medium confidence._ They contrast negative income tax, UBI, and broad ownership, then note that missing Anthropic, SK Hynix, or similar concentrated winners could leave citizens poorly indexed.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- A near-term AI recession from demand collapse is less plausible if displaced income is reinvested into high-return compute, fabs, and data centers.
- The key distributional question may be whether ordinary citizens and poorer countries can own the right AI-linked assets before returns concentrate.
