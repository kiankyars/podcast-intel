# Adam Brown – A deep but accessible introduction to general relativity

- Podcast: Dwarkesh Podcast
- Published: 2026-07-10
- Source: https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/adam-brown-gr
- Relevance: 3/5

Mostly a general-relativity lecture, but the final section has useful signal on where AI science systems may and may not help: parallel LLMs look more promising when the theory search tree is small, consistency checks are strong, and human interpretation remains possible.

**Why it matters:** The discussion is a concrete scientist's view on AI-assisted theoretical discovery. It argues that parallel model search is not a universal substitute for experiments, but could be powerful in constrained theory spaces and in translating hard proofs into human-comprehensible ideas.

## Signals

- **AI science systems may be most useful when sparse empirical constraints leave only a small theory tree to search.** [01:29:33] _frontier_labs_models; opinion; medium confidence._ Brown says general relativity was unusually discoverable from little empirical input; many LLMs could explore finite branches such as equivalence principle variants, but this works only when consistency and known limits narrow the space.
- **Fields with many internally consistent theories will still need experiments to prune AI-generated candidates.** [01:29:33] _frontier_labs_models; inference; medium confidence._ Brown contrasts string theory's hope that consistency uniquely selects a gravity theory with condensed matter, where experiments are often needed to choose among many possible theories.
- **Brown is more optimistic that LLM proof systems will also become strong explainers, not just opaque proof generators.** [01:29:33] _frontier_labs_models; opinion; medium confidence._ He cites recent AI math examples where the machine-produced idea was human-interpretable and then reused by mathematicians, arguing against the default 'billion-line Lean' pessimism.
- **LLMs' patience may be a genuine research advantage on conjectures humans avoid because they look low-probability.** [01:29:33] _agents_developer_tools; inference; medium confidence._ Brown says models can keep pushing at paths that humans would treat as wasted effort, using the unit distance conjecture as an example where willingness to attack a presumed-true conjecture mattered.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- AI-for-science progress should be segmented by how much the field can be searched with consistency checks versus how much it needs new experimental evidence.
- Opaque formal proof is not the only plausible AI math future; the same systems may also produce compressed, human-usable explanations.
