Walter Goodwin describes how Fractile's $720 million Series C will support production planning and team expansion, and discloses DRAM stacked directly over compute with a projected 20-fold bandwidth advantage over HBM-based accelerators. These extend October's architectural discussion but provide no measured system benchmark. Darren Farber explains Ignium's strategy of supplying components across competing defense platforms, backed by a large existing manufacturing footprint, and his requirement that a dual-use investment have a path to commercial leadership. Dan Goldenberg's proposed AI assistance for employment counselors is not yet evidence of deployed productivity gains.

**Why it matters:** Fractile now provides a more specific memory architecture and capital to pursue manufacturing capacity, making delivery, yield and matched-workload economics the next decisive evidence. Funding and projected bandwidth do not establish a competitive production system. Farber offers a concrete capital-allocation distinction: components shared by many platforms can diversify program risk, while dual-use suppliers still need commercial scale to withstand cheaper competitors and defense-budget cycles.

- The October 3 digest already covered Fractile's shift from SRAM toward DRAM and a second-half-2027 ramp. This interview adds the explicit DRAM-on-logic stack, the $720 million financing and the need to arrange 2027-2028 production capacity now. It does not validate the earlier performance thesis.
