# Grant Sanderson – AI and the future of math

- Podcast: Dwarkesh Podcast
- Published: 2026-06-30
- Source: https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/grant-sanderson-2
- Relevance: 5/5

Grant Sanderson gives a high-density model of why AI math progress is fast but jagged: math is verifiable and grindable, but the hardest future capabilities may be conjecture generation, definition generation, taste, and curation rather than simply proving known problems.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the clearest discussions of where current RL/verifier loops explain AI progress, where benchmark logic breaks down, and what human roles may remain when theorem proving and explanation improve together.

## Signals

- **IMO-level math performance is not a clean AGI threshold because progress inside math is still highly jagged.** [00:00:00] _frontier_labs_models; observation; high confidence._ Sanderson says geometry was effectively cold-solved by 2024 via brute-force methods, while combinatorics remained a harder holdout, so a gold-medal result can still mask narrowness.
- **The next hard AI-math capability may be generating good conjectures and definitions, not just proving stated problems.** [00:00:00] _frontier_labs_models; inference; high confidence._ He frames 'conjecture generator' and 'definition generator' as the premium-tier mathematician work, while noting these are hard to benchmark because usefulness may emerge through subjective community judgment.
- **Current RLVR-style loops struggle with conceptual breakthroughs because verification can take decades.** [00:11:32] _agents_developer_tools; inference; high confidence._ The Galois/group theory discussion is used to show that a valuable mathematical abstraction may fail near-term human review and only prove its importance much later through cryptography, physics, and modern group theory.
- **Coding and math are unusually AI-friendly because their tasks are both verifiable and grindable.** [00:53:48] _agents_developer_tools; observation; high confidence._ Patel argues deterministic repositories and math problems let systems run many parallel attempts and assign credit, while business, trading, and most real-world tasks cannot be replayed in the same way.
- **Lean may matter less as the current training driver than as a future autonomous exploration substrate and correctness certificate.** [00:53:48] _frontier_labs_models; inference; medium confidence._ Sanderson notes recent natural-language math progress without Lean, but says a formal Mathlib-like system could let AI extend math without constant human review and give later readers a green-check proof.
- **LLMs still lag at writing that requires theory of mind, even when they are useful explainers and distillers.** [01:07:07] _agents_developer_tools; observation; high confidence._ The discussion contrasts modular code/math outcomes with writing, where every sentence is the product, and links weak flashcard generation to poor modeling of a learner's future mental state.
- **Human mathematicians' post-AI role may shift toward curation, teaching, and deciding which AI-generated math is worth attention.** [01:16:02] _applications_business_models; forecast; medium confidence._ Sanderson argues mathematicians may become more like museum curators, and that if AI reaches farthest in math, people who can explain and point that work toward useful domains become more valuable.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- AI math progress should be evaluated by task structure: verifiable and grindable tasks move fastest, while taste, problem selection, and concepts with long verification loops remain harder to train directly.
- Formal proof systems may be more important as scalable correctness infrastructure than as the sole source of present-day math progress.

## Follow-Ups

- Watch for AI-math benchmarks that measure conjecture or definition quality rather than only theorem proving.
- Track projects that let agents autonomously extend Mathlib or equivalent formal corpora.
- Look for evidence that AI-generated math becomes useful to human mathematicians beyond proof certificates.
