## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Scientific-agent performance may depend more on a deliberate scientist-coder division of labor than on assigning every task to the largest available model.
- Open-ended scientific RL requires process-level reward design and human-taste calibration, not merely a final verifiable answer.
- Agent infrastructure increases the strategic importance of CPUs, per-core memory bandwidth, and I/O because coordination and tool execution sit outside accelerator inference.
- China's leading-edge compute constraints may redirect startup activity toward compact local inference systems rather than prevent capable agent deployment altogether.
- The viability of photonic compute depends as much on limiting data conversion and reusing mature-node fabs as on optical arithmetic performance.
