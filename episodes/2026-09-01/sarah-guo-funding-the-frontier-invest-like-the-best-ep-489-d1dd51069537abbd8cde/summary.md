# Sarah Guo - Funding the Frontier - [Invest Like the Best, EP.489]

- Podcast: Invest Like the Best
- Published: 2026-09-01
- Source: https://colossus.com/episode/no-priors-just-conviction/
- Relevance: 5/5

Conviction founder Sarah Guo gives a first-party investor view of where AI enthusiasm is colliding with physical and commercial constraints. She reports that a one-to-two-year recursive-self-improvement timeline has become newly common among frontier researchers, while warning that the same researchers increasingly see compute rather than individual effort as decisive. Her more concrete evidence is downstream: an unnamed hyperscaler infrastructure leader sees no supply addition large enough to matter before 2030; Sunday Robotics is targeting an in-home beta by year-end after less than two years of development; and Chai Discovery's work with major pharmaceutical companies, including a contract she describes as worth $10 million, changed her view that AI model companies can capture software-like value in biology. She also argues that open models are already too widespread to contain through US usage restrictions and should instead face rigorous capability and backdoor testing.

**Why it matters:** The episode connects frontier-model expectations to investable bottlenecks and emerging application evidence. Guo's account suggests that power, fuel, construction, supply-chain redundancy, and capital formation may constrain AI progress well beyond the software cycle, while consolidated accelerator demand is making semiconductor and industrial businesses more plausible venture investments. At the application layer, the Sunday Robotics and Chai examples are early but measurable tests of whether AI can escape software into homes and drug research. Her open-source position also frames model policy as an evaluation and resilience problem rather than a distribution-control problem. Each claim carries selection bias because Guo is discussing Conviction's portfolio and investment thesis, and the most ambitious timelines remain unverified.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Frontier researchers' short recursive-self-improvement timelines appear more widespread than a year ago, but the prediction remains socially reported and chronically overconfident rather than measured.
- The limiting AI-infrastructure clock may be 2030-to-2032 physical capacity rather than the next model cycle, with natural gas, construction, regulation, labor, and supply-chain redundancy all binding.
- Household robotics now has a concrete near-term deployment claim from a technically credible team, but a portfolio investor's year-end beta forecast is not shipment evidence.
- Consolidated accelerator demand and compute-independence spending may improve venture economics for semiconductor and industrial companies that were structurally unattractive in the prior software era.
- AI biology may support valuable model and software platforms before an AI-originated drug reaches market, with a reported eight-figure customer contract serving as an early commercial signal.

## Follow-Ups

- Identify the hyperscaler and audit its pre-2030 capacity pipeline, including power, gas, interconnection, permitting, and construction assumptions.
- Track Sunday Robotics' promised 2026 household beta, number of deployed units, task success rates, intervention frequency, hardware cost, and safety performance.
- Verify Chai Discovery's reported $10 million contract, top-ten-pharma customer count, renewal structure, gross margins, and experimentally validated R&D acceleration.
- Compare open-model backdoor and dangerous-capability evaluations across leading Chinese, US, and European releases before accepting either prohibition or safety claims.
- Test whether current semiconductor and infrastructure startups have durable technology margins or are primarily project-finance and execution businesses exposed to concentrated buyers.
