# Ep. 026 - PJM's $12B Modeling Mistake Is Hitting Ratepayers Again (Datacenter, Energy) | Robert Boswell, Jordan Nanos

- Podcast: SemiAnalysis Weekly
- Published: 2026-08-20
- Source: https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--026---PJMs-12B-Modeling-Mistake-Is-Hitting-Ratepayers-Again-Datacenter--Energy--Robert-Boswell--Jordan-Nanos-e3nkns1
- Relevance: 5/5

Robert Boswell argues that PJM's recent capacity-price shock is not simply the physical consequence of AI data-center demand. His model attributes $12 billion of avoidable cost across the 2025-26 and 2026-27 auctions to PJM undervaluing existing winter capacity while procuring against a supply curve made unusually steep by short auction lead times and interconnection delays. The same issue now matters for an emergency procurement: PJM sees a 6.8 GW shortfall for 2028-29, while Boswell estimates seasonal capability and winterization reduce the need to roughly 3 GW. The proposed structure could commit capacity through 2042-43 without a guaranteed data-center counterparty, potentially transferring a liability Boswell sizes at up to $21 billion to existing ratepayers. These are consequential, mechanistically explained estimates from SemiAnalysis, but the episode does not independently validate the model or the auction assumptions.

**Why it matters:** PJM covers 66 million people and the core U.S. data-center corridor, so its auction design affects both household power bills and the pace and location of AI infrastructure deployment. If Boswell's estimates hold, a material portion of the price increase commonly attributed to data-center load instead reflects reliability accounting, procurement timing, interconnection bottlenecks, and liability allocation. That changes the investment and policy question from whether demand must be suppressed to whether PJM can credit existing capacity accurately, enable new supply soon enough, and make the customers creating long-term demand bear the corresponding commitment risk.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- PJM rate increases cited as evidence against data centers may be driven materially by auction design and reliability modeling rather than load growth alone, although the $12 billion attribution remains Boswell's estimate.
- PJM's apparent capacity scarcity is partly institutional: compressed procurement lead times and slow interconnection can prevent supply from responding even when the auction price is high.
- The emergency auction creates an asymmetric financing risk because PJM can commit long-duration capacity before the data centers expected to pay for it have accepted the obligation.
- Seasonal capability and winterization are not minor engineering details in a uniform-price capacity market; they can alter both procurement quantity and every cleared generator's payment.
- Large data-center loads are not necessarily a zero-sum burden on ratepayers; with incremental supply and flexible resources, their demand could spread fixed grid costs and improve utilization.

- Compare PJM's auction lead times and interconnection completion rates with other U.S. grid operators to determine how much of the shortage is specific to its process rather than common equipment or permitting constraints.
- Measure whether winterization mandates have actually reduced forced-outage rates enough to justify Boswell's reliability adjustment.
