# OpenRouter: from Seed to Stripe — with OpenRouter’s Alex Atallah & AMP’s Anjney Midha

- Podcast: Latent Space
- Published: 2026-09-25
- Source: https://www.latent.space/p/openrouter
- Relevance: 4/5

OpenRouter co-founder Alex Atallah and investor Anjney Midha explain the platform's growth, model-fusion experiments and planned combination with Stripe. The strongest operating claims are token volume growing roughly 9% week over week from more than 10 trillion tokens per day, and a tenfold month-over-month increase in the dollar volume of abuse blocked. They describe cheap agent heartbeat routing as a new demand driver and security as a central rationale for partnering with Stripe. The publisher transcript repeatedly swaps the guests' labels; operating claims are therefore attributed by role rather than by its unreliable speaker labels.

**Why it matters:** A model gateway's economics depend on traffic quality, fraud control and how efficiently it routes heterogeneous workloads. Rising token throughput alone does not establish revenue growth, margins or productive agent adoption. The episode identifies concrete pressures on the gateway business and a model-fusion product hypothesis that still needs independent, cost-adjusted evaluation.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- The interview adds fraud handling and workload segmentation to the model-gateway thesis: routing volume can grow quickly while both security exposure and low-value background calls grow with it.
- Model fusion's earlier failure appears sensitive to relative model quality, but the revived product's superiority remains an evaluation question rather than an established consequence of using more models.

## Follow-Ups

- Track paid token volume, active paying customers, revenue/take rate, cache treatment and sustained growth before translating the reported throughput into business economics.
- Request blocked-volume definitions, false positives, realized fraud losses and loss rates; distinguish deliberate abuse from runaway-agent overspend.
- Evaluate fusion against the best single model using held-out tasks, independent grading and equal cost/latency budgets.
