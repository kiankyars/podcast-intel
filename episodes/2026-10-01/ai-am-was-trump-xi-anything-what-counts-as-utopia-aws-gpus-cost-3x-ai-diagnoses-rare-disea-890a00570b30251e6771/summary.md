Silicon Data's Steve Hou explains why hyperscaler GPU rentals retain a large premium and why changes in a token-price index can reflect customers switching models rather than providers changing prices. Gamow Labs founder Daniel McKinnon describes using agents to revisit unresolved genomes, repairing model-specific tool failures, and acquiring laboratory capacity to test variants whose biological effects remain unknown. The useful evidence is about measurement design and operational bottlenecks: neither a normalized GPU-hour nor a variant-prioritization score directly measures the end customer's outcome. The remaining segments cover proposed US–China verification arrangements, OpenAI DevDay reactions and human judgment in AI-assisted writing; their broad policy speculation and repeated product announcements add less incremental signal.

**Why it matters:** Compute economics can be misread if service bundles, usage mix and realized workload performance are collapsed into one price. Gamow offers a concrete account of where vertical agents add value after sequencing: searching beyond analyst filters, coordinating evidence and measuring system failures. Its reported reanalysis throughput and new wet-lab program are useful early operating signals, while prospective diagnostic yield, treatment benefit, fully loaded cost and independently validated assay results remain unestablished in this interview.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- September 27's Ornn discussion and September 29's San Francisco Compute interview emphasized term, scale and physical delivery. Hou adds that service bundles and the exclusion of device-level measured performance remain important limits on treating normalized GPU hours as interchangeable economic output.
- Gamow provides a concrete vertical-agent case in which searching more persistently can recover evidence that analyst triage excluded. Missing biological evidence is a separate constraint: a stronger model cannot by itself establish the effect of every uncertain variant.
- The reported roughly 50% RareBench performance is a variant-prioritization benchmark result. It should not update beliefs about the fraction of real patients diagnosed or treated without prospective clinical evidence.
