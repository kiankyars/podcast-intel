# Ep. 038 - GLM 5.3 on AMD, NVIDIA, and Chinese Chips From Day One (InferenceX) | Kimbo Chen, Wenyao Gao, Jordan Nanos

- Relevance: 4/5

Kimbo Chen, Wenyao Gao and Jordan Nanos examine GLM-5.3 through serving measurements, memory hierarchy and post-training. Their strongest point is that sparse attention reduces the data used by an attention operation without eliminating the need to store the full context; HiSparse moves much of that storage into host memory. AMD's improving serving results demonstrate the importance of engine optimization, but hardware rankings depend on workload, latency targets and cost assumptions. They also discuss long-horizon reinforcement learning and Chinese accelerator support, carefully separating documented adaptation from an unconfirmed hardware co-design theory.

**Why it matters:** Inference economics cannot be inferred from a model architecture or a single tokens-per-second figure. Context retention, host-device transfers, first-token latency, concurrency and software versions jointly determine useful capacity. The episode provides concrete reasons to compare complete serving systems and to test post-training on existing open weights, while leaving actual memory demand, customer economics and cyber-capability rankings unresolved.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Sparse attention does not imply that full-context storage vanishes. System-level offloading can lower GPU residency while preserving substantial host-memory requirements.
- Nanos acknowledges earlier skepticism about AMD software at 11:22–11:55; the team now points to materially improved serving curves. This revises a static software judgment, not every workload's hardware ranking.
- Gao describes GLM-5.3's post-training gains as a wake-up call at 26:26–27:52. The opportunity to improve existing weights is credible, but the episode does not quantify transferable gains, required human data effort or total training cost.

## Follow-Ups

- Reproduce the InferenceX comparison using a fixed run date, model, precision, engine version and AgentX trace; include p90 first-token latency, streaming speed, generated-output-only cost and realistic rental assumptions.
- Measure GPU and host KV residency, transfer traffic, cache misses and tail latency jointly across context length and concurrency. Separate per-request efficiency from forecasts of total HBM or DRAM demand.
- Inspect SAO ablations and slime training traces for critic overhead, policy staleness, rollout throughput and human data effort; do not attribute the entire capability gain to a single algorithm.
- Seek vendor-confirmed hardware selection and measured performance on the seven domestic platforms before treating the Moore Threads roofline match as evidence of a design target.
- Evaluate security workflows under matched authorization, tools and task budgets, recording both refusal rates and successful task completion rather than collapsing access and raw capability into one rank.
