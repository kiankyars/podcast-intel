# Lab-in-the-Loop at Compute Speed — Ian Quigley, Leash Bio

- Podcast: Latent Space
- Published: 2026-10-14
- Source: https://www.latent.space/p/lab-in-the-loop-at-compute-speed
- Relevance: 4/5

Leash Bio co-founder Ian Quigley describes how dense, deliberately varied protein–molecule measurements support both binding models and a fast experimental sandbox for reasoning agents. The most consequential material is his account of hidden chemical-library generalization failures, chemist-authorship confounding in public benchmarks, and physical protein mutations used to challenge memorization. He also reports early agent-search and mouse pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic results, with substantial limits on extrapolation. The publisher says this conversation was recorded roughly two months before its October 14 publication; the later Minos release is separate evidence.

**Why it matters:** A plausible path to scientific agent training is to measure a bounded experimental landscape once and reuse its outcomes for many adaptive search trajectories. Leash's experience also argues that data diversity, meaningful negatives, and evaluation splits can matter more than model complexity or raw measurement counts. Binding rankings and replayed searches remain far upstream of reliable absolute affinity, off-library discoveries, safety, and clinical benefit.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- The agent opportunity is more specific than autonomous drug discovery: a bounded, experimentally measured landscape can supply fast, repeatable search feedback, while off-library and therapeutic validation remain separate bottlenecks.
- Raw molecular-data scale is a weak proxy for useful learning signal. Hidden-library tests, chemist style, and protein-identity shortcuts make split design and measured perturbations central to evaluating claimed generalization.
- Leash's reported commercial optionality comes partly from chemical matter already found during screening; the incremental value of its models must be shown in faster optimization and prospective discoveries beyond that measured corpus.

## Follow-Ups

- Compare the older sandbox account with the October 14 [Minos release](https://leashbio.substack.com/p/making-reality-replayable) and [preprint](https://github.com/Leash-Labs/minos-paper/blob/trunk/Minos_Leash.pdf). Keep release-only numbers separate; check per-target success distributions, query-budget accounting, training versus evaluation separation, and controlled RL gains.
- Audit the [Hermes benchmarks](https://leashbio.substack.com/p/we-now-know-what-it-will-take) by protein family and chemical distance, including author overlap, promiscuous-hit leakage, decoy construction, and comparator training exposure. Require matched-data ablations before attributing the mutant experiment's gains specifically to targeted mutations.
- Track prospective off-DNA validation, measured absolute affinity, and off-library compound selection. At 01:48:06–01:49:03 Quigley identifies massively parallel purification for orthogonal assays as a desired bottleneck removal; reproducible DEL rankings alone do not establish those results.
- Seek disclosed targets, blinded controls, exposure-response data, safety findings, and signed partner terms for the six-month mouse program before revising drug-development or revenue expectations.
