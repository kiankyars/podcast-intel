Maya Barkin, Jeremie Eliahou Ontiveros, Wayne Ma and Jordan Nanos examine the specific approvals that could interrupt the US datacenter pipeline. Barkin puts 2028 US additions at roughly 62 GW in SemiAnalysis's model, with Texas accounting for close to 30%; much of that capacity has not begun construction. The useful update to their earlier 2027 construction argument is that a funded building can still be waiting for its power-generation permit. The conversation was recorded on November 3 before election results, so its polling assumptions and candidate scenarios are historical, not a current post-election assessment. The complete official video is 42:39 versus 42:38 in RSS, with closing captions through 42:32.

**Why it matters:** The next constraint is project-specific: which approval remains outstanding, whether it blocks a building or its power supply, and how much schedule contingency remains. A nominally funded or under-construction campus is not necessarily deliverable capacity. Separate delays from higher construction or financing costs, and distinguish the analysts' gross political exposure scenarios from megawatts actually delayed. Their 2028 forecast is a model estimate, not installed capacity.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Relative to the October 22 digest's construction-led confidence in 2027 delivery, the new distinction is between a permitted shell and a permitted power plant. Existing construction evidence should be paired with the remaining power-phase approvals.
- Bring-your-own-power is not an unconditional escape from grid-related delays: generating equipment may depend on environmental permits covered by a broader state hold.
- Political exposure is not a canceled-capacity estimate. Campaign positions, actual agency action, project-specific outstanding permits and schedule contingency must be checked separately.
