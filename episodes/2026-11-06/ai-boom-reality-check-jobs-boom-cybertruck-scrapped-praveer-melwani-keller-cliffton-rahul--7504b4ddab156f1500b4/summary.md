Figma CFO Praveer Melwani supplies operating evidence for agents strengthening an incumbent software platform: MCP usage, paid-seat conversion and credit consumption are growing, while model and workflow changes reduce execution costs. Zipline CEO Keller Cliffton describes factory expansion, battery insourcing and a funded national rollout whose announced scale remains largely ahead of deployment. Julius founder Rahul Sonwalkar explains why financial diligence requires reconciliation and judgment beyond document retrieval, and how DeepFinance evaluates that gap. The full publisher transcript was reviewed; market commentary, sponsor reads, fashion biography and broad medical claims were excluded.

**Why it matters:** These interviews connect AI adoption to specific economic and operational constraints: owning useful context and billable workflows at Figma, manufacturing complete delivery systems at Zipline, and auditing assumptions in financial agents. Figma adds measured adoption to the product architecture covered on October 7, but credit consumption is not revenue and management reports do not establish causal retention lift. Zipline separates an operating network from contracted expansion and manufacturing targets. DeepFinance is evidence about evaluated model-and-harness configurations, not autonomous dealmaking or investment returns. Figma is also a listed sponsor of the show; its interview claims remain attributed and are checked against primary releases.

- **Julius’s DeepFinance targets a diligence failure Sonwalkar sees in capable coding models: retrieving a revenue number without reconciling conflicting records or checking the assumptions behind it.** [01:12:08; 01:13:27; 01:13:59; 01:14:17; 01:14:25; 01:14:37; 01:15:19; 01:15:34; 01:15:45; 01:16:03; 01:16:21] _agents_developer_tools; observation; high confidence._ Sonwalkar describes messy data rooms, inconsistent management and audit figures, and assignments requiring financial models plus investment-committee recommendations. Separate methodology context: Julius’s [November 5 publication, checked November 8](https://www.juliusresearch.ai/blog/deep-finance), describes 21 anonymized-data tasks averaging 27.1 expert hours and 84 rubric criteria. Scores are the share of criteria satisfied, not the share of complete deals successfully executed. Errored attempts are rerun to obtain ten successful attempts per task, so scores do not measure raw operational reliability. Provider-specific harnesses mean model-and-tool-stack comparisons, not a controlled base-model ranking. The actionable lesson is to evaluate source reconciliation and downstream assumption consistency alongside calculation accuracy.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Compared with October 7 coverage of Figma’s native-agent architecture, this interview adds usage and monetization mechanisms: external agents can initiate Figma workflows and support paid seats. Retention causality and incremental AI revenue remain unquantified.
- Figma’s usage acceleration needs a pricing and instrumentation adjustment: more add-on credits at the same price and newly credit-consuming features can raise credit volume without proportional revenue growth.
- Zipline’s next scaling hurdle is tangible manufacturing and deployment capacity. A funded multi-year location agreement is stronger evidence than a delivery-market extrapolation, but it is not installed capacity or demonstrated site profitability.
- DeepFinance shifts financial-agent evaluation toward auditing sources and assumptions across a workflow; a code-search habit that finds the right-looking number can still produce an internally consistent but wrong recommendation.

## Follow-Ups

- For Figma, track AI add-on revenue, inference cost per completed workflow and retention/seat expansion for comparable MCP and non-MCP cohorts; keep pricing changes separate from task-volume growth.
- For Zipline, track commissioned Walmart sites and aircraft output against factory targets, plus site utilization, delivery contribution margin, uptime and the planned early-2027 Los Angeles launch.
- For DeepFinance, examine task-level traces, grader agreement and unretried failure rates before using leaderboard scores for a deployment decision; the live page has changed model configurations, so do not backdate its current ranking to the November 6 interview.
- Sonwalkar calls Inkling 2 Small open source, but Thinking Machines’ [November 5 launch](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-small/) says Tinker access initially had closed weights, with Apache 2.0 weights intended the following week after safety testing. Verify actual weight availability and the precise comparison set before adopting his best-American/second-overall open-model characterization; same-day benchmark/model launches do not prove absence of contamination.
