# Controlling Biology - Fabian Theis, Helmholtz Munich

- Relevance: 4/5

Fabian Theis describes progress from mapping cell states to predicting responses and choosing subsequent experiments. His strongest concrete report is a LabCompass collaboration that completed three or four experimental/model cycles; he explicitly places it closer to active learning than autonomous reinforcement learning. He also separates representation learning from intervention prediction, explains when simple baselines remain competitive, and describes pharma participation in a consortium generating organoid data. These are research and commercialization observations, not demonstrated clinical effectiveness or a general-purpose virtual cell. The publisher transcript identifies Theis and both hosts and runs through their farewell at 50:36, against a 50:50 RSS duration.

**Why it matters:** Compared with earlier virtual-cell coverage focused on data generation and future feedback loops, this interview supplies a bounded example of repeated experimental feedback and a more specific division of labor between models. It also points to two practical filters for AI-biology businesses: improvement on the intended biological task, and access to useful proprietary or early-access experimental data. Model size and a compelling platform narrative do not establish either.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Adds a specific report of repeated model/experiment cycles to earlier Biohub and DeepMind discussions of future feedback infrastructure; the claimed progress remains limited to the described research system.
- Makes the separation between broad observational pretraining and task-specific intervention learning explicit. More cells in a corpus do not automatically mean better predictions of what an intervention will do.
- Provides a reported commercial mechanism for data generation: pharma participation in exchange for early access, rather than a generic claim that virtual-cell platforms will monetize.
