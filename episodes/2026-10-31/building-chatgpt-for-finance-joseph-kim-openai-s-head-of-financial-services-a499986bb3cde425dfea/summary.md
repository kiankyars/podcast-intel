# ⚡ Building ChatGPT for Finance — Joseph Kim, OpenAI's Head of Financial Services

- Podcast: Latent Space
- Published: 2026-10-31
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v-d1WTqIULs
- Relevance: 4/5

Joseph Kim explains how OpenAI built ChatGPT for Financial Services around reliable data access, shared model training and domain-specific product work. The most concrete disclosures are high error rates in some tested financial-data connectors, human preference training for presentation quality, and flaws Kim says his team found in financial evaluations. This is an October 31 interview about an existing product: OpenAI's [announcement is dated September 10](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-financial-services/), and its current availability statement is for eligible financial institutions through sales. Kim's discussion of Pro access and more senior financial tasks in 2027 describes future ambitions. The complete official-channel captions cover the introduction and farewell of the 27:34 edition.

**Why it matters:** The interview locates useful differentiation in data reliability and domain-specific workflows even when the underlying intelligence is shared with developers. It also separates numerical correctness from professional judgment: human preference labels and valid, task-specific evaluations remain bottlenecks. Kim describes the current product as human-reviewed knowledge-work assistance; neither the interview nor the announcement establishes autonomous investment performance or measured reductions in consequential financial errors.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- A financial AI product can differentiate through reliable licensed-data delivery while its model improvements remain available to other developers. Shared model access does not eliminate application-level engineering or data economics.
- Checkable outputs and professional preferences need distinct evaluation signals. Fixing answer keys and measuring human-reviewed work quality should precede claims that benchmark saturation implies job automation.

## Follow-Ups
