The useful additions are deployment-level evaluations of human agency, a still-unpriced catalog of 106 security capabilities, and human fallback capacity that makes shutting down AI practically possible. ## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Retained human control should be evaluated across a sequence of delegations and the surrounding institution, not inferred from the presence of a final approval button.
- AI shutdown readiness has an operational dependency requirement: recovery procedures, retained human skills and tested fallbacks affect whether an organization can actually use its technical controls.
- Preserving strategic choice requires construction before certainty. However, RAND's catalog and proposed cost sprint are preparation work, not a funded implementation plan or proof that the required capabilities can arrive in time.

## Follow-Ups

- Read RAND's planned security-catalog follow-up for capability-specific lead times, cost ranges, owners and implementation milestones; do not use the interview's illustrative 1.5-times comparison as a budget estimate.
- Look for the forthcoming agency-evaluation work and test whether it measures agenda control, persuasion, permissions and changes in human authority over time in real deployments.
- For agent deployments, inspect whether operators retain independent access to credentials, revocation and recovery controls, and whether essential workflows have a tested fallback when agents are unavailable.
- Seek empirical multi-agent evidence that separates emergent capability or coordination failures from the much stronger claim of aggregate superintelligence.
