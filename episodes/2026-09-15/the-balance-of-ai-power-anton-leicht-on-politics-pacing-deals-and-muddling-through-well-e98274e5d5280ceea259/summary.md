# The Balance of AI Power: Anton Leicht on Politics, Pacing Deals, and Muddling Through Well

- Podcast: The Cognitive Revolution
- Published: 2026-09-15
- Source: https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/the-balance-of-ai-power-anton-leicht-on-politics-pacing-deals-and-muddling-through-well/
- Relevance: 5/5

Anton Leicht separates the technical value of a frontier pause from its geopolitical and market consequences. He argues that AI valuations depend on premium research automation, that proprietary post-training could break the familiar closed-to-open capability catch-up pattern, and that middle powers should exchange secure compute capacity for durable frontier access. His proposals center on preserving bargaining power and effective oversight, rather than assuming economic gains guarantee political autonomy.

**Why it matters:** The interview connects frontier-model economics to semiconductor supply, access agreements, and government incentives. It supplies concrete reasons why a pause, an open-model release, or a foreign data-center investment may have different consequences from their headline interpretation.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Leicht says his technical case for a pause strengthened because recent misalignment now resembles the consequential problems he expects future systems to pose.
- He challenges the assumption that open models and personal hardware must always inherit frontier capabilities on a fixed delay.
- He distinguishes broad economic improvement from retained political agency: countries may become richer while losing leverage over essential AI suppliers.

## Follow-Ups

- Track premium research-automation contract values and renewals separately from broad office-productivity adoption when assessing the interview's valuation thesis.
- Compare newly announced evaluator access with actual rights to inspect infrastructure, publish findings, and escalate safety concerns.
- Watch for binding model-access guarantees in European compute deals, including cutoff remedies and security requirements.
- Treat orbital compute as a conditional challenge to host-country leverage; check launch economics and useful deployed capacity before assuming terrestrial demand disappears.
