# What will computer chips look like in 2045?

- Podcast: Dwarkesh Podcast
- Published: 2026-10-22
- Source: https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/intel-tour
- Relevance: 5/5

Dwarkesh's Intel fab tour connects AI supply constraints to concrete manufacturing decisions. CTO Pushkar Ranade estimates that the existing global tool stock could support roughly doubling Intel's leading-edge wafer output, not a tenfold expansion; that is a conditional estimate, not a ceiling on future compute growth. Engineers explain why backside power changes High-NA EUV economics, why customer-ready design kits and restrained process innovation matter, and how hybrid bonding, ruthenium wiring and stacked transistors could extend scaling. The strongest evidence concerns present integration bottlenecks. The 2045 architectures are research possibilities, not a committed product roadmap.

**Why it matters:** The episode shifts attention from fab construction budgets to qualified equipment, customer design infrastructure and integration yield. Those determine how quickly capital becomes usable AI silicon. It also explains why greater bandwidth and lower communication energy can matter as much as more transistors. Intel's competitive claims remain management claims: the interview supplies neither comparable foundry yields nor customer economics proving parity with TSMC. Ranade refers to mid-2026 during the interview, so later supplier disclosures are necessary context for an October publication.

## Signals

- **Ranade estimates that the existing global equipment stock could support about 2x Intel's leading-edge wafer production, not 10x; scaling further requires the upstream tool supply chain to expand.** [00:48:00; 00:53:14] _semiconductors_compute; forecast; medium confidence._ At 48:00 he says qualified process flows lock Intel to specific tools even when alternative vendors exist, and estimates about 80% tool reuse from 18A to 14A. At 53:14 he describes an illustrative 18-month equipment lead time before new-fab ramping. This is not a measured global capacity ceiling or a FLOPs forecast. Later context: [ASML's October 14 remarks](https://investor.asml.com/static-files/40c56a6b-1460-4f69-9340-6a869560d24b) target 30% more low-NA EUV capacity in 2027 and explore more than 30% additional growth in 2028, plus throughput upgrades. Supplier output is constrained but changing.
- **Chris Auth says High-NA EUV is a customer-specific option in Intel's 14A design kit, with economics tied to backside power and the number of patterning passes it eliminates.** [00:13:13] _semiconductors_compute; observation; high confidence._ Auth explains that moving power wiring off the front side relaxes signal-wire pitch enough for selected layers to use a single High-NA exposure. He explicitly describes adoption as an option discussed with each customer, not a universal requirement. His TSMC comparison should be limited to the flows under discussion: [TSMC describes backside power for A16](https://www.tsmc.com/english/dedicatedFoundry/technology/logic/l_2nm) and [announced planned High-NA volume manufacturing from 2030](https://pr.tsmc.com/english/news/3338). This supports evaluating cost per completed layer and product, rather than scanner price or adoption date alone.
- **Auth describes Intel's foundry transition as a change in development discipline: customer-ready design kits and maturity gates are now central to delivering a process on schedule.** [00:03:22; 00:13:13; 00:53:14] _companies_capital_allocation; observation; high confidence._ He says 18A began without the formal external-customer PDK process Intel subsequently had to build, while 14A incorporates that process from inception. Separately, lessons from 10nm led to explicit yield, defect and performance gates: an immature innovation gets deferred to a later node rather than delaying the entire process. Ranade prioritizes predictable delivery and rapid, high-yield ramps over small headline differences in density. These are first-party accounts of operating changes, not independent proof that customer qualification or manufacturing execution is solved.
- **Tyler Osborn says chip-to-wafer hybrid bonding gives logic packaging an economic advantage by selecting tested good dies, while finer connections can reduce communication energy as well as increase bandwidth.** [00:43:53] _semiconductors_compute; observation; high confidence._ Whole-wafer bonding requires matching die layouts and can waste good dies when defect locations differ. Chip-to-wafer assembly instead places known-good chips on known-good base positions; Osborn still expects both methods to serve different architectures. He distinguishes 25-micrometer Foveros production pitch from sub-10-micrometer work in the lab. [Imec and EVG's May 2026 result](https://www.imec-int.com/en/press/imec-and-ev-group-demonstrate-wafer-wafer-hybrid-bonding-200nm-interconnect-pitch-and-record) demonstrates 200-nanometer wafer-to-wafer pitch on a test vehicle, corroborating research headroom without establishing production readiness. The opportunity is better system communication, not automatically better total manufacturing yield.
- **Jack Kavalieros explains why a functioning stacked-transistor prototype does not imply a production-ready CFET: vertical contacts, material compatibility and manufacturing integration must outperform the existing architecture together.** [00:32:41; 00:19:20] _semiconductors_compute; observation; high confidence._ Intel uses simplified short-loop experiments, including one ribbon above another, before combining components into fuller devices. Taller structures make deep etching, void-free filling and subsequent processing harder; a lab device may still offer no advantage over side-by-side nanoribbons. [Intel's IEDM 2023 disclosure](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/news/research-advancements-extend-moore-law.html) establishes CFET research demonstrations, not commercial availability. Auth separately identifies heat removal as a limit for deeper logic stacks and describes exotic 2045 structures as possibilities beyond his roughly four-year visibility. No firm CFET shipment date is established here.
- **Kavalieros places ruthenium's potential advantage over copper near 20-nanometer wiring pitch, because avoiding copper's liner can outweigh ruthenium's worse bulk resistivity at very small dimensions.** [00:29:30] _semiconductors_compute; observation; medium confidence._ He says directly etching ruthenium avoids the liner used in copper integration, recovering conductive cross-section as lines shrink. The crossover is an approximate, process-dependent research assessment, not a claim that all copper wiring will be replaced. [Intel's IEDM 2024 work](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/news/intel-foundry-unveils-technology-advancements-iedm-2024.html) demonstrated ruthenium plus airgaps in R&D vehicles; [imec's 2025 results](https://www.imec-int.com/en/press/imec-demonstrates-16nm-pitch-ru-lines-record-low-resistance-obtained-using-semi-damascene) target the same sub-20-nanometer resistance problem. Integration improvements can also restore copper's competitiveness, so material selection must be judged at the process level.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Stronger case that equipment qualification and supplier lead times constrain near-term wafer expansion even when funding and fab space exist; the conditional 2x estimate should not become a fixed global compute ceiling.
- High-NA adoption is better evaluated as a process-architecture and customer-economics decision than as a simple measure of foundry leadership.
- Hybrid bonding and local interconnect changes offer concrete scaling paths, while stacked-logic research still faces integration and thermal obstacles that a working prototype does not settle.

## Follow-Ups

- Track actual ASML tool shipments and installed-base throughput against its October expansion plans, keeping tool counts, wafer output, accelerator performance and delivered AI compute separate.
- Look for external 14A customer commitments, PDK qualification, production yields and customer-specific High-NA choices before treating Intel's technical confidence as evidence of commercial foundry parity.
- Require product-scale yield, reliability, thermal and cost data for hybrid bonding, ruthenium and CFET; record laboratory pitch demonstrations separately from high-volume production milestones.
- Watch whether Ranade's closing expectation of increased investment becomes an announced capital plan or binding equipment commitments; the interview states intent without a budget.
