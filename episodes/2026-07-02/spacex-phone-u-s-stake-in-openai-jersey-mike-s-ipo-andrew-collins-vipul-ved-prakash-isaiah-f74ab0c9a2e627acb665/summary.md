# SpaceX Phone, U.S. Stake in OpenAI, Jersey Mike's IPO | Andrew Collins, Vipul Ved Prakash, Isaiah Taylor, Dean Ball, Rob Toews, Tuhin Srivastava

- Podcast: TBPN
- Published: 2026-07-02
- Source: https://share.transistor.fm/s/eebe04f7
- Relevance: 5/5

TBPN assembled unusually dense first-party updates: Together AI reported 12x bookings growth and an $800 million round; Valar Atomics reported first electricity from its Ward 250 reactor; and Baseten reported a $1.5 billion round plus a 20-cloud inference footprint. The episode also exposed a material tension: open-model infrastructure executives see rapid enterprise substitution, while Dean Ball expects frontier-capable open weights to face de facto licensing and fall further behind closed models.

**Why it matters:** The claims strengthen the case that independent inference and open-weight infrastructure are becoming major businesses, while Valar's milestone gives AI-focused nuclear deployment a concrete technical proof point. At the same time, model-access regulation is becoming a central dependency, and Ball's analysis suggests a federal equity stake in AI labs could create competition and constitutional-governance risks rather than merely distribute economic upside.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Independent inference and open-weight infrastructure may be scaling into a major standalone market faster than public model-share narratives imply.
- Small nuclear for AI has advanced from planned prototypes to reported electricity generation outside a national lab, although commercial NRC licensing remains ahead.
- Enterprise open-model adoption and frontier-model access should be modeled separately: substitution can accelerate even while regulation widens the capability gap.
- A government AI-lab equity stake carries competition and constitutional-governance implications, not just distributional effects.
