# The AI Slowdown Debate | Nico Wittenborn, Scott Keogh, Mitchell Green, David Rosenthal & Ben Gilbert, Faraj Aalaei

- Podcast: TBPN
- Published: 2026-09-14
- Source: https://share.transistor.fm/s/e172867c
- Relevance: 4/5

Cognichip CEO Faraj Aalaei describes a chip-specific model and workflow strategy, with shorter design cycles intended to reduce both engineering effort and the speculative features added to hedge against future workloads. A same-day company release supplies an early front-end design benchmark, not proof of compressed full-silicon delivery. Adjacent's Nico Wittenborn says AI-driven app proliferation has made simple consumer software less attractive for his venture strategy. Scout Motors CEO Scott Keogh explains how in-house prototype production is preparing a new factory workforce for a planned 2028 launch.

**Why it matters:** The episode connects AI productivity to three concrete constraints: semiconductor data and verification workflows, competition when software becomes cheaper to create, and the learning required to turn a new factory into production capacity. Cognichip's opportunity depends on independently measurable design outcomes, while Scout's workforce plans remain a ramp target rather than delivered manufacturing output.

He argues that useful open semiconductor data are scarce and that a model must operate within precise design workflows. Separate from the interview, the company's [September 14 launch release](https://www.cognichip.ai/news/cognichip-launches-aci-enterprise-full-stack-intelligence-infrastructure-for-chip-design) reports over 40 engagements, including Renesas and SiTime, and an unnamed customer's front-end ASIC exercise completed by one engineer in days versus a claimed 4–5 months for a team. That is a company-reported benchmark, not independently reproduced evidence of fabrication, silicon bring-up, or end-to-end schedule savings.
- **Aalaei argues that faster chip design could reduce architecture risk and unnecessary power and cost, not merely engineer-hours.** [02:38:24; 02:39:16] _semiconductors_compute; inference; medium confidence._ He describes a recurring design response to uncertain future demand: teams add capabilities that may not be needed as insurance against the market changing before production. He says this increases complexity, power consumption and cost, and expects shorter development cycles to let designers target a better-known workload. This is a causal thesis from his semiconductor experience; the interview does not demonstrate that Cognichip has eliminated those features or improved production-chip power, performance or cost.
- **Wittenborn says cheaper AI-assisted development has pushed him away from simple consumer-app bets because increased supply makes venture-scale outcomes harder to obtain.** [00:47:43; 00:48:05; 00:48:20; 00:50:11] _applications_business_models; observation; medium confidence._ The Adjacent founder and RevenueCat investor reports a sharp increase in app launches and says he has moved somewhat away from his earlier consumer-software focus. His distinction is between more niches being served and enough value accruing to an individual company for a venture return. He favors deeper differentiation, citing a telecom business building its own core infrastructure, and says personal agents face uncertain overlap with frontier labs and Meta. This is his investment selection response, not measured proof that all consumer AI businesses have worse returns.
- **Scout is using in-house prototype builds to train its new factory workforce before a planned 2028 production ramp.** [01:08:57; 01:09:09; 01:09:35; 01:10:05] _companies_capital_allocation; observation; medium confidence._ Keogh says more than half of Scout's roughly 1,600 employees are already at the factory, where early prototypes are being built internally instead of outsourced. He presents this as a way to give workers who have not made cars before practical experience ahead of ramp-up. First customer deliveries remain planned for 2028, with a target of roughly 3,000 factory employees and 3,800–4,000 across the company. Current prototype work is therefore evidence of preparation, not mass-production capacity or achieved launch volumes.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Chip-design automation should be evaluated on architectural choices and verified downstream outcomes as well as task speed. A front-end benchmark cannot establish a shorter fabrication-to-production cycle.
- Lower consumer-app development costs can expand the number of useful products while reducing the chance that any one new entrant captures a venture-scale business. Distribution and differentiation become more important selection criteria.

## Follow-Ups

- Seek the Cognichip benchmark's design size, baseline team and effort, verification coverage, physical-design completion, PPA measurements and customer confirmation; distinguish engineering acceleration from tape-out and silicon results.
- Track RevenueCat cohorts for retention, acquisition costs and revenue concentration as app launches increase, rather than using launch counts alone as evidence of a stronger consumer investment opportunity.
- For Scout's 2028 ramp, distinguish prototype completion, validated production processes, supplier readiness and customer deliveries from workforce targets.
