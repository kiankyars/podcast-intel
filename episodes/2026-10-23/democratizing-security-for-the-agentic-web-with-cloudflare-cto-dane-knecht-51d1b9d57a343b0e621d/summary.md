# Democratizing Security for the Agentic Web with Cloudflare CTO Dane Knecht

- Podcast: No Priors
- Published: 2026-10-23
- Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6VprwfSc4Xk
- Relevance: 4/5

Cloudflare CTO Dane Knecht supplies concrete examples of changing how a company uses AI: a customer-email workflow that previously crossed three teams now runs through one tool with manager approval; daily model-spend alerts escalate at roughly $300, $500 and $1,000; and interns help colleagues turn tasks into agent skills. He says Cloudflare OS is used more by go-to-market and administrative teams than engineering. On security, he anticipates a temporary surge in remediation work and expansion from one red team to four or five, while continuing feature development. These are operating anecdotes and management expectations, not measured company-wide productivity or margin gains.

**Why it matters:** The useful evidence is the organizational mechanism behind enterprise AI adoption: shared context, governed data access, redesigned handoffs, human approval and spending feedback. Cloudflare is turning its internal approach into a developer-platform reference architecture. Its security forecast also suggests a near-term claim on engineering capacity and inference budgets. Neither the interview nor the discovery pilot establishes that more AI usage automatically produces economic value.

## Signals

- **Knecht says Cloudflare replaced a customer-email workflow spanning three teams with a single product-manager tool that retains manager approval.** [24:18; 24:48; 25:29; 25:46] _applications_business_models; observation; high confidence._ Previously a product manager used BI to select a customer cohort, marketing to draft the message and marketing operations to send it. In his account, the new tool selects a described cohort, rewrites the email in Cloudflare's voice and sends it after the manager approves. He ties this redesign to the May restructuring. The concrete change is fewer handoffs, rather than merely faster work inside each team; he gives no before-and-after staffing, error-rate or cost measurements.
- **Cloudflare uses spending notifications rather than a token-use leaderboard: Knecht describes daily alerts around $300 to the user, $500 to the manager and $1,000 to a VP.** [27:36; 27:54; 28:16; 28:32; 28:59; 29:12] _companies_capital_allocation; observation; high confidence._ He says employees are evaluated on what they ship, with broad freedom to choose tools; high-spend alerts are uncommon and often reveal unnoticed usage. These are notification thresholds, not stated hard caps or an average budget per employee. His frontier-model planning, open-model execution and frontier-model review example remains fairly manual. Automatic routing is his preferred future, not an established company-wide system.
- **Knecht expects a temporary security-work surge over roughly 18 months and says Cloudflare will probably expand from one red team to four or five.** [02:12; 02:49; 07:39; 07:58; 35:42; 36:21; 37:25] _policy_geopolitics_security; forecast; medium confidence._ He reports severe vulnerability-response events becoming weekly rather than annual, without a published dataset, and anticipates security absorbing over half of engineering time during a catch-up period. The red-team expansion is prospective; his spoken end-of-year/next-year deadline is ambiguous. He wants more adversarial discovery to force patching automation, while preserving feature delivery even at the expense of cost optimization. The equal security-versus-engineering token-spend prediction is Sarah Guo recounting Greg Brockman's view, not a measured Cloudflare budget split.
- **Knecht describes interns as an AI-adoption channel: Cloudflare expanded a roughly 200-intern program toward about 1,000 over a year and used interns to turn colleagues' requests into skill files.** [29:44; 30:17; 30:24; 30:32; 32:42; 33:08] _agents_developer_tools; observation; high confidence._ He says managers working with interns adopted AI faster. An agents email alias invited employees to submit tasks; replies initially came from interns writing skills, even when users assumed the service was automated. This is a concrete way to discover useful workflows without first cataloguing every job. The interview's rounded hiring number is not a simultaneous headcount: Cloudflare's earlier [internal rollout account](https://blog.cloudflare.com/how-we-use-ai-with-cloudflare-os/) described a goal of 1,111 interns across the year. Neither source supplies a controlled adoption comparison or a completed-hiring reconciliation.
- **Knecht says Cloudflare OS is used more by go-to-market and administrative teams than engineering, with company context and governed data access reducing the blank-chatbox adoption problem.** [20:17; 20:55; 22:04; 22:48; 23:45] _agents_developer_tools; observation; high confidence._ He describes an internal workspace that packages company practices, data connections and security, then exposes that design as a forkable reference architecture on Cloudflare's developer platform. He says the conference marketing team built hundreds of internal tools that previously required agency work. No usage denominator or measured agency-spend reduction is supplied. The [official repository](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-os) confirms the open-source workspace; availability of its code does not establish general availability of a managed service.
- **Knecht describes Cloudflare's Signals work with OpenAI as a way to discover new or changed content without repeatedly crawling unchanged pages; publisher payments remain a separate commercial proposition.** [15:11; 17:37; 18:07; 19:33] _applications_business_models; observation; high confidence._ At 18:07 he explains the discovery mechanism and intended reduction in needless crawling. The [October 15 joint pilot report](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-openai-discovery-agentic-web/) adds that a cohort covering about 1% of Cloudflare's network helped index hundreds of millions of pages. It also says discovery can initially increase crawling, access controls still apply and business outcomes remain to be measured. Knecht's separate micropayment and content-creator income discussion supplies no transaction-volume or publisher-revenue results.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Cloudflare's clearest AI-adoption evidence concerns removing handoffs and giving non-engineers governed access to company systems, not maximizing token consumption.
- The security opportunity has an explicit internal resource cost: more adversarial teams and potentially less cost-optimization work. The 18-month catch-up horizon is a management scenario.
- Content discovery and publisher monetization should be evaluated separately: finding and indexing additional pages is measurable, but does not by itself demonstrate publisher income.

## Follow-Ups

- Measure the customer-email workflow's cycle time, approval rejection rate, delivery errors and staffing before and after automation.
- Track model-spend distributions and outcomes around the $300/$500/$1,000 notifications; determine whether model routing becomes automatic and whether quality survives cheaper execution.
- Verify actual red-team expansion and its timing, remediation throughput and critical-vulnerability backlog rather than extrapolating the 18-month forecast.
- Reconcile annual intern hiring with completed starts and retained employees; compare adoption and useful-output changes in teams receiving intern support.
- For Cloudflare OS, distinguish open-source deployments from paid managed availability and request active-user, retained-workflow and operating-cost measurements.
- For Signals and publisher payments, track incremental citations, referral conversions, crawl load and net publisher receipts independently.
