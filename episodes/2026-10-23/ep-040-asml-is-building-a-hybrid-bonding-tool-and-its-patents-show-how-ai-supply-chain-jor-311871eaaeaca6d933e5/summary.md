- Relevance: 5/5

SemiAnalysis's Jordan Nanos, Howie and Sherry Zhang examine an ASML patent for a track-based hybrid-bonding system, a possible extension of its metrology and wafer-stage capabilities. The useful distinction is between a disclosed technical concept and a commercially qualified tool. They identify integration, cleanliness and yield as gates, forecast HBM volume adoption in 2029 or later, and explain the larger-mask transition for High-NA. Besi's October 22 management statement disputes both the competitive interpretation and the HBM timing. Their China discussion supplies a mechanism for performance gains through three-dimensional partitioning, but does not establish equivalent process technology or superior production economics.

**Why it matters:** Hybrid bonding creates opportunities across alignment, inspection, cleaning and die handling, while its value depends on application-specific economics. Logic stacking, HBM and larger High-NA masks have different adoption schedules. Treating them as one imminent equipment cycle would obscure the important risks: customer qualification, usable throughput, bonding yield, mask infrastructure and whether existing packaging methods remain sufficient.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- ASML's patent supports a technical design direction, while Besi publicly disputes the competition thesis. Customer qualification and product scope will distinguish these accounts.
- Logic and HBM need separate adoption assumptions. SemiAnalysis's 2029-plus HBM forecast is later than Besi's expectation, making qualification scope and production volume critical to resolving the disagreement.
- The larger-mask program is a 2030s ecosystem project layered onto earlier High-NA adoption, rather than an immediate fix available on every installed scanner.
- China's stacking strategy can change system performance without reproducing a leading transistor process; that does not remove thermal, yield, design-tool or manufacturing constraints.

## Follow-Ups

- Track an ASML product announcement, customer qualification, scope of collaboration with existing bonding suppliers, and first delivery commitments before assigning commercial market share to the patent.
- Request measured overlay, contamination, uptime, throughput and cost per good bonded die across comparable chip sizes and pitches.
- Compare SemiAnalysis's 2029-plus HBM scenario with memory-vendor qualification and volume schedules, distinguishing test tools, base-die bonding and complete DRAM-stack adoption.
- Track the 2031 mask pilot and 2033 system-readiness milestones, supplier commitments, actual mask dimensions, pellicle/inspection readiness and whether scanner changes require new tools.
- Validate LogicFolding through reproducible die analysis and performance, thermal and yield measurements; compare equivalent multi-tier designs and EDA capabilities rather than nominal process-node labels.
