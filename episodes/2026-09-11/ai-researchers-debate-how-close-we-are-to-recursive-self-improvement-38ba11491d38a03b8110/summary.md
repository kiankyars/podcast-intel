# AI researchers debate how close we are to recursive self-improvement

- Podcast: Dwarkesh Podcast
- Published: 2026-09-11
- Source: https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/john-beren-charlie
- Relevance: 5/5

John Schulman, Beren Millidge and Charlie O’Neill separate strong coding and benchmark progress from the harder requirements for autonomous research: choosing useful objectives, transferring into realistic work and learning repeatedly without forgetting. O’Neill describes failures in small, iterative model updates; Schulman explains why realistic prompts matter for distillation; and Patel reports a small-scale experiment in which improved datasets contributed more compute efficiency than training-recipe changes. Their explanations of RL progress emphasize strong starting models, broader task coverage and longer task horizons, not a demonstrated self-improving research loop.

**Why it matters:** The discussion identifies concrete tests for both frontier-model differentiation and application-specific learning: realistic multi-turn performance, retention after repeated updates and independent selection of useful experiments. It also cautions against extrapolating small-model efficiency measurements or longer coding horizons into general research automation. The transcript supplies chapter-level timestamps rather than timestamps for individual remarks.

He says RL is better at adding capabilities than explicit company-specific knowledge. Millidge argues that training a fresh model with the accumulated data can work better, suggesting a plasticity and distribution-shift problem rather than simply insufficient capacity. No model sizes, retention curves or controlled comparisons are provided.
- **Schulman argues that access to a strong teacher is insufficient for useful distillation unless the prompt distribution exercises realistic, multi-turn work.** [00:18:39] _frontier_labs_models; inference; medium confidence._ He distinguishes environment difficulty from realism: hard, verifiable puzzles can improve benchmarks without reproducing coding sessions involving changing human requests and multiple objectives. His hypothesis is that larger models transfer better from narrow tasks, while a student trained on similarly narrow prompts can match benchmark scores but miss the teacher’s practical behavior. He explicitly does not know competitors’ post-training details. The implication is that realistic interaction data, not just teacher access or more difficult tasks, can differentiate distilled models.

Patel stresses the small scale. Millidge cautions that architecture can unlock capabilities such as long context that a fixed short-context comparison misses. The authors also flag evaluation noise and limited hyperparameter tuning. The study has not been independently reproduced here, and the ratio is not established at frontier scale.
- **Millidge and O’Neill explain recent RL gains as a combination of strong mid-training, wider task coverage and longer-horizon persistence, rather than uniform reasoning transfer across domains.** [01:18:03] _frontier_labs_models; inference; medium confidence._ Millidge says synthetic reasoning mid-training supplies much of the starting behavior, leaving RL to adjust a policy with a concentrated success signal instead of matching every token of another model’s reasoning. O’Neill distinguishes learning to keep working productively for longer from acquiring all the reasoning skills of a new domain; math training does not remove the need for coding environments. Millidge allows some cross-domain transfer and emphasizes the much broader set of tasks labs now train on. - **The researchers identify choosing and revising research objectives as a distinct bottleneck that faster experiment execution does not automatically solve.** [00:00:00; 00:28:06] _agents_developer_tools; opinion; high confidence._ O’Neill separates optimizing an already specified objective from discovering the next useful objective or paradigm. Millidge says current models propose many small, miscellaneous experiments and that a self-contained loop must repeatedly select objectives without drifting off course. Schulman describes research as turning an intuition into a simplified test, then restoring realism, and expects researcher feedback plus practice environments to train successive model generations. Millidge counters that verifiable environments can exceed human performance, but the group does not establish that this transfers to autonomous, open-ended research.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Benchmark parity after distillation need not imply parity on realistic multi-turn work; prompt coverage and feedback realism deserve separate evaluation.
- Continual learning should be assessed over repeated updates with retention checks, not inferred from a successful one-off fine-tune or a faster model-release cadence.
- Longer task horizons and broad RL coverage can explain useful agent progress without establishing that the model can choose the right research objectives autonomously.

## Follow-Ups

- Reproduce the Patel–Han experiment with broader evaluations and hyperparameter sweeps; test whether its data-versus-recipe comparison survives larger models and architectural regime changes.
- Look for O’Neill’s continual-learning results with model sizes, update counts, task-specific learning and general-capability retention measured together.
- Evaluate students distilled from the same teacher on realistic changing-user-intent tasks as well as verifiable benchmarks, varying the prompt distribution.
- Track research agents on independent objective selection, experiment interpretation and subsequent experiment choice, separating these from coding throughput.
