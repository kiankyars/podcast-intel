- Relevance: 5/5

Max Kan and Andrew Megalaa explain SemiAnalysis' measurements of AI subscription limits by model and token type. Their $200-to-$12,000 Opus comparison assumes a particular agentic workload, and their subscription-margin estimates depend on utilization they cannot observe directly. The more durable findings concern cache economics, the disadvantage of reselling someone else's model, and the gap between token allowances and completed useful work. Jordan Nanos also describes how access restrictions led him to adopt an open model for adjacent tasks.

**Why it matters:** Subscription spending, API-equivalent consumption, inference cost and useful output are different measures. This episode supplies a concrete way to separate them, while exposing the assumptions behind confident-looking plan comparisons and frontier-lab margin claims. Procurement and financial models need workload mix, cache performance, utilization and outcome quality alongside headline prices.

[Anthropic's documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching) confirms prefix caching and 5-minute or paid 1-hour lifetimes; [OpenRouter documents](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/best-practices/prompt-caching) routing subsequent requests to the same provider to improve cache hits. Those sources support the mechanism, not Kan's speculative historical 99% cache-read margin. A workload's fraction of cache-read tokens also does not establish a provider-wide hit rate.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- A large API-equivalent allowance is a conditional price comparison, not evidence of equally large customer savings, productivity or provider losses.
- Token limits are measurable from usage meters; average subscription utilization and provider margins require separate evidence.
- Cache reuse links systems engineering directly to agent economics, but the episode's 96% cache-read workload share should not become a universal serving statistic.
- Open-model adoption may start with availability for one task and persist through shared context; router token share alone cannot establish a market-wide revenue shift.

## Follow-Ups

- Repeat matched plan/model measurements with the reader's own token mix and completed-task quality; log five-hour and weekly constraints, account cohort and measurement date.
- Independently verify the October 29 transition for older OpenAI $200 subscriptions described in the companion article before treating its lower limits as universal today; the episode's comparisons are explicitly a snapshot.
- Seek first-party utilization distributions and a definition of inference gross margin before incorporating the estimated 10% utilization or 50–60% subscription margins into a financial model.
- Compare providers using cache hits, misses, TTL expiry, latency and total successful-task cost under identical agent traces.
- Test whether reseller workflow or routing gains offset smaller model allowances; do not substitute unverified API discounts for actual contracts.
