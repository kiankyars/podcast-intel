# Gavin Baker on Anthropic's S-1, GPU Prices, and SpaceX

- Podcast: TBPN
- Published: 2026-10-27
- Source: https://share.transistor.fm/s/9c66fec3
- Relevance: 4/5

Gavin Baker adds useful investor-side evidence about compute procurement and financing: portfolio companies are struggling to secure large GPU clusters for late 2027, faster installation could reduce capital tied up before revenue, and contract repricing could finance more of the next buildout internally. He also reports broader open-weight inference demand beyond Cursor and proposes an IPO-driven release of mutual funds’ private-investment capacity. These are consequential leads from an investor with disclosed interests, not independently measured sector-wide growth or guaranteed returns. The strongest new material is outside the Anthropic S-1 figures already covered on October 27.

**Why it matters:** The key distinction is announced GPUs versus usable, contracted capacity. Baker’s procurement anecdotes support investigating continued scarcity, while his financing thesis depends on actual contract renewals, utilization and cash conversion. Primary checks materially narrow the story: NVIDIA’s faster DSX deployment requires prepared sites, and Silicon Data’s public spot-price sample does not support treating all rental prices as rising every day.

## Signals

- **Baker reports that portfolio companies seeking another 4,000-Blackwell cluster for September 2027 face sharply higher quotes and difficulty finding capacity, despite announced supply additions.** [00:13:51] _semiconductors_compute; observation; medium confidence._ At 13:51–14:37 he compares clusters secured in May/June 2026 with follow-on procurement for late 2027; no prices, providers or like-for-like terms are disclosed. His contango claim comes from Silicon Data, in which he says Atreides invested. Separately, [Silicon Data’s October 21 study](https://www.silicondata.com/blog/gpu-spot-prices-dynamic-pricing-price-discovery) found more than half of tracked spot series unchanged during October 8–16. That different market slice does not disprove the cluster anecdotes, but prevents promoting his broader daily-price assertion into verified market fact.
- **Baker argues that reducing the delay between GPU delivery and productive operation could release substantial financing capacity; DSX POD targets part of that delay.** [00:18:37] _infrastructure_energy; inference; medium confidence._ He describes three-to-six-month energization delays and idle equipment tying up capital. [NVIDIA’s October 21 announcement](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Introduces-DSX-POD-AI-Factories-to-Accelerate-Europes-Infrastructure-Deployment/default.aspx) confirms factory-integrated Vera Rubin containers and a target reduction from six months to as little as 14 days at sites with power, cooling and networking already prepared; partner delivery is expected in early 2027. This is a vendor deployment target, not demonstrated fleet-wide performance or a solution to utility delays. Baker’s aggregate savings are hypothetical.
- **Baker forecasts that repricing older compute contracts and selling more capacity at current prices could let cloud operators fund a larger share of the 2027 buildout from operating cash flow.** [00:37:06] _companies_capital_allocation; forecast; medium confidence._ At 15:26–16:32 he describes CoreWeave’s older contracts as below market and favors more spot exposure plus customer prepayments. At 37:06–38:21 he connects repricing and additional capacity to accelerating cloud revenue and a possible approaching peak in external financing. Contract terms, repricing dates and cash-flow projections are not supplied. His sub-one-year payback assertion is excluded as an unverified return estimate; spot exposure also removes revenue certainty. This is Baker’s financing scenario, not established company guidance.
- **Baker says open-weight inference demand has broadened beyond Cursor, with growth at suppliers including Fireworks remaining strong even as Cursor compute is reportedly brought in-house.** [00:07:38] _applications_business_models; observation; medium confidence._ At 5:28 and 7:12–7:59 he says the ecosystem is outgrowing frontier labs and describes Fireworks revenue changing materially during financing negotiations. [Fireworks’ July announcement](https://fireworks.ai/news/fireworks-raises-1-5-billion-series-d) confirms Atreides co-led its round, establishing both proximity and financial interest. His October growth comparisons and ex-Cursor demand are not backed by matched periods, denominators or customer-level figures, and the insourcing claim is attributed to him. The useful lead is demand diversification, not a verified market-share ranking.
- **Baker expects eventual public listings and lockup expiry for SpaceX and Anthropic to free mutual funds’ capacity for additional private investments.** [00:35:54] _companies_capital_allocation; forecast; medium confidence._ At 35:29–36:10 he explicitly requires holdings to be public and unlocked. The [SEC’s liquidity-rule guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/investment-company-liquidity-risk-management-program-rules) supports the mechanism for covered open-end funds: illiquid holdings face a 15% limit, with liquidity assessed using saleability and market depth. Listing alone does not ensure reclassification, and released allocation capacity is neither new cash nor a committed investment. He gives no affected-fund holdings, dollar estimate or listing timetable.
- **Baker acknowledges a downside to the compute-scarcity thesis: if frontier labs reduce training demand, existing capacity could be redirected toward inference without waiting for new data centers.** [00:28:20] _frontier_labs_models; inference; medium confidence._ At 27:57–28:43 he credits a bearish argument from another commentator and treats reallocation as conditional. The quoted 74% training share was not verified in the available Anthropic filing text and is not retained as a fact. No lab announces such a reduction here, and the interview does not establish that all training hardware, networking or contracts can switch economically to inference. The signal is a supply sensitivity worth monitoring, not an observed supply release.

## Changed Views Or Tensions

- Raises the priority of checking delivered cluster availability into late 2027 rather than inferring abundance from announced capacity; does not establish a universal daily increase in GPU rental prices.
- Adds two financing mechanisms to monitor: shorter installation delays and contract repricing. Neither demonstrates that external funding needs have peaked.
- Makes training-to-inference reallocation an explicit counter-scenario to persistent scarcity, with the allocation share and practical fungibility still unresolved.

## Follow-Ups

- Obtain comparable late-2027 cluster quotes with GPU type, networking, region, term, service level and prepayment terms; separate reserved-cluster pricing from preemptible spot indices.
- Track DSX POD partner deliveries and measured time from prepared-site arrival to production, alongside site-readiness delays and capital tied up before revenue.
- Check cloud operators’ disclosures for contract renewal schedules, spot mix, prepayments and operating cash flow after equipment commitments; do not treat unverified resale premiums as realizable proceeds.
- Seek matched-period inference-provider revenue and customer concentration excluding Cursor; retain Baker’s disclosed investment interests when evaluating comparisons.
- Quantify relevant mutual funds’ private holdings, liquidity classifications and lockups before estimating released allocation capacity; verify training allocation from filing graphics or company disclosures before using a percentage.
