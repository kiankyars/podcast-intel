## Episodes

### [Nathan Goes to China #3: US-China Relations, the Art of the AI Deal & the Road to Pax Robotica](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/nathan-goes-to-china-3-us-china-relations-the-art-of-the-ai-deal-the-road-to-pax-robotica/)

The Cognitive Revolution · Relevance 4/5

In this solo commentary following a two-week China visit, Nathan Labenz argues for a cooperative US-China AI framework rather than a race for decisive superiority. His most concrete contributions are a report that export-control uncertainty is deterring safety collaborations, a warning that divergent hardware and model architectures could make each side's capabilities harder to assess, and proposals for shared research compute, confidential audits, and reciprocal technology exchange. These are his observations and policy recommendations, not announced government agreements.

**Why it matters:** The episode identifies costs of technological separation beyond chip availability: interrupted safety research, loss of shared evaluation methods, and weaker confidence about the other side's capabilities. Its proposed alternatives connect semiconductor access, manufacturing know-how, deployment liability, and research transparency. The strongest material is the specific mechanism behind each recommendation; his informal conversations do not establish representative Chinese opinion, and the feasibility of the proposed safeguards remains unproven.

### [YC Demo Day, Spoon = Bent (Again), iPhone Duo Reactions | Garry Tan, Om Buddhdev, Karen Serfaty, Antonio Li, Edward Ge, Alfredo Gonzalez](https://share.transistor.fm/s/35e8db3b)

TBPN · Relevance 4/5

YC's demo-day interviews expose practical bottlenecks beyond model capability: proving training-data value through post-training experiments, completing agentic purchases through existing checkout defenses, abstracting robot control for LLMs, and handling genomic datasets in legacy healthcare systems. Garry Tan also reports a larger hard-tech presence and substantial token credits for YC companies.

**Why it matters:** The concrete opportunities are in integration and measurement, not merely better prompts. These founders describe how they sell model improvements, payments execution, physical control, and data infrastructure. Early revenue and production figures are company self-reports, not proof of durable economics, broad autonomy, or clinical effectiveness.
