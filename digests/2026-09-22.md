## TL;DR

- **Almeida describes Jev as TypeSafe's first System 1 model: a machine-native, programmable model whose consumer is code and whose design target is intelligence per dollar.** [00:03:21; 00:03:52; 00:05:03] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/jev)). He contrasts pretrained language models, RLHF chat models, and RLVR models with a class designed to return results directly consumed by code. He says TypeSafe is optimizing the model's internals for software and will use Jev for models on the frontier of intelligence per dollar. This is the founder's product and model-design description, not an independently measured frontier claim.
- **Almeida argues that RLHF mode dropping makes string-based models conservative and miscalibrated for programmatic decisions, motivating RLCD's calibrated-decision objective.** [00:06:03; 00:07:31; 00:08:23; 00:24:59; 00:27:18] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/jev)). He says preference optimization drops minority modes, makes errors in long strings especially costly to the learned probability distribution, and leaves strings poorly suited to decision-making. He later defines RLCD as a new North Star for programmatic use rather than a claim about a particular published algorithm. TypeSafe had not published the method at the time of the interview, so this remains an informed but unvalidated company thesis.
- **Almeida presents TypeSafe as a data lab whose advantage comes from task-shaped synthetic data and curation, and says he would not spend $1 billion on pretraining.** [00:20:43; 00:21:37; 00:22:31; 00:23:04; 01:49:33; 01:49:57] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/jev)). He says the right task and North Star matter more than compute, calls model capability work a data problem, and says each training objective needs its own data. He says TypeSafe avoids user data because repeated requests would overfit the present, wants curated teams to repair model jaggedness for future use cases, and would use model composition and other training work instead of pretraining if given a billion dollars. These are strategic claims from the CEO, not disclosed budget or capability results.

## More findings

- **TypeSafe's API is designed to turn model judgments into composable program primitives, with structured state and many small questions replacing a giant prompt.** [00:57:57; 00:58:16; 00:59:47; 01:01:22; 01:02:10; 01:02:39] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/jev)). Almeida maps Choice to an enum or switch, Noul to an if statement, and Score to sorting or thresholding. He says state, instructions, and criteria can remain structured JSON, and recommends decomposing a workflow into small semantic decisions whose behavior can be evaluated and fixed in code. He argues that this makes policies and thresholds inspectable rather than dependent on a model remembering a large system message.
- **Almeida treats robustness as more important than exact determinism and says TypeSafe will trade deterministic sampling and long-term model stability against intelligence per dollar.** [00:41:37; 00:42:19; 00:42:31; 00:48:11; 00:49:35] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/jev)). He defines determinism as identical input producing identical output but prefers similar outputs for semantically equivalent inputs, tested with UUID or nonce perturbations. He says deterministic serving is possible but consumes more GPUs for the same intelligence, while TypeSafe plans rapid model iteration and does not promise long-term support for every version even though it says deployed model versions will not be changed in place. The operational policy is stated by the founder and is not a contractual guarantee here.
- **Almeida reports that Jev passed one trillion tokens per day with calls continuing overnight, which he interprets as machine workloads rather than launch experimentation.** [00:35:23; 00:35:32; 00:36:23] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/jev)). He distinguishes signups and waitlist traffic from software usage, says the platform was continuously training at night because machines were calling it, and gives more than one trillion tokens per day as the milestone. He supplies no request counts, customer mix, token accounting, revenue, or independent telemetry, so this is an early vendor-reported demand signal.
- **Almeida sees a major coding-agent redesign opportunity outside the single-model KV-cache loop, using explicit task state, hierarchical context lookup, and shared memory for multi-agent coordination.** [01:38:56; 02:09:25; 02:10:13; 02:10:48; 02:11:50; 02:13:21] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/jev)). He says current Claude Code and Codex are built around a one-model world, while appended KV-cache state makes routing, compaction, sub-agents, and software-style decomposition difficult. His proposed direction is to label state and sub-tasks, search the task tree for relevant context, and let parallel agents read and write shared state with intelligent coordination. He presents this as an open research direction with no guarantee that it will work.
- **The hosts' hands-on tests led them to say Seedance 2.5 has moved AI video from face swapping to high-fidelity whole-person and object replacement, including coherent replacements across scale, occlusion, and aggressive motion; they still expect near-term production use to be hybrid and targeted.** [00:05:31] ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/81a5a42e)). ## Episodes

### [Jev: System One models for Prod, not God — with Diogo Almeida, CEO, TypeSafe AI](https://www.latent.space/p/jev)

Latent Space · Relevance 5/5

TypeSafe CEO Diogo Almeida frames Jev as a new System 1 model class for typed, programmable decisions rather than chat or code completion. He argues that RLHF mode dropping and human-preference optimization leave text models poorly calibrated for unattended software, while RLCD targets programmatic use; the method is unpublished and the performance claims are not independently established here. The interview also gives a concrete data-first training and capital-allocation thesis, a structured API and decomposition philosophy, a robustness-over-determinism tradeoff, a self-reported greater-than-one-trillion-token-per-day usage milestone, and a proposal to redesign coding agents around explicit state beyond the KV cache.

**Why it matters:** If the TypeSafe thesis holds, AI integration could shift from one large conversational call toward many typed, testable decisions embedded in ordinary software, changing evaluation, inference economics, and coding-agent architecture. The episode provides useful first-party mechanisms and product commitments, but most claims about calibration, intelligence per dollar, reliability, demand, and future agent coordination remain company assertions or forecasts; independent workflow-matched measurements are still needed.

### [Amazon Ban Hammer, Seedance Strategy, Amazon Blocks Muse | Vinod Khosla, Augustus Doricko, Ben Hylak, Aaron Levie, Lucas Shaw](https://share.transistor.fm/s/81a5a42e)

TBPN · Relevance 5/5

The episode connects two adjacent frontiers: AI video becoming a usable, agent-orchestrated production tool, and personal agents colliding with commerce platforms and enterprise systems of record. The guests also provide first-party startup claims about autonomous cloud seeding and agent reliability.

**Why it matters:** The strategic thread is that capability alone may not determine value capture. The hosts and guests point to distribution, advertising control, trusted task completion, authoritative data, and customer-specific alignment as the bottlenecks. Several quantitative claims are speaker-reported and should be independently checked before treating them as market facts.
