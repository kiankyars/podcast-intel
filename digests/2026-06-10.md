## Episodes

### [Biohub: The Future of Biology is Open-Source with Co-Founders Mark Zuckerberg, Priscilla Chan, and Head of Science Alex Rives]()

**No Priors | relevance 5/5**

Biohub's leaders describe a $500 million virtual biology effort that couples frontier AI with new wet-lab measurement systems, then releases the resulting tools as open science. The most concrete update is ESMFold2: Rives says the model folded 1.1 billion proteins, hit state-of-the-art structure benchmarks, and generated nanomolar binders from a small lab validation cycle.

**Why it matters:** The episode frames biological AI as a data-generation and institutional-design problem, not just a model-scaling problem. If Biohub's open-source protein and cell world models work, they could become shared infrastructure for drug discovery and synthetic biology rather than proprietary biotech point products.

### [Why Video Agent models are next — Ethan He, xAI Grok Imagine](https://www.latent.space/p/video-agents)

**Latent Space | relevance 5/5**

Ethan He gives a technical account of frontier video generation from NVIDIA Cosmos to xAI Grok Imagine, with unusually concrete details on synthetic captions, VAEs, temporal compression, iteration speed, and why image models bootstrap video models. His main thesis is that video generation is becoming a video-agent problem: LLMs plan, rewrite prompts, call diffusion and editing tools, and iterate toward production-quality media.

**Why it matters:** This changes the competitive lens for generative media. The winner may not simply be the lab with the best standalone video diffusion model, but the lab or product that combines strong language reasoning, cheap iteration, media tools, and workflow integration into an agentic creative system.

### [The Age of Async Agents — Cognition's Walden Yan & OpenInspect's Cole Murray](https://www.latent.space/p/cognition)

**Latent Space | relevance 5/5**

Cognition's Walden Yan and OpenInspect's Cole Murray describe the move from local coding assistants to background cloud agents that take specs, run in managed environments, test work, and return PRs. The strongest signals are Devin's reported internal PR growth, the security architecture of separating brain from machine, the hidden VM/filesystem work needed for usable agents, and SRE/support/security use cases beyond ordinary coding.

**Why it matters:** This is a first-hand look at what production agent infrastructure actually requires. The market is not just paying for model calls; it is paying for secure execution environments, repo setup, snapshotting, testing loops, integrations, onboarding, and operational trust.

### [State of Enterprise AI 2026: Aaron Levie on Tokenmaxxing, Rise of Headless, and AI-Proofing Your Job](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/State-of-Enterprise-AI-2026-Aaron-Levie-on-Tokenmaxxing--Rise-of-Headless--and-AI-Proofing-Your-Job-e3k0gsp)

**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck | relevance 5/5**

Aaron Levie gives a dense enterprise-AI readout: token spend has become a top CIO issue, single-task agent runs can reach roughly $1,000, AI spend is moving from IT to business-unit budgets, and enterprises will use multiple models rather than one frontier default. He also argues headless agent access will force SaaS vendors toward dual seat-plus-consumption pricing.

**Why it matters:** This is a concrete operating model for enterprise AI adoption. The bottlenecks are no longer generic enthusiasm; they are budget ownership, model routing, access control, FinOps for tokens, SaaS pricing, and the implementation layer between frontier labs and real workflows.

### [Babysitting the Machine: Glean's Rebecca Hinds on the Hidden Human Labor of AI at Work](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/babysitting-the-machine-glean-s-rebecca-hinds-on-the-hidden-human-labor-of-ai-at-work/)

**The Cognitive Revolution | relevance 5/5**

Rebecca Hinds summarizes Glean's Work AI Index 2026, built from a 6,000-worker survey plus Glean telemetry, and argues that enterprise AI adoption has a productivity paradox: high individual usage and reported time savings, but weak organization-level performance gains. The mechanisms are botsitting, failed AI sessions, botshitting, missing context, and incentives that reward individual output rather than collective value.

**Why it matters:** This is one of the more quantitative enterprise AI adoption readouts. It supports the thesis that context layers, measurement, incentives, and org design may matter as much as model capability for turning AI tools into business performance.

### [Building an AI Guardian for Enterprise with Onyx Security CEO Maxim Bar Kogan]()

**No Priors | relevance 4/5**

Onyx CEO Maxim Bar Kogan describes agent security as a new control-plane problem: enterprises want agents to inherit flexible user permissions, but existing identity and API tools cannot infer the agent's intent. Onyx's answer is a hierarchy of small specialized models that cheaply route suspicious actions to more capable oversight agents.

**Why it matters:** As coding and workflow agents gain permission to touch real systems, the security surface shifts from static access control to contextual intent verification. That creates a new vendor category between model labs, enterprise security teams, and the applications agents act on.

### [Palantir CEO Alex Karp on Tokenmaxxing & Taste](https://share.transistor.fm/s/14a1df49)

**TBPN | relevance 4/5**

Alex Karp gives a characteristically blunt Palantir thesis: enterprises are tired of tokenmaxxing, model intelligence is becoming more commoditized, and the scarce layer is taste plus ontology embedded in real workflows. The transcript is messy and lacks usable timestamps, but the core first-party signal is clear: Palantir wants to be judged by deployed enterprise outcomes rather than raw AI consumption.

**Why it matters:** The episode captures how Palantir is positioning itself against both frontier labs and generic enterprise AI vendors. Karp's framing is that durable value comes from problem selection, workflow ontology, deployment credibility, and political navigation, not just better LLMs.
