## Episodes

### [Ben Thompson on Big Tech, China, and the AI Boom Running Out of Money - [Invest Like the Best, EP.487]](https://colossus.com/episode/winners-losers-ai-era/)

**Invest Like the Best | relevance 5/5**

Ben Thompson presents a coherent investment framework for the AI buildout: the near-term failure mode is a financing gap rather than disbelief in the technology, because capital is being committed years before advanced-node capacity and revenue arrive. He connects that mismatch to TSMC's conservative capacity policy, hidden economic discounts in Nvidia's customer financing, variable inference costs that strain Microsoft's per-seat bundle, and an IBM-like middleware strategy that could preserve Microsoft while agents attack its legacy interfaces. His geopolitical argument is equally contrarian: decisive U.S. AI superiority could increase Taiwan risk rather than remove it.

**Why it matters:** The episode separates durable AI demand from the solvency and timing of the companies financing it. That distinction changes how to read hyperscaler equity issuance, Nvidia's reported margins, foundry diversification, and enterprise AI pricing: a painful bust can coexist with continued technical progress, and apparent scarcity profits may be compensation for risk that has merely moved elsewhere. Thompson also supplies concrete mechanisms for evaluating platform winners, including whether AI costs can remain bundled, whether TSMC scarcity makes Intel or Samsung qualification rational, and whether ad marketplaces provide a better model-improvement and monetization loop than standalone subscriptions.

### [Bending Spoons Buys Airtable, Snap Rips, Ads in BMW | Grace Li, Samir Kaji, John Quinn, Nikhil Reddy, Art Levy & Russell Kaplan, Brendan Carr](https://share.transistor.fm/s/b35b3f03)

**TBPN | relevance 5/5**

An unusually dense episode combines Quinn's first-party account of a reported Perplexity appellate win, Cognition's model-routing and enterprise-deployment lessons, a nine-week Pentagon counter-drone marketplace, expanding FCC hardware-security controls, and Intelligence's 5.6-million-person preference-data flywheel. John Quinn also flags cross-default and labor risk beneath the AI data-center buildout.

**Why it matters:** The common thread is that AI advantage is moving beyond raw model quality into deployment structure: choosing models by metric, reorganizing work around agents, capturing preference data through useful products, and managing legal, procurement, infrastructure, and supply-chain constraints. The reported court ruling and FCC developments could directly shape agent access rights and the U.S. hardware market, while Cognition's examples show organizational design becoming a binding adoption bottleneck.
