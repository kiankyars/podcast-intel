## TL;DR

- **Goodwin says Fractile moved away from its initial SRAM-based approach toward much higher-bandwidth access to DRAM, with long-context capacity a central reason for the change.** [10:47; 11:25; 11:44; 12:04; 12:18; 12:34] ([No Priors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OpeCP4wCxkA)). At 10:47–12:29 he describes the first roughly two years of SRAM work and concerns arising in late 2023–2024 about model and context growth. He says memory-vendor and foundry collaboration produced a different platform, targeting a second-half-2027 ramp at 12:34–12:39. The [company's DRAM engineering role](https://job-boards.eu.greenhouse.io/fractile/jobs/4895770101) corroborates work on DRAM devices, interfaces and reliability. It does not establish the undisclosed memory architecture, measured bandwidth, production readiness or achieved cost savings; the ramp date remains a forecast.
- **The InferenceX team describes Engram as learned token-pattern lookup tables whose addresses are known early enough to prefetch from DRAM or SSD; this parameter offload is distinct from KV-cache offload.** [01:54; 02:19; 04:28; 04:46; 05:18; 06:23; 06:36] ([SemiAnalysis Weekly](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--034---Engrams-How-DeepSeek-Offloads-KV-Cache-to-DRAM-and-SSD-Core-Research--Jordan-Nanos--Cam-Quilici--Alec-Ibarra--Bryan-Shan-e3pp7av)). At 01:54–02:25 they describe learned two- and three-token embeddings, and at 04:28–06:39 explain the tradeoff between earlier model access and time available to overlap retrieval with computation. Their [September 18 research](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/blog/engrams-embedding-entendre-codesign) reports roughly 189 GiB of Engram memory in its DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash configuration and explains that moving those parameters out of HBM frees room for weights and KV cache. Engram is not a detachable dictionary: ablations change downstream features and expert routing. Neither a lookup nor offload is free, and this does not establish a universal reduction in total memory demand.
- **Ho's practical monitoring design uses cheap activation probes to screen activity and escalate suspicious cases to an LLM; the two detectors remain complementary.** [50:21] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/Why-AI-Agents-Cheat--Eric-Ho-Goodfire-e3pm35t)). At 50:21–53:11 he proposes cascades and explains that probes reuse activations already computed during inference. The interviewer introduces the 90% number; Goodfire's report specifically claims 90% lower LLM-monitoring cost on Kimi K3 with about a 1% precision drop. This is not an end-to-end serving-cost reduction. The paper reports DeepSWE recall of 0.41 versus 0.44 for LLM monitor versus probe on Kimi, but 0.41 versus 0.33 on GLM at matched false-positive rates. [Cost claim](https://www.goodfire.com/research/reward-hacking-activation-monitors); [comparative results](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19101v1).

### [Academia is for Ambition — Alex Zhang, MIT](https://www.latent.space/p/rlm)

Latent Space · Relevance 4/5

MIT researcher Alex Zhang argues that agent harnesses can improve what a model learns, not just how it uses tools. He explains how recursive language models offload context and delegate domain-specific work so a reusable decomposition strategy transfers across task lengths and domains. His published experiments support that claim on bounded benchmarks, with higher training runtime. He also reports a gap between AI kernel leaderboard results and stability in complete systems, describes Prime Agent's persistent programmatic subagents, and outlines speculative tool execution. Broad claims about frontier-lab swarm training, research breakthroughs and capability overhang remain interpretation or hypothesis rather than new first-party evidence.

**Why it matters:** The consequential research direction is to evaluate the model and its execution environment together: a harness can change generalization, coordination and latency without changing the base architecture. The practical tradeoffs are measurable—extra training runtime, production verification, durable context management and speculative calls that may consume unused compute. This adds mechanisms and bounded results beyond a generic argument for better agent orchestration.

Less adaptation data could lower integration costs, but it does not remove integration or supervision. Long sequences require reliable progress detection and recovery as well as motor skills. The data discussion also identifies why inexpensive human video may complement rather than replace precise action-labelled data.
