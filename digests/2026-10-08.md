# 2026-10-08

## TL;DR

- **Martin says OutSystems makes coding agents edit an application model before deterministic code generation, allowing existing controls and components to be reused; this does not establish that applications cannot break.** [08:13; 31:32; 39:25] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/software-that-never-breaks-outsystems-ceo-woodson-martin-on-building-enterprise-grade-apps-at-ai-speed/)). At 08:13, Martin describes agents changing an abstract model rather than raw code, followed by platform code generation and reuse of existing permissions and enterprise assets. At 31:32, he describes Mentor services exposed through MCP with tuned models underneath. Official [ODC architecture documentation](https://github.com/OutSystems/docs-odc/blob/main/src/eap/agentic-development/architecture.md) confirms that AI and manual developers change the same application model and the compiler enforces the roles that model defines. Its [guarantees document](https://github.com/OutSystems/docs-odc/blob/main/src/eap/agentic-development/odc-ai-and-platform.md) explicitly separates deterministic compilation from probabilistic interpretation: humans must validate requirements, business logic and authorization boundaries. The [September 25 announcement](https://www.outsystems.com/blog/posts/agentic-guardrails-and-context) says Agent Experience and Enterprise Context Graph are reaching general availability. The defensible advantage is constrained generation and reuse, not a demonstrated guarantee of correctness, compliance, or zero vulnerabilities.
- **Martin reports 4, 19 and 26 major-feature releases across successive quarters, then lower token spend after a June-July peak as OutSystems improved its harness and routed work to cheaper models.** [27:32; 31:32] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/software-that-never-breaks-outsystems-ceo-woodson-martin-on-building-enterprise-grade-apps-at-ai-speed/)). At 27:32, he gives counts for Q4 of the prior year and Q1/Q2 of the current year, attributing faster delivery to the company's AI push. He then says a context-rich internal harness and an LLM gateway reduced cost for many tasks, with current spend below the Q3 forecast. He distinguishes software creation from routine business operations, where older models or deterministic code can suffice. This is useful evidence of cost optimization following adoption, but he supplies no spend amount, savings percentage, token/payroll ratio, task-volume denominator, staffing baseline, or comparable quality metric. Feature counts do not establish a 6.5-fold productivity gain, and his view that most enterprise workloads do not need frontier models is broader than the measured evidence presented.
- **Martin says tested customer agent systems can remain blocked on model approval, making compliance review a separate bottleneck from development; he has not seen agent-insurance uptake in his customers' platform workloads.** [13:06; 17:07; 19:05] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/software-that-never-breaks-outsystems-ceo-woodson-martin-on-building-enterprise-grade-apps-at-ai-speed/)). At 13:06, he describes systems awaiting approval of the underlying model, including questions about training-data provenance, even for document-to-structured-data workflows. These are reported customer requirements, not a statement that every jurisdiction mandates such proof. At 17:07, he distinguishes lower-risk internal IT workflows from lending decisions that customers need to audit and defend, while acknowledging organizational inertia. At 19:05, he says he has not observed adoption of agent insurance for these customers and workloads, although he considers it potentially helpful. This limits the inference that more capable models or available insurance alone will unlock deployment; the interview gives neither a blocked-project count nor review-cycle statistics.

## More findings

- **Martin says OutSystems has put a telemetry-driven agent into its customer-success teams to recommend what customers should automate and forecast time savings, then generate an initial implementation.** [51:34; 1:03:47] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/software-that-never-breaks-outsystems-ceo-woodson-martin-on-building-enterprise-grade-apps-at-ai-speed/)). At 51:34, he describes using the structure, telemetry and data flows of existing applications to identify manual work worth automating, estimate returns, and produce a first version for development, testing and validation. At 1:03:47, he calls this an agent foundry that inspects architecture for opportunities. He locates the new capability in the hands of customer-success teams, rather than establishing a generally available self-service product. The strategic inference is that visibility into an installed application portfolio could help a platform discover demand and sell additional automation. No recommendation-acceptance rate, forecast accuracy, customer revenue contribution or realized savings are given.
- **Martin describes legacy-modernization plans shrinking from six years to six months, while self-service AI apps create new integration work; neither claim demonstrates completed modernization or financial returns.** [43:02; 46:04] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/software-that-never-breaks-outsystems-ceo-woodson-martin-on-building-enterprise-grade-apps-at-ai-speed/)). At 43:02, he says customers are reconsidering difficult COBOL, AS/400 and Lotus Notes projects, citing insurance case-management modernization now planned over six months instead of six years. He does not identify a completed project meeting that schedule. He also describes a changed backlog: business users build dashboards themselves, then IT must reconcile common data definitions, access controls and integrations. At 46:04, he names Axos Bank and YESCO as productivity examples but explicitly leaves financial results to the customers and says not every AI investment will pay off. The opportunity is broader modernization and integration demand; the interview does not quantify its conversion into revenue, margins or aggregate productivity.

### [Software That Never Breaks: OutSystems CEO Woodson Martin on Building Enterprise-Grade Apps at ...](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/software-that-never-breaks-outsystems-ceo-woodson-martin-on-building-enterprise-grade-apps-at-ai-speed/)

The Cognitive Revolution · Relevance 4/5

OutSystems CEO Woodson Martin describes an architecture in which coding agents edit an application model and the platform generates code from established components. He reports major-feature releases rising from 4 to 19 to 26 across successive quarters, followed by lower token spend after investing in an internal harness and model router. Customer adoption remains constrained by model approval and compliance, while a new internal agent recommends automation opportunities from application telemetry. Modernization timelines and projected returns remain plans and estimates rather than demonstrated outcomes.

**Why it matters:** The interview gives specific mechanisms through which an established application platform can benefit from coding agents: reusable components, enterprise context, cheaper model routing, and visibility into existing workflows. It also separates faster development from permission to deploy and from realized financial returns. OutSystems sponsors this episode; the operating figures and customer anecdotes are management reports, not independent measurements.
