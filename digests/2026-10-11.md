# 2026-10-11

## TL;DR

- **Nishball's PORTS example shows how NVIDIA can secure future GPU demand through credit support, but the $105 billion figure is capped contingent lease-and-power support, not a GPU resale-value guarantee or current cash expenditure.** [01:15:10;01:15:21;01:15:29] ([SemiAnalysis Weekly](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--037---Whos-Funding-the-11-Trillion-AI-Buildout--Capital-Markets--Dan-Nishball--Jordan-Nanos-e3q81p6)). At 01:15:10 he calls the PORTS arrangement residual-value guarantees and links it to NVIDIA hardware exclusivity. [NVIDIA's fiscal Q2 2027 filing, Note 10](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm) instead specifies support for defined lease and power payments on 4.25 GW, triggered by tenant defaults. Exposure activates in nine phases, with the first expected in fiscal 2029 and declines as obligations are paid; an additional 3.8 GW is discretionary. The distinction changes both timing and the risk being insured.
- **Nanos reports one-year H100 rentals approaching $3 per GPU-hour and some renewals roughly doubling; he attributes the repricing primarily to unavailable newer GPUs, rather than superior Hopper inference economics.** [35:45;36:18;36:38;37:25;38:05;38:36;39:34;41:25;42:40] ([SemiAnalysis Weekly](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--037---Whos-Funding-the-11-Trillion-AI-Buildout--Capital-Markets--Dan-Nishball--Jordan-Nanos-e3q81p6)). He describes roughly $1.30 contracts renewing around $2.50 and says research users also value familiar software and numerical stability. These are his market observations, not matched-provider averages or a universal quote. Nishball says their price survey diverged from a hardware-performance-based depreciation model in late 2025. SemiAnalysis' [published index methodology](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/the-great-gpu-shortage-rental-capacity) uses surveys and transaction checks, supporting the distinction from advertised on-demand prices. Rental cash flow and hardware resale value remain different measures: current scarcity does not establish a permanent residual-value floor when Blackwell supply becomes available.
- **Bigelow says much of the apparent token-by-token instability in resampling plots is sampling noise; preserving sharp changes while smoothing stable stretches can make uncertainty analysis substantially cheaper.** [1:21:09] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/how-agents-decide-goodfire-s-eric-bigelow-on-critical-tokens-phase-shifts-in-context-learning/)). At 1:21:09 he explains that heavier resampling reveals smooth regions separated by real jumps, motivating Forking Fast. The [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19611v1) tests 100 tinyMMLU questions on Llama-3-8B-Instruct and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B. For Llama at four-token spacing, smoothing produces effective sample-size multipliers of 5.0× with five samples and 3.3× with 30. These are estimation-efficiency results, not model accuracy gains or measured production savings. Longer agent trajectories, larger models and open-ended outcomes remain untested; wider sampling intervals reduce precision around branch points. The paper recommends point estimates because its nominal 90% credible intervals under-cover, so this is not a calibrated confidence-interval service.

## More findings

- **Nishball separates a neocloud project's customer credit from its delivery risk; Nanos explicitly warns that a ClusterMAX tier cannot substitute for underwriting the particular site and contract.** [27:18;27:42;27:57;28:51;30:10;30:40;31:20] ([SemiAnalysis Weekly](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--037---Whos-Funding-the-11-Trillion-AI-Buildout--Capital-Markets--Dan-Nishball--Jordan-Nanos-e3q81p6)). Nishball compares a hyperscaler-backed CoreWeave project with the customer's own credit spread and treats the additional spread as execution risk. That decomposition is an analyst approximation, not an independently identified risk premium. [CoreWeave's DDTL 4.0 presentation](https://s205.q4cdn.com/133937190/files/doc_presentations/2026/Mar/2026-03-DDTL-4-Overview.pdf) confirms an $8.5 billion facility rated A3/A(low), floating pricing at SOFR+225 bps and an investment-grade underlying customer; it does not name that customer. Its project rating is not CoreWeave's corporate rating, and non-recourse parent treatment does not remove consolidated debt. Nanos notes that managed-cluster tests and large bare-metal builds are different products, with teams and execution varying by site.
- **Nishball forecasts $11.3 trillion of cumulative AI IT and data-center capex over 2024–2029 and approximately $7 trillion of debt outstanding by 2029; these are different modeled quantities, not one year's spending or already secured funding.** [06:38;06:56;07:08;07:34;07:58;08:38;08:50;09:06;10:50;11:08;11:25;01:08:15;01:10:26] ([SemiAnalysis Weekly](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--037---Whos-Funding-the-11-Trillion-AI-Buildout--Capital-Markets--Dan-Nishball--Jordan-Nanos-e3q81p6)). He builds capex from accelerator shipments and system costs, including networking and CPUs, plus data-center construction across hyperscalers, neoclouds and independent developers. Nanos says upstream fabs and power plants are outside this particular total. Nishball separately discusses debt amortization and net financing additions. His roughly $400 billion deal tally since June mixes about $260 billion described as raised with approximately $150 billion reported but not documented, so it cannot be treated as all closed or drawn. The episode supplies the model's scope, but not sufficient inputs to independently reproduce its forecast or establish an apples-to-apples comparison with US auto-loan or securities-issuance markets.
- **Nishball argues that NVIDIA's AI Cloud Partner arrangements can make shorter customer contracts financeable by replacing some demand risk with NVIDIA counterparty exposure; the support also gives NVIDIA a route to revenue sharing.** [16:22;16:44;32:10;34:12;01:12:08;01:12:31;01:12:52;01:13:11;01:14:04] ([SemiAnalysis Weekly](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--037---Whos-Funding-the-11-Trillion-AI-Buildout--Capital-Markets--Dan-Nishball--Jordan-Nanos-e3q81p6)). He contrasts lenders' preference for long investment-grade offtake with operators seeking smaller or shorter-duration customers. Nanos describes some buyers booking six months ahead and prepaying 30–50% of contract value, an anecdotal financing burden rather than a market-wide requirement. [NVIDIA's filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm) confirms $36 billion of typically six-year AI cloud commitments as of July 26, reduced by third-party usage, with conditional revenue sharing. Nishball describes a 50% share above a rental floor; the filing does not establish that percentage as universal. His mechanism broadens bankability, but does not establish that all customers can now obtain credit or that execution risk disappears.
- **Nishball's conclusion is that backstops buy time for independent underwriting; NVIDIA alone cannot support the whole buildout even in his capacity scenario, and the $500 billion financing initiative is a mobilization target, not funded capital.** [01:15:42;01:16:09;01:16:16;01:16:54;01:17:09;01:17:21;01:17:26] ([SemiAnalysis Weekly](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--037---Whos-Funding-the-11-Trillion-AI-Buildout--Capital-Markets--Dan-Nishball--Jordan-Nanos-e3q81p6)). He explicitly labels roughly 46 GW as a modeled support capacity rather than a forecast, against approximately 240 GW of buildout in his discussion, and has not performed the equivalent exercise for hyperscalers. His senior/junior tranche diagram is a proposed structure, not an executed deal. [NVIDIA's August 10 announcement](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Partners-With-Apollo-BlackRock-Blackstone-Brookfield-Goldman-Sachs-and-KKR-to-Establish-AI-Compute-Infrastructure-Financing-Platforms-to-Mobilize-Over-500-Billion-of-Third-Party-Capital/default.aspx) describes memorandums of understanding to mobilize over $500 billion of third-party capital over time, subject to final agreements. The implication is to track closed facilities, risk allocation and borrower cash flows rather than count the headline as an unconditional NVIDIA guarantee.
- **Bigelow's resampling experiments show why a confident final answer can conceal a fragile decision: an earlier single token, sometimes punctuation, can redirect the eventual outcome.** [06:08; 10:41; 1:12:41] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/how-agents-decide-goodfire-s-eric-bigelow-on-critical-tokens-phase-shifts-in-context-learning/)). At 06:08 he describes resampling roughly 30 continuations at each token in GPT-3.5 reasoning and observing abrupt changes in final-answer distributions. At 1:12:41 he distinguishes that distribution from proof that the model internally represents every possible continuation. The [original study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07961) covers seven tasks across four domains. This is conditional outcome variability in an older model, not calibrated probability of correctness or a universal claim that all modern reasoning is random. The practical implication is to test alternative continuations around consequential decisions, rather than infer robustness from the last answer token.
- **Bigelow has reduced his confidence in chain-of-thought monitoring: optimizing final outcomes need not preserve human-readable reasoning, while enforcing readability need not preserve faithfulness.** [1:32:52; 1:39:20] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/how-agents-decide-goodfire-s-eric-bigelow-on-critical-tokens-phase-shifts-in-context-learning/)). At 1:32:52 he explains his changed view as a risk from reinforcement learning shifting intermediate language away from ordinary human communication. This is a mechanism-level concern, not evidence that every unusual token encodes deception. His coauthored [Reasoning Theater paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05488) provides narrower evidence: probes recover some answers before the visible reasoning discloses them in DeepSeek-R1 and GPT-OSS, especially on easier tasks, while harder tasks show genuine reasoning and inflection behavior can track uncertainty. The implication is to validate monitoring against behavior and internal signals where available, rather than treating readable traces as a complete safety guarantee.
- **Bigelow reports completing the Forking Fast research with Silico within a week, but credits human problem selection and ongoing guidance as essential to the result.** [1:46:24] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/how-agents-decide-goodfire-s-eric-bigelow-on-critical-tokens-phase-shifts-in-context-learning/)). At 1:46:24 he says several exploratory directions went nowhere, and that noticing smoother curves at high sample counts led him to choose the statistical-model approach. He recommends supplying experimental context, explaining why results look wrong, and retaining control of the scientific narrative. The [paper's LLM-use statement](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19611v1) confirms that Silico produced the research and initial manuscript under human direction. The one-week timeline is his account; there is no controlled productivity baseline, compute budget or evidence of autonomous project selection.

### [Ep. 037 - Who's Funding the $11 Trillion AI Buildout? (Capital Markets) | Dan Nishball, Jordan Nanos](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--037---Whos-Funding-the-11-Trillion-AI-Buildout--Capital-Markets--Dan-Nishball--Jordan-Nanos-e3q81p6)

SemiAnalysis Weekly · Relevance 5/5

Daniel Nishball and Jordan Nanos examine how customer credit, execution risk and NVIDIA support determine which AI infrastructure projects can obtain financing. Their most useful additions to the prior token-economics discussion are the separation of project risk from the operator's corporate rating, rising H100 contract prices despite newer hardware, and the different obligations hidden under the term backstop. The $11.3 trillion buildout and $7 trillion debt figures are SemiAnalysis forecasts; NVIDIA's filings also materially qualify the episode's description of the $105 billion PORTS support.

**Why it matters:** Strong demand and attractive modeled inference margins do not by themselves make a project financeable. The important questions are who must pay, when guarantees activate, whether a specific site can deliver, and how cash flows behave when scarcity ends. Those distinctions affect the financing capacity of neoclouds and the contingent exposure NVIDIA accumulates to enable hardware sales.

### [How Agents Decide: Goodfire's Eric Bigelow on Critical Tokens, Phase Shifts, & In-Context Learning](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/how-agents-decide-goodfire-s-eric-bigelow-on-critical-tokens-phase-shifts-in-context-learning/)

The Cognitive Revolution · Relevance 4/5

Goodfire researcher Eric Bigelow explains how single sampled tokens can redirect an LLM's eventual answer, why final-answer confidence can conceal uncertainty earlier in a reasoning chain, and how statistical smoothing makes that uncertainty cheaper to study. He has become less confident in chain-of-thought monitoring, favors context and memory over per-user fine-tuning in the near term, and describes a research workflow in which Silico ran experiments while humans selected the scientific direction. The strongest evidence comes from specific resampling studies; his claims about future personalization and frontier-model generalization remain judgments.

**Why it matters:** Agent evaluations should measure alternative trajectories, rather than assuming one plausible reasoning trace explains a decision. Bigelow's work offers a concrete way to examine where answers become committed, plus an approach to reduce the cost of that examination. It also exposes two limits: small-model research may miss behaviors requiring frontier capabilities, and readable reasoning does not establish faithful reasoning. These are actionable distinctions for teams building evaluations, monitoring and research agents.
