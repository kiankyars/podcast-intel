Separately, at 01:08:23–01:08:43, he says his own restaurant employee-retention startup redesigned onboarding around delivering an actionable warning of a possible employee departure within the first two weeks. That firsthand example concerns early customer value, not a quantified churn reduction or proof the prediction was accurate.

Three findings from one episode. This is a practical product-development discussion rated 3/5 for relevance; the examples do not establish new company growth, retention or margin results.

### [An 86-minute guide to finding PMF | Rob Snyder (Author, The Power of Pull)](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/an-86-minute-guide-to-finding-pmf)

Lenny's Podcast · Relevance 3/5

Rob Snyder's PMF playbook is mostly familiar, but three examples sharpen how to evaluate AI applications: Jump's finance-specific workflow requirements, paid service delivery as a test before automation, and an unnamed horizontal AI vendor narrowing to freight forwarders after examining retention. The evidence is practitioner experience and advice, not audited growth or unit economics.

**Why it matters:** AI application traction needs separate tests for workflow fit, willingness to pay, and repeatable delivery to customers who stay. A polished demo, a helpful design partner, or a signed services deal can pass one test while failing the others. These examples identify useful failure mechanisms without establishing a company's market size or software margins.
