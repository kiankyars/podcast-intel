## TL;DR

- **Patel cites METR's estimate of roughly doubled progress at Anthropic as evidence that AI-assisted research is already accelerating model development; the underlying reports retain substantial uncertainty and human supervision.** [00:01:07; 00:01:22; 00:01:45; 00:01:53] ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsi)). The [November 5 METR assessment](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/) estimates about 2x progress on well-specified tasks, with 1.3x or 3x both plausible, and judges that most current acceleration likely comes from AI. It distinguishes that subjective causal judgment from a 1.9x AECI trend estimate, notes extensive evidence redactions and says contributions remain primarily assisted. Patel also cites Claude leading 26% of R&D work by August, up from under 1% in February. [Anthropic's definition](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development) means completing most of a task from a high-level prompt under human supervision; the measured fully autonomous share was zero. These are stronger indicators than coding-agent expenditure alone, but neither establishes that entire researcher jobs or frontier model-development cycles have been automated.

- **Malani says every poker bot must decide within one millisecond; the rules make this a test of AI-assisted software construction followed by tightly constrained execution.** [01:34:00; 01:34:34; 01:34:39; 01:34:50] ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/16016545)). Thus a winning bot is not evidence that an online LLM itself makes good poker decisions within one millisecond; strategy can be encoded in generated code and local models.

## More findings

- **Patel moves his median forecast for matching the best human AI researchers to 2028, partly because he now thinks extended in-context learning could overcome the memory bottleneck, while retaining at least a 25% chance of reaching that milestone only after 2032.** [00:00:04; 00:03:17; 00:08:18; 00:08:28; 00:10:00; 00:34:48; 00:35:12; 00:36:26] ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsi)). His forecast assumes labs continue at full speed without voluntary or government slowdowns. He previously expected a much longer path and considered missing continual learning a major obstacle; now he argues that months-long effective sessions or automated research into learning algorithms could suffice. This remains a hypothesis: the linked [Cartridges paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06266) studies offline-trained context representations and benchmark improvements, not months of successful on-the-job learning. His mathematical analogy also needs qualification. [OpenAI's September 8 announcement](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/) reports a forced Navier-Stokes counterexample from an internal multiagent system, with human decisions about resource allocation and follow-up prompts; the 88-hour search was followed by 17 hours of formalization. That company-reported result, already covered in the September 9 digest, is background for Patel's extrapolation, not independent proof of autonomous AI-research scaling or a new November discovery.
- **Patel argues that verifiable objectives across pretraining, post-training and inference can train research agents and coordinate parallel work; the critical unresolved step is transferring local benchmark gains into better frontier models.** [00:27:31; 00:28:02; 00:29:12; 00:29:25; 00:31:42; 00:32:46; 00:33:13] ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsi)). His proposed objectives include compute needed to reach a held-out loss, data needed to learn a new environment, and latency or GPU utilization. He suggests training agents to predict whether improvements at 100 million parameters survive at 10 billion; this is a proposed research-taste environment. The [nanoGPT speedrun](https://github.com/KellerJordan/modded-nanogpt) demonstrates a bounded competition targeting a specified validation loss on eight H100s. [Anthropic's CoBench description](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/f61d49fa5596956a5dec75fea0e973bf6a6a8378/Redacted%20Risk%20Report%20August%202026%20.pdf) specifies 449 difficulty-filtered historical root-cause problems, largely from February-April, graded against known solutions; it does not establish deployment of Patel's proposed RL loop. [Magic's pretraining report](https://magic.dev/blog/pretraining), which he also cites, explicitly says many improvements to tiny models fail at larger scale and describes testing changes across multiple scales. These sources support measurable subproblems while leaving end-to-end research judgment and transfer unproven.
- **Patel argues that data need not remain an externally supplied bottleneck because models can improve the pipelines that construct and verify their next training tasks; DeepSeek offers a concrete, bounded example of that mechanism.** [00:38:35; 00:38:40; 00:39:23; 00:39:27; 00:39:41] ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsi)). He points to automated filtering, synthetic data and RL-environment construction as research work that can itself be optimized against measured outcomes. Section 5.1 of the linked [DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19969) says the release retained established SFT, RL and on-policy-distillation methods and attributes essentially all observed post-training gains to better synthesized data and environments. It describes training task constructors on difficulty and correctness, plus re-auditing tasks using subsequent rollout evidence. The same report says task construction remains imperfect and describes inputs from employee and partner workflows. This is a developer-reported training pipeline, not evidence that human feedback has disappeared or that self-generated environments will cover every domain. The potentially consequential shift is that part of the data bottleneck becomes an automatable engineering and verification problem.
- **Patel assigns equal odds to a compute-limited burst of three to six years of AI progress in one year and to a software-only singularity; his mechanism relies on cheaper, better-designed experiments, not a demonstrated elimination of compute constraints.** [00:11:46; 00:15:15; 00:15:44; 00:15:59; 00:40:37; 00:42:10; 00:43:21] ([Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsi)). In his burst scenario, diminishing returns eventually require more chips and datacenters. He proposes substituting abundant agent labor for wasteful experiments and learning more from small runs. The [GPT-4 technical report](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.08774) supports one narrow precedent: predicting final held-out code loss from similarly trained models using up to 10,000x less compute; it does not show that arbitrary new research ideas transfer. His numerical compute argument is conditional on continued quadrupling. The [written essay's footnote 16](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsi) qualifies the audio's nearly 10,000 agents each running a GPT-3-sized experiment daily: the projected whole end-2027 fleet supports about 7,660 such runs per day, and allocating one-third to experiments gives each of 10,000 agents roughly one run every four days. These are scenario calculations, not announced fleet capacity, measured utilization or agent research productivity.

At 00:14:34–00:14:51 he separates the existing product trajectory from longer-term strategic decisions, in the context of retaining the technical team. The [November 12 announcement](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-CEO-Transition/default.aspx) says the board expects uninterrupted operations, customer commitments and roadmap execution. Those statements establish the transition and management's expectation; they do not verify Kim's two-to-three-year quality forecast or guarantee delivery, market share or future innovation.
- **Malani uses unfamiliar poker variants to require new strategy: Boston competitors had to bid over which three of five proposed cards would become the flop.** [01:33:14; 01:37:21; 01:37:26; 01:37:56; 01:38:05] ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/16016545)). This is a concrete attempt to test adaptation to newly specified rules, not proof of freedom from all prior-code reuse or training-data influence.
- **Malani reports that the winning CMU regional team classified opponents from roughly 15 hands and adjusted its strategy to exploit their playing styles.** [01:32:56; 01:33:01; 01:33:07; 01:33:20; 01:33:35; 01:33:49] ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/16016545)). The organizer's account shows a specific learning mechanism under runtime constraints; no classifier code, accuracy, ablation or controlled estimate of its contribution to winning is supplied.
- **Patel and Schneider prioritize controls on semiconductor manufacturing capability, while distinguishing that policy from restrictions on remote access to GPUs; the proposed legal mechanisms are broader and more conditional than a simple choice between selling chips and banning all rentals.** [02:50; 04:02; 22:46; 23:20; 26:16] ([SemiAnalysis Weekly](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--044---Is-NVIDIA-Over--Emergency-Pod-with-ChinaTalk--Dylan-Patel--Jordan-Nanos--Jordan-Schneider-ChinaTalk-e3sagjc)). ### [The avalanche that hit math is coming for AI development](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsi)

Dwarkesh Podcast · Relevance 4/5

In this solo essay, Dwarkesh Patel advances his median date for AI systems matching the best human AI researchers to 2028 and expects expert-level capability across fields by 2030. His consequential argument is that verifiable research objectives, automated training-data production and cheaper experiments could turn current researcher assistance into a compounding development loop. METR's assessment supplies evidence of acceleration inside Anthropic, but the dates, magnitude of a subsequent takeoff and transfer to general expertise remain Patel's forecasts. Mathematical results and historical-task benchmarks do not establish autonomous frontier research or economy-wide deployment.

**Why it matters:** The useful update is a testable account of where AI development could accelerate: research automation, data generation, sample efficiency and experiment design. It also identifies what would keep compute scarce even after labor becomes abundant. The assessment supports watching measured research throughput and frontier-scale transfer; it does not establish Patel's timelines, unlimited returns to agent swarms or the disappearance of physical infrastructure constraints.

Tae Kim offers a conditional path for Nvidia after Jensen Huang's departure: retain its senior technical and financial leaders, give them clear authority and choose a CEO who complements their capabilities. He separates the next two to three years of products from strategic decisions that could matter three to five years out. These are succession judgments, not evidence of executive departures, completed retention packages or a changed roadmap. This clip duplicates the Tae Kim segment in the November 13 full TBPN show; findings appear only here, with clip timestamps.

**Why it matters:** Kim adds a specific counterpoint to yesterday's concern that Nvidia could lose Huang's ability to coordinate decisions: distributed technical leadership might preserve execution if key people stay and receive real authority. That makes retention and decision rights observable tests of the transition. Near-term product delivery alone would not resolve the longer-term strategic question he raises.

### [TSMC Hacked, Vibes Down Outlook Down, NVIDIA v2 | Tae Kim, Ben Smith, Gunjan Banerji, Abhishek Malani](https://share.transistor.fm/s/16016545)

TBPN · Relevance 3/5

Abhishek Malani explains how Sequoia's No Limit competition tests AI-assisted bot development: strict runtime limits, unfamiliar poker rules and opponent adaptation. The official rules establish a separation between using models to write code and the submitted bots making decisions. His account of the Pittsburgh winner supplies a concrete adaptive strategy, but no source code or controlled comparison. The Tae Kim interview also appears in this full show and is analyzed separately using the clip's timestamps; its findings are not repeated here.

**Why it matters:** This is a useful evaluation design for code-producing agents: test the generated program under a real execution budget and changed task rules, then measure its interaction with other programs. The competition can reveal rapid engineering and strategic adaptation, but it does not directly rank foundation models or establish the reliability of autonomous agents in production.

### [Ep. 044 - Is NVIDIA Over? Emergency Pod with ChinaTalk | Dylan Patel, Jordan Nanos, Jordan Schneider (ChinaTalk)](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--044---Is-NVIDIA-Over--Emergency-Pod-with-ChinaTalk--Dylan-Patel--Jordan-Nanos--Jordan-Schneider-ChinaTalk-e3sagjc)

SemiAnalysis Weekly · Relevance 3/5

Dylan Patel, Jordan Nanos and ChinaTalk's Jordan Schneider debate where US semiconductor restrictions should act. Their most useful incremental contribution is the distinction between limiting manufacturing equipment through MATCH and regulating remote compute through RASA, plus a substantive disagreement over whether controlled cloud access preserves US leverage or gives up scarce capacity and strategic advantage. The Nvidia succession discussion largely overlaps other coverage; the retained findings focus on the policy mechanisms and their unresolved counterfactuals. The RSS dates the episode November 12; the matching official video was uploaded November 13. Timestamps refer to that video.

**Why it matters:** Equipment restrictions and remote-access restrictions affect different parts of the AI supply chain. The former target the ability to manufacture at scale and maintain production tools; the latter target who can use compute already deployed elsewhere. Their economic and strategic effects depend on implementation, substitution, enforcement and retaliation. The conversation sharpens those assumptions without establishing an optimal access level or proving either side's forecast.
