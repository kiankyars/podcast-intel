# 2026-07-12

## TL;DR

- **Catanzaro says Nemotron's first job is to help NVIDIA design future accelerated-computing systems in a post-Moore-law world.** [22:19] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/Inside-Nemotron--NVIDIAs-AI-Lab--Bryan-Catanzaro-e3li7o6)). He says NVIDIA has to deeply understand AI to co-design systems and software, and that specialization now requires understanding rather than simple transistor shrinkage.
- **NVIDIA's open-model strategy is explicitly ecosystem development, not an attempt to be the only AI-model provider.** [23:20] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/Inside-Nemotron--NVIDIAs-AI-Lab--Bryan-Catanzaro-e3li7o6)). Catanzaro says whenever AI is further developed and deployed it creates opportunity for NVIDIA's business, so open technology lets companies build and deploy their own AI on NVIDIA platforms.
- **Nemotron Ultra and Super were pretrained in NVFP4, which Catanzaro frames as an energy and throughput answer to hard compute and power limits.** [35:42] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/Inside-Nemotron--NVIDIAs-AI-Lab--Bryan-Catanzaro-e3li7o6)). He says FP4 pretraining required invention because training can diverge, but Blackwell Ultra has higher throughput on these formats and much lower memory/energy movement costs.

## More findings

- **NVIDIA found hybrid state-space/attention models can be smarter than either architecture alone while lowering sequence-memory requirements.** [39:48] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/Inside-Nemotron--NVIDIAs-AI-Lab--Bryan-Catanzaro-e3li7o6)). Catanzaro says their sweep found mostly state-space plus some attention had lower perplexity, with constant-size SSM cache improving GPU utilization for long sequences.
- **Catanzaro says Blackwell NVL72 was built around MoE inference because dynamic token routing requires fast, low-latency GPU-to-GPU memory access.** [42:31] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/Inside-Nemotron--NVIDIAs-AI-Lab--Bryan-Catanzaro-e3li7o6)). He says NVL72 lets up to 72 GPUs read and write each other's memory so experts can be partitioned across GPUs while tokens route dynamically through layers.
- **Nemotron's latent MoE compresses routed vectors to reduce NVLink communication and claims four times as many experts for the same inference cost.** [46:00] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/Inside-Nemotron--NVIDIAs-AI-Lab--Bryan-Catanzaro-e3li7o6)). Catanzaro says latent MoE down-projects token vectors before network transfer, uncompresses them later, saves bandwidth, and expands the expert library at fixed cost.
- **Multi-token prediction is positioned as a direct agent-inference cost lever, especially for low-batch interactive workloads.** [49:31] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/Inside-Nemotron--NVIDIAs-AI-Lab--Bryan-Catanzaro-e3li7o6)). Catanzaro says fetching weights dominates at low batch size, so predicting multiple tokens can reuse the same weights, preserve accuracy through verification, and offer roughly 3x to 4x speed or cost improvement when acceptance rates are high.
- **Isaiah Taylor says Valar used the DOE testing pathway, revived by EO14301, to break the nuclear data/regulatory chicken-and-egg problem.** [07:17] ([No Priors]()). He contrasts NRC commercial deployment review with DOE testing authority and says the reactor behind them went critical under an order calling for three advanced reactors on US soil by July 4.
- **Taylor says Valar is already running a live 100 kW reactor and collecting empirical data, not just producing simulation-backed paper reactors.** [10:37] ([No Priors]()). He says the company is making 100 kW and splitting about 10^17 atoms per second in the reactor while recording operational data.
- **Valar frames nuclear economics as a manufacturing tick-rate problem rather than a fuel-cost problem.** [24:36] ([No Priors]()). Taylor says first atom split took 2 years and 4 months, the next reactor took about 7 months, and the target is eventually new reactors turning on every few minutes because uranium fuel is already cheap.

## Episodes

### [How Nuclear Will Unlock Energy Abundance with Valar Atomics Founder Isaiah Taylor]()

No Priors · Relevance 5/5

Valar Atomics argues it has moved nuclear from paper design to hardware iteration: a DOE-authorized live reactor, a nuclear-powered Blackwell demo, aggressive verticalization, and a gigasite strategy aimed at AI-scale power buyers.

**Why it matters:** If Valar's execution claims hold, nuclear for AI data centers may be less a 2031-2032 procurement story and more a venture-backed manufacturing/tick-rate race. The episode gives concrete evidence for the company's theory of advantage rather than generic nuclear optimism.

### [Inside Nemotron & NVIDIA’s AI Lab | Bryan Catanzaro](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/Inside-Nemotron--NVIDIAs-AI-Lab--Bryan-Catanzaro-e3li7o6)

The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck · Relevance 5/5

Bryan Catanzaro explains Nemotron as NVIDIA's model-building instrument for post-Moore hardware/software co-design: open models grow the CUDA ecosystem, while FP4 pretraining, hybrid SSM/attention, MoE/NVL72, latent MoE, and multi-token prediction feed directly back into Blackwell-era inference economics.

**Why it matters:** The episode makes NVIDIA's open-model work look strategically central rather than charitable. Catanzaro ties model architecture choices to GPU architecture, power limits, agent workloads, and NVIDIA's inference-cost roadmap.

### [Alignment with Awakening: Davidad on Moral Realism, AI Wisdom, & why His p(Doom) is Down to 5%](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/alignment-with-awakening-davidad-on-moral-realism-ai-wisdom-why-his-p-doom-is-down-to-5/)

The Cognitive Revolution · Relevance 5/5

Davidad says his alignment worldview has shifted: formal proof systems are still valuable, but mainly as infrastructure for coalitions of aligned AIs rather than as the basis for a globally coordinated pause. He reports p(doom) below 5%, argues prosaic alignment is going better than expected, and redirects policy toward misuse safeguards and training-method choices.

**Why it matters:** This is a unusually explicit update from a formal-verification-oriented AI safety researcher. It changes the strategic emphasis from containment plus coordination to prosaic alignment, proof-mediated AI coalitions, misuse controls, and avoiding verifier-gamed training.

**Why it matters:** This is a concrete example of non-LLM generative AI crossing from benchmark performance into potentially workflow-changing scientific automation.
