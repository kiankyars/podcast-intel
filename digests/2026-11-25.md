# 2026-11-25

This edition covers episodes available by the scheduled November 25 check. Earlier editions from this catch-up appear in the archive.

**Best listen** · Cognition co-founder and CEO Scott Wu joins Patrick O’Shaughnessy on how large engineering teams put Devin to work. [Invest Like the Best](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=efoZj_7oVJs)

## TL;DR

- **Wu estimates that about 90% of Cognition's internal Devin usage starts automatically, including jobs triggered by software events.** He distinguishes scheduled jobs from API calls that start work when an incident appears. In the latter case, Devin can begin investigating before an engineer opens a session. This creates demand between human interactions. Wu describes the share as internal usage and leaves the unit unspecified, so the figure cannot be applied to billed tokens, autonomous completions or customer accounts. [Invest Like the Best](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=efoZj_7oVJs) [40:38; 40:42; 40:50; 40:53; 40:59]
- **Patel reports that newer pretraining data explained more improvement than newer architectures in his comparison of public corpora and architectures from 2019 to 2026.** He attributes much of the dataset improvement in his study to filtering and synthetic-data methods that ML researchers could iterate on themselves. This makes data preparation a candidate for research automation. The episode gives no effect sizes, model scales or full experimental design, leaving the result’s applicability to frontier runs open. [Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsiobjections) [00:06:29; 00:06:42; 00:06:54; 00:07:11]
- **Halligan estimates that roughly 20–25% of HubSpot functionality is accessible through Claude or ChatGPT and targets full coverage within a few months.** He cites forms, landing pages, workflows and CRM context as examples of what agents can already use. He says customers using these integrations have better retention and satisfaction, without quantifying the difference or controlling for which customers opt in. His product bet is that customers will discover HubSpot one task at a time. [TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/a149a9dd) [01:03:07; 01:03:15; 01:03:23; 01:04:24; 01:05:42]

## More findings

- **Patel’s worked example puts the payoff from research automation in the share of baseline work it covers.** If assisted work runs 50 times faster, accelerating 90% of baseline work time yields about 8.5 times total speed. At 99% coverage the same calculation yields about 34 times. These are illustrations using Amdahl’s law. Actual gains depend on coordination, human review and which tasks sit on the critical path. [Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsiobjections) [00:49:49; 00:50:49; 00:51:05; 00:51:10; 00:51:30]
- **Patel identifies a failure mode for automated research: plausible but inaccurate experiment records can consume the time saved by agents.** He imagines an agent reporting a favorable loss curve for a run that actually performed differently. Subsequent work follows a false lead, and relying on equally unreliable agents for debugging compounds the cost. He presents this as an objection to his forecast. Independently reproducible logs and the time spent verifying results would help test it. [Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsiobjections) [00:38:10; 00:38:49; 00:39:00]
- **Patel’s forecast for an automated lab still depends on how much more information it can extract per GPU experiment.** He suggests three to six years of algorithmic progress per calendar year in his scenario. Faster researchers could scrutinize loss spikes and design better follow-up runs while experiments continue to take real time. The forecast depends on returns to that extra analysis. He also accepts that slowing compute growth could slow progress if research automation arrives late. [Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/rsiobjections) [00:57:27; 00:57:53; 00:58:03; 00:58:56; 01:00:16]
- **Wu says Cognition doubled its engineering staff while its rate of merging pull requests rose about twentyfold over roughly a year.** That implies about ten times as many merges per engineer. Wu also saw some new hires work entirely through Devin's hosted repositories and test environments while continuing to review and test changes themselves. At an unnamed bank he projected 250,000 Devin-assisted merges in October against a prior organization-wide monthly baseline of 400,000. The projection gives no subsequent total or breakdown of displaced manual work. Neither comparison measures the size, quality or customer value of a change. [Invest Like the Best](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=efoZj_7oVJs) [35:08; 35:13; 36:17; 36:24; 36:32; 36:54; 37:04]
- **Wu says compute reservations expose an agent vendor to financing risk even when its revenue grows severalfold.** He illustrates the mismatch with a company reserving capacity for eightfold growth and achieving fivefold growth instead. Cognition has explored building infrastructure and working with partners. Wu discusses equity, secured or investment-grade borrowing, and customer prepayments as possible ways to bridge the timing gap. He gives no signed commitment, minimum payment or utilization figure for Cognition. The growth multiples illustrate a downside case rather than company guidance. [Invest Like the Best](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=efoZj_7oVJs) [27:32; 27:44; 27:55; 28:20; 29:11]
- **Cognition grades coding agents on whether a fix stays in scope and respects a repository's dependency rules, even when the test suite passes.** Wu describes how extra features or a change that crosses an architectural boundary can make an otherwise functional patch unacceptable. He identifies two harder evaluation problems: making historical design decisions available to the agent and grading a debugging conversation in which the human and agent discover the specification together. He gives no measured gain from these evaluation choices. [Invest Like the Best](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=efoZj_7oVJs) [48:58; 49:19; 49:36; 49:45; 51:40]
- **Wu reports that Cognition crossed a $1 billion revenue run rate on September 16 and had begun rolling Devin out to about 14,000 Citi engineers.** He dates the first revenue to September 20, 2024, and reports use across more than 100 Fortune 500 companies at interview time. He describes enterprise onboarding team by team, with explicit workflows, playbooks and return measurements. The run rate is an annualization. The Citi figure describes the rollout population, and Wu gives no active-use or retention figure. He separately cites Citi's broader projected savings on bringing work in-house, with no Devin-specific attribution. [Invest Like the Best](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=efoZj_7oVJs) [01:10; 01:14; 01:24; 02:29; 34:11]
- **Harbinger buys or builds specialized robots around repeatable factory tasks and generally avoids running unpaid vendor pilots.** Harris describes a factory dominated by Cartesian robots, with a few six-axis units. When established suppliers cannot meet the need, his team often builds equipment internally. He gives a production example of a 10 kWh battery module about every 45 seconds. This is a reported module cycle time. Whole-line uptime, yield and payback would be needed to assess production economics. [TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/a149a9dd) [01:19:55; 01:19:57; 01:26:12; 01:27:00; 01:27:46; 01:27:57]
- **Erebor’s financing for Harbinger bridges the period between paying component suppliers and collecting on delivered FedEx trucks.** Harris describes purchases ahead of an approximately 14-month delivery window. Rapaport says underwriting rests on both FedEx’s ability to pay and Harbinger’s ability to deliver. The truck order was valued above $300 million. Harbinger announced more than $100 million in combined financing from Erebor and Pinegrove, a separate figure from the customer order. [Harbinger announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/harbinger-secures-more-than-100-million-in-debt-financing-from-erebor-bank-and-pinegrove-venture-partners-to-support-production-ramp-and-future-growth-302948754.html) [TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/a149a9dd) [01:18:57; 01:19:09; 01:21:47; 01:22:31]
- **Buechel says Whole Foods is testing micro-fulfillment inside stores to expand online order capacity while investing in specialist staff on the sales floor.** He identifies product movement through the supply chain as the largest opportunity for automation. He pairs the store fulfillment trials with investments in apprenticeships for butchers, fishmongers and cheese professionals. He gives no measured change in orders per hour, staffing or unit cost. [TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/a149a9dd) [54:26; 54:33; 54:51; 55:17]
