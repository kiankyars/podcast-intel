## Episodes

### [Rethinking Legacy Data Infrastructure with Eon Co-Founders Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YscDZpVF4CQ)

**No Priors | relevance 4/5**

Eon co-founders Ofir Ehrlich and Gonen Stein argue that enterprise AI is turning historical corporate data from a passive backup asset into training material, application context, and a control problem. They describe Eon as a cross-cloud foundation that maps, classifies, ingests, and permission-controls structured and unstructured data for both recovery and AI use. Their strongest technical point is that autonomous agents operate with legitimate credentials and at much higher velocity than human users, so enterprises need continuous data discovery, anomaly detection, granular recovery, and non-human identity controls. The episode also suggests that AI adoption is compressing enterprise deployment cycles and favoring forward-deployed engineering, although the market anecdotes and product capabilities are founder-reported and not independently substantiated here.

**Why it matters:** If proprietary enterprise histories become scarce inputs for agent training and deployment, value may shift from narrow backup or point-pipeline products toward systems that combine cheap retention, semantic discovery, access control, and recoverability. The agent-security mechanism is especially consequential: damage can come from authorized identities rather than perimeter breaches, making blast-radius control and auditable recovery part of the AI application stack. Eon's claims imply a large infrastructure and security opportunity, but the interview provides no customer counts, performance benchmarks, pricing, or independently verified incident data, so adoption and product differentiation remain open questions.
