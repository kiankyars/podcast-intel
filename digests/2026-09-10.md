# 2026-09-10

## TL;DR

- **Armstrong says Coinbase is requiring code-review corrections to update persistent team and repository memory consumed by future coding agents.** [13:01; 13:43; 14:04; 14:18] ([No Priors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uLDK4l_-gUE)). He describes Markdown 'brains' containing incidents, financial controls, experiments, and accepted/rejected pull requests, integrated with Coinbase's Toshi harness. Reviewer corrections must flow back into that memory. He says one-shot PR acceptance then improves, but gives no measured improvement or baseline; this is a workflow-memory loop, not evidence of autonomous model-weight improvement.
- **Armstrong reports that about 76% of the agentic-commerce transactions Coinbase observes are below $0.30, with information purchases a prominent use case.** [06:47; 07:12; 07:23; 07:48] ([No Priors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uLDK4l_-gUE)). He contrasts these amounts with an approximately $0.30 flat card-processing charge and describes agents buying search, financial data, and specialized agents' outputs. The statistic concerns the transactions his team sees, not a measured share of all agent commerce; he supplies no sampling period or methodology.
- **Coinbase plans identity-linked agent accounts with segregated funds alongside a separate self-custodial-wallet route, according to Armstrong.** [04:51; 05:20; 05:41; 05:53] ([No Priors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uLDK4l_-gUE)). He says a near-future Coinbase account will let a human delegate trading and payments using a separate balance, while agents can also use self-custodial stablecoin wallets. He identifies payment handoffs as a recurring obstacle and expects stablecoin-backed cards to provide compatibility with existing merchants, alongside x402 payments.

He describes the next two or three years as focused on recursive self-improvement while biological training data accumulates elsewhere. Asked about releases, he promises activity this year, says the company is building products and talking to customers, but declines to disclose product specifics. - **Socher identifies CUDA-kernel generation for faster inference as an early practical output of Recursive's automated research system.** [01:08:35; 01:08:54; 01:09:09; 01:09:22] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/When-AI-Improves-Itself--Richard-Socher-Recursive-e3oiodo)). He says the narrow system has outperformed lengthy human efforts on particular research problems, can generate new CUDA kernels, and has received positive feedback from people using them. He positions these as intermediate artifacts toward recursive self-improvement. The interview gives no speedup figures; [Recursive's earlier benchmark release](https://www.recursive.com/articles/first-steps-toward-automated-ai-research) supplies company-reported comparisons and artifacts, but they were not independently reproduced for this digest.
- **Socher argues that affordable simulations and verifiers determine where AI can progress rapidly; today's biological data and simulation gaps prevent simply carrying software's improvement loop into biology.** [17:32; 18:03; 20:52; 21:31; 23:57; 24:31; 57:32] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/When-AI-Improves-Itself--Richard-Socher-Recursive-e3oiodo)). He ties superhuman performance to repeated experiments in simulations or verification tools, explicitly requiring experiments that do not take years. He contrasts software with cells that cannot yet be adequately simulated, calls for many more gene-knockout and molecular perturbation measurements, and says a comprehensive virtual cell does not yet exist.

## Episodes

### [When AI Improves Itself | Richard Socher (Recursive)](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/When-AI-Improves-Itself--Richard-Socher-Recursive-e3oiodo)

The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck · Relevance 4/5

Richard Socher describes Recursive's near-term strategy as automating AI research before extending into the natural sciences. He puts its financing at roughly $670 million, promises initial releases this year, and points to generated CUDA kernels as an early output. The useful counterweight to his broader optimism is a staged deployment thesis: cheap verification makes software attractive first, while biology still needs much more experimental data, better simulations, and time-consuming real-world validation.

**Why it matters:** The interview provides first-party information about Recursive's capital intensity, product timetable, and initial technical direction. It also separates the economics of software research from physical science: repeated digital experiments can be cheap, whereas biological discovery depends on data collection and validation that better models alone cannot immediately accelerate. Reported kernel results remain company claims without quantitative comparisons in this interview.

### [Coinbase’s Everything Exchange: Agentic Finance, Stablecoins, and Tokenization with CEO Brian Armstrong](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uLDK4l_-gUE)

No Priors · Relevance 4/5

Brian Armstrong describes Coinbase's shift toward agent-operated financial accounts and persistent memory for its internal coding agents. The strongest details are reviewer feedback written back into team/repository memory, a reported concentration of agent payments below $0.30, and a two-track agent-account architecture. He also discusses diversification beyond Bitcoin spot fees, tokenized equities outside the US, and NewLimit's AI-guided drug-discovery pipeline; commercial metrics and clinical timelines remain attributed management claims.

**Why it matters:** The interview connects two practical agent bottlenecks: retaining organizational knowledge across coding tasks and paying for external services without repeatedly handing control back to a human. It also gives concrete product and revenue markers for assessing Coinbase's broader financial-platform strategy, without establishing that crypto-cycle exposure has disappeared or that NewLimit's therapies work in humans.

### [Apple Unveils iPhone Duo, Muse AI Agent, Keith Rabois Joins | David Cahn, Mikey Shulman, David Perell, Jim Reilly, Fletcher Richman](https://share.transistor.fm/s/3cda42af)

TBPN · Relevance 4/5

Suno CEO Mikey Shulman outlines forthcoming opt-in artist products with user-attributable revenue sharing and explains the deliberate creativity-versus-song-quality tradeoff in V6 Wild. David Cahn discusses the proprietary technology and financing requirements behind Sequoia's energy investments, Fletcher Richman describes Type's nontechnical-team deployment niche, and Keith Rabois identifies a conditional IPO risk to AI valuations.

**Why it matters:** The interviews supply specific mechanisms for AI application economics and investment durability: how artists could participate in product revenue, why creative models need different optimization targets, where shared agent tooling finds demand, and why hardware intellectual property still needs exceptional access to capital. The IPO discussion is a downside scenario, not evidence that a market reset is imminent.
