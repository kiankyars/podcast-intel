## TL;DR

- **Vélez says Nubank is redesigning 120 workflows with AI while reducing management layers from ten to five and increasing his direct reports from one to twenty.** [50:54; 51:22; 52:11; 52:22; 52:57; 54:43] ([Invest Like the Best](https://colossus.com/episode/david-v-goliath/)). At 50:54–51:34 he lists coding, design, KYC, audit, compliance and regulatory capital management, says evaluations are needed throughout, and acknowledges uneven progress. At 52:11–52:25 he gives the organizational numbers; at 52:57–53:58 he attributes the change partly to AI and partly to deliberate management redesign. At 54:43–55:04 he describes an information system that proactively reports progress to him. This is first-party evidence of a broader transformation than buying coding tools, not proof that 120 workflows are fully autonomous, staffing halved or productivity doubled.

Vegetable cutting remains a use case under consideration, not a confirmed deployment. At 51:29 he puts Dyna’s total deployed fleet in the tens and targets hundreds next year. Dyna’s [deployment report](https://www.dyna.co/research/scaling-customer-deployments) also describes network expansion. This supports a company-reported repeat-customer signal, but the interview gives no contract value, site count, margin or audited payback.

The dedicated team is a reported operating decision; broad third-party product routing is a direction, with no disclosed agent-originated sales, integration coverage or conversion metrics.

## More findings

- **Dyna’s recipe combines million-hour human-video pretraining with small amounts of task-specific robot data, rather than eliminating robot adaptation.** [00:07:14] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/dyna)). At 07:14 Ma describes over one million hours of human pretraining; at 09:04–11:25 he explains why the human-to-robot embodiment gap still requires more costly robot data. At 30:05 he says adaptation can take tens of minutes to a few hours of demonstrations; the host’s 45-minute figure concerns a chopping example, not arbitrary tasks or production qualification. The [Dyna-2 report](https://www.dyna.co/dyna-2) supports the training recipe and adds an important distinction: its scaling-study policy learns from a future-video objective but does not generate or attend to predicted future video at inference. The podcast’s visual-reasoning analogy is not a precise description of every deployed variant.
- **Ma attributes hour-long laundry autonomy to a hierarchical agent system that schedules and combines skills around the world-action model.** [00:46:37] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/dyna)). At 33:20 Ma says the demonstrated laundry loop takes about an hour. At 46:37 he describes a higher-level harness deciding what to do while a washer runs, switching when machines finish, and composing low-level skills; training all of that into continuous motor actions would be inefficient. Dyna’s [Dyna-2.1 report](https://www.dyna.co/dyna-2.1) presents an uncut hour-long demonstration and separates workflow orchestration from policy and control. This is evidence of a demonstrated workflow architecture, not an intervention-rate estimate across customer sites or proof of general hour-long autonomy. Ma describes an SDK as a future offering.
- **Vélez sees proprietary financial-behavior models as a way to accelerate new-market entry, but the published evidence supports reusable methods with local data rather than zero-shot international underwriting.** [16:07; 18:24; 19:03; 19:11; 22:31] ([Invest Like the Best](https://colossus.com/episode/david-v-goliath/)). At 16:07–19:20 he connects customer history, the Hyperplane acquisition and proprietary models to credit decisions, fraud work and faster entry into new countries. At 22:31–23:14 he says the models reduce feature-experimentation effort. Nu's [Q2 results](https://nu.com/en/newsroom/company/nu-holdings-ltd-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results) confirm live credit use in Brazil and Mexico, while Colombian credit cards were still testing in August; this is distinct from his fraud claim. The [nuFormer paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23267v2) combines transaction-sequence representations with tabular features and uses local transactions for its second-market experiment. It does not establish immediate accuracy for a new country's borrowers without local adaptation.
- **Vélez describes open-finance advice that prompts customers to address liabilities at other banks; the cited fifteen-million scale should not be reported as verified daily active usage.** [13:23; 13:37; 13:49] ([Invest Like the Best](https://colossus.com/episode/david-v-goliath/)). At 13:23–13:57 he describes reminders about upcoming payments, funding gaps and refinancing opportunities using information across banks. This illustrates how financial assistants can compete for existing balances and loans rather than merely answer questions. His roughly fifteen-million figure sits beside an everyday-service description, but Nu's [Q1 results](https://nu.com/en/newsroom/company/nu-holdings-ltd-reports-first-quarter-2026-financial-results) explicitly define AI Private Banker usage as more than 15 million monthly active users. That is an earlier disclosed milestone, not a new October growth figure or proof that 15 million people use a fully packaged agent daily. Realized savings and refinancing conversion are not quantified here.
- **Vélez frames international expansion as the next test of Nubank's model, with US brand-building a key constraint; US banking products and Nu Global represent different routes to market.** [36:44; 38:37; 39:14; 01:15:03] ([Invest Like the Best](https://colossus.com/episode/david-v-goliath/)). At 36:44–39:18 he discusses the US launch, a stablecoin-backed global account and the difficulty of earning trust as a primary bank. Nu's [September 10 announcement](https://nu.com/en/newsroom/company/nu-united-states-nu-global) confirms the US launch through partner Lead Bank while Nu's own charter remained conditional. Nu Global announced transfers across more than 35 countries using USDC and EURC, with several integrations still planned at launch. Transfer coverage is not 35 newly licensed local banks. His 100-country vision at 01:15:03 is a multidecade ambition; neither it nor domestic economics establish successful international execution.
- **Nubank requires senior managers to build software quarterly so that their understanding of AI capabilities comes from use as well as reports.** [49:39; 50:04; 58:54; 59:22; 59:41] ([Invest Like the Best](https://colossus.com/episode/david-v-goliath/)). At 49:39–50:43 Vélez says earlier coding-model failures on Clojure left the company slow to recognize a later capability improvement. At 58:54–59:45 he describes reserving time to use tools and asking senior managers to build real software every quarter. His afternoon crypto-app example is a prototype, not a disclosed customer launch. His named-model chronology is inconsistent with [Anthropic's February 5, 2026 Opus 4.6 release](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-6), so the useful takeaway is repeated task-specific testing rather than his precise December dating. The interview provides no controlled estimate of this management practice's impact.
- **Ma identifies dexterous-hand durability and missing sensory information as limits that more video data alone may not overcome.** [00:33:20] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/dyna)). At 33:20–35:06 Ma says dexterous hands can overheat and lack durability, slowing both data collection and real-hardware evaluation. He contrasts tasks that parallel-jaw grippers can already perform with tasks requiring different hardware or senses. At 56:28 he explicitly allows that a vision-plus-touch model could be substantially more data efficient than a vision-only model because the additional sensor exposes information video misses. The hardware account is first-party experience; his hypothetical 100-fold improvement is not a measured result. The implication is that sensor coverage and evaluation throughput can constrain the value of larger training corpora.

### [David Vélez - Building Nubank and the Future of Global Banking - [Invest Like the Best, EP.495]](https://colossus.com/episode/david-v-goliath/)

Invest Like the Best · Relevance 4/5

David Vélez describes Nubank's AI transformation through concrete organizational changes: a redesign covering 120 workflows, management layers reduced from ten to five, a dedicated team studying agents as customers, and a requirement that senior managers build software quarterly. He connects proprietary transaction models and open-finance advice to international expansion. His account is stronger evidence of changed operating practices than of a quantified productivity gain: workflow maturity varies, adoption metrics require careful denominators, and the US partner-bank launch differs from Nu Global's international transfer coverage.

**Why it matters:** Nubank is testing AI across a regulated operating business with existing customers, transaction data and distribution. The useful questions are whether redesigned workflows preserve decision quality, whether agents change customer acquisition, and how much of a domestic data advantage transfers into a new market. Vélez supplies specific practices to track, while also acknowledging that organizational simplification itself creates gains. Headcount ratios, model improvements and customer outcomes should therefore be measured separately.

### [Dyna-2: General Purpose Physical Agents with Hour-Long Autonomy — Jason Ma, Dyna Robotics](https://www.latent.space/p/dyna)

Latent Space · Relevance 4/5

Dyna Robotics chief scientist Jason Ma explains the company’s route from a world-action model pretrained on over one million hours of human video to task adaptation and an hour-long laundry demonstration. The strongest commercial claim is expansion at Din Tai Fung beyond a pilot: Ma reports thousands of napkins folded daily across multiple locations, tens of robots deployed overall, and a target of hundreds next year. He also explains why workflow orchestration, local inference, hardware durability and missing sensory data can matter as much as model scale. These are first-party accounts and demonstrations, not independently established fleet reliability or unit economics.

**Why it matters:** Dyna offers a concrete test of whether human-video pretraining can reduce the expensive robot demonstrations needed for each customer task. Its account shifts the commercial evaluation from a successful manipulation demo to sustained workflow throughput, customer expansion and the human support still required. The interview also exposes constraints on a pure video-model strategy: physical hardware determines which data can be collected and how quickly improvements can be evaluated.
