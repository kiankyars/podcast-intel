## Episodes

### [Ep. 027 - OpenAI Jalapeño: Better Than Nvidia Blackwell (Accelerators)](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--027---OpenAI-Jalapeo-Better-Than-Nvidia-Blackwell-Accelerators-e3o3oms)

**SemiAnalysis Weekly | relevance 5/5**

SemiAnalysis's hosts present OpenAI's first-generation Jalapeño inference ASIC as a credible challenge to Nvidia's performance and economics after observing the chip and its InferenceX runs on site. They say the current A0 silicon beats GB300 and July-era Vera Rubin results on three reported models, including roughly twice GB300's tokens per megawatt at matched DeepSeek interactivity. They attribute the result to HBM4 bandwidth per watt, a microarchitecture designed for irregular inference shapes, and AI-assisted RTL and kernel development. The headline remains narrower than production superiority: the Rubin comparison uses older software, the principal workload is single-turn and non-agentic, the TCO is modeled rather than disclosed, and OpenAI has not yet demonstrated deployment at 100-megawatt scale.

**Why it matters:** The episode weakens the view that Nvidia's CUDA moat and peak-FLOPs lead prevent a frontier lab from building competitive silicon quickly. OpenAI may be able to convert privileged model access into faster chip design and software bring-up, avoid Nvidia's supplier margin, and optimize inference around scarce power rather than package count. That would pressure Nvidia's share of hyperscale inference and raise the strategic value of HBM, Broadcom, and system-integration capabilities. The remaining moat may lie increasingly in manufacturing scale, networking, deployment reliability, and supply-chain execution rather than kernel portability alone.
