# 2026-11-08

## TL;DR

- **Figma’s CFO reports that MCP has passed one million weekly active users and is feeding paid seats and native AI workflows, giving the incumbent a route to capture value from external agents.** [00:47:06; 00:47:15; 00:47:50; 00:49:35; 00:50:38; 00:50:56; 00:51:27] ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/b1ba345a)). Melwani reports just over one million MCP weekly active users, 3x overall usage growth and 4x canvas write-back growth. He says MCP initiated about 5% of quarterly credit consumption through first-party workflows, and associates MCP use with paid-team conversion, higher retention and account expansion; no comparable-cohort lift or causal test is supplied. Separate corroboration: Figma’s [November 4 results](https://investor.figma.com/news-events/news/news-details/2026/Figma-Announces-Third-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx) date the 3x/4x growth to Q3 versus Q2 and report $391.4 million Q3 revenue, up 43%, with 131% net dollar retention. Those are company-wide financial metrics, not revenue attributable to MCP. The useful mechanism is that an external agent can invoke a paid, context-rich Figma workflow rather than simply extract a file.
- **San Miguel says B300 contract revenue and headline hourly rental quotes describe different businesses; using the higher hourly number to value an entire contracted GPU fleet can misstate its economics.** [32:18; 32:52; 33:38; 34:46] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/ai-am-is-this-the-ai-we-want-alarming-acceleration-ai-election-bets-gpu-trading-ai-native-vc/)). In the November 5 conversation, he quotes about $550,000–$650,000 for a complete eight-GPU HGX B300 server, varying with configuration, OEM and warranty, and lower prices for bulk purchases. He contrasts approximately $4.20–$4.50 per GPU-hour under AI-factory rental contracts with roughly $8–$9 on hourly rental indices. These are his market observations, not verified completed trades or realized returns; contract duration, occupancy, service costs, financing and margins are unspecified. [NVIDIA’s reference architecture](https://docs.nvidia.com/enterprise-reference-architectures/hgx-ai-factory/latest/components.html) confirms eight B300 GPUs per node. Separately, [Luxor’s index methodology](https://luxor.tech/news/corporate-news/article/luxor-launches-ai-data-sets-on-hashrate-index) describes vendor asking prices rather than closed transactions. His suggestion that smaller deployments can earn better returns remains conditional on their actual utilization and costs.
- **Zhou’s election experiment separates forecast quality from profitable execution: latency and corrupted information between forecasting and trading agents can erase a useful prediction.** [01:55; 20:48; 22:47; 23:05] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/ai-am-is-this-the-ai-we-want-alarming-acceleration-ai-election-bets-gpu-trading-ai-native-vc/)). Zhou describes expert-competitive forecasting but emphasizes preliminary results with roughly 100–200 races still unresolved on November 6; agents often aggregate existing market and human forecasts. He says traders reacted too slowly on election day and sometimes invented or misparsed information in exchanges with their forecasting agents. His chamber-by-chamber ranking is not sufficiently clear or final to establish a universal winner. The [methodology](https://ballotbench.com/methodology) waits for certified results and cautions that correlated races and access to existing forecasts limit independent evidence. Separate [trading data](https://ballotbench.com/trading) distinguish $50 per agent in real Kalshi money from $10,000 simulated accounts; the displayed November 6 snapshot includes some profitable accounts. The claim is an execution failure mode, not that every agent lost money or that the $500 research budget was its trading stake.

10 findings from two episodes published November 6–7. AI:AM compiles discussions from November 5–6; its election-evaluation results were preliminary. Operator and company claims remain attributed, with separately checked primary-source context and limits on comparisons preserved.

## More findings

- **Melwani describes Figma using cheaper model execution and larger AI credit packages to stimulate demand while increasing its profit outlook.** [00:47:30; 00:52:23; 00:54:09; 00:55:13; 00:55:24; 00:55:56] ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/b1ba345a)). He reports 2.5x credit-consumption growth between the agent’s October 6 general availability and October-end, describes smaller first-party models exploiting Figma-specific context, and says model/architecture changes let some engineers run similar tasks for a fraction of earlier spending. He links serving and internal efficiencies to a higher full-year profit guide. Separate source context: Figma’s [credit FAQ](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/42614902212887-AI-credit-updates-FAQ) specifies that the August 25 repricing doubled Professional and increased Organization/Enterprise add-on and pay-as-you-go credits by 60% at unchanged cost; monthly seat credits did not change. It also says the agent began consuming credits at October 6 GA. Thus 2.5x credit consumption does not establish 2.5x paid revenue or comparable pre/post task demand. The [November 4 release](https://investor.figma.com/news-events/news/news-details/2026/Figma-Announces-Third-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx) identifies the raised profit outlook as non-GAAP and reports more than doubling the share of work handled by the first-party model from July 1–October 31, distinct from his spoken claim about doubling actions during the quarter.
- **Zipline’s CEO says the company quadrupled factory space and brought battery manufacturing in-house after a supplier failed, making manufacturing execution a concrete constraint on expansion.** [00:56:49; 00:57:06; 00:57:20; 00:57:56; 00:58:36; 00:58:58; 01:00:31; 01:00:48; 01:09:54] ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/b1ba345a)). During a factory walkthrough near SFO, Cliffton says Zipline builds aircraft, delivery droids, charging docks and partner drop boxes; an aircraft has 43 major subassemblies and 700 main components. He says a San Jose supplier failed to deliver adequately, prompting Zipline to buy equipment on eBay and take battery manufacturing inside. The fourfold change concerns factory space, not proven fourfold output. His statement that the site needs to build 50,000 aircraft annually is a production objective; no achieved throughput, yield, aircraft unit cost or utilization is disclosed. This is a specific supplier-recovery and vertical-integration account, with management-reported operational details rather than audited manufacturing performance.
- **Cliffton distinguishes Zipline’s current U.S. network from a much larger funded rollout: 35 operating sites precede 300 additional planned Walmart locations.** [00:59:27; 00:59:43; 01:03:28; 01:04:04; 01:04:37; 01:06:26; 01:06:56; 01:08:10] ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/b1ba345a)). He reports 35 U.S. sites, shows a Rowlett delivery feed, describes operating hours of 6 a.m.–11 p.m., and announces $1.5 billion of financing at a $21.5 billion valuation. Separate corroboration: Zipline’s [November 5 announcement](https://www.zipline.com/newsroom/zipline-raises-1-5-billion-to-build-nationwide-drone-delivery-network) specifies post-money valuation and manufacturing/hiring uses; [Walmart’s same-day release](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/11/walmart-expands-drone-delivery-network-with-zipline) calls the 300 sites additional locations under a multi-year agreement. They are not 300 already operating sites. Cliffton’s million-deliveries-per-day ambition with Uber is a future target, dated to 2029 in Zipline’s release; his early-2027 Los Angeles launch is also prospective. His extrapolation that national demand could make the delivery market ten times larger supplies no measured national demand or unit-margin evidence. Capital and partner commitment are concrete; rollout pace and profitable site utilization remain the tests.
- **Julius’s DeepFinance targets a diligence failure Sonwalkar sees in capable coding models: retrieving a revenue number without reconciling conflicting records or checking the assumptions behind it.** [01:12:08; 01:13:27; 01:13:59; 01:14:17; 01:14:25; 01:14:37; 01:15:19; 01:15:34; 01:15:45; 01:16:03; 01:16:21] ([TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/b1ba345a)). Sonwalkar describes messy data rooms, inconsistent management and audit figures, and assignments requiring financial models plus investment-committee recommendations. Julius used Codex for OpenAI models and Claude Code for Claude instead of its own harness; he says the research group has not decided how to monetize the work. Separate methodology context: Julius’s [November 5 publication, checked November 8](https://www.juliusresearch.ai/blog/deep-finance), describes 21 anonymized-data tasks averaging 27.1 expert hours and 84 rubric criteria. Scores are the share of criteria satisfied, not the share of complete deals successfully executed. Errored attempts are rerun to obtain ten successful attempts per task, so scores do not measure raw operational reliability. Provider-specific harnesses mean model-and-tool-stack comparisons, not a controlled base-model ranking. The actionable lesson is to evaluate source reconciliation and downstream assumption consistency alongside calculation accuracy.
- **Zhou reports that a long time budget did not ensure sustained research: some agents treated submitting every required forecast as completion and stopped after minimal work.** [15:26; 17:24] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/ai-am-is-this-the-ai-we-want-alarming-acceleration-ai-election-bets-gpu-trading-ai-native-vc/)). In BallotBench, nine agents received roughly a week and a $500 paid-information budget. Zhou says Muse Wonder produced lightly perturbed prior-election forecasts in minutes, then its task-list completion condition ended the session; the team restarted it in new containers with prior work available. He contrasts this with Opus 5.5 delegating roughly 170–180 subagents in a day, an anecdote about behavior rather than proof of better accuracy. The [published methodology](https://ballotbench.com/methodology) confirms 863 races, session restarts and the research budget, and explicitly evaluates models inside different agent tools. It lists Muse’s tool as Meta AI, so the interview’s Muse Code label is not treated as a verified product name. The failure implicates the model–harness combination, not an isolated model capability score.
- **San Miguel attributes some secondary GPU inventory to delayed deployment sites, while arguing that site constraints can preserve uses for older hardware even after newer GPUs arrive.** [35:52; 36:47; 37:24] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/ai-am-is-this-the-ai-we-want-alarming-acceleration-ai-election-bets-gpu-trading-ai-native-vc/)). He describes power delivery, cooling and data-center readiness as current bottlenecks, with near-term liquid-cooled capacity largely booked and some providers quoting Q3 2027 or 2028. When a customer’s site slips, an OEM may resell its allocated hardware and backfill later; that is a delivery mismatch, not necessarily evidence of speculative inventory or weak compute demand. On retired H100s, he argues that sites unable to support denser systems can still serve suitable workloads. [NVIDIA’s DGX Vera Rubin NVL72 guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/dgx/dgxvr-user-guide/hardware.html) confirms the rack’s liquid-cooled design, but the interview’s specific rack-power estimate is not generalized across Rubin products. The capacity dates and resale practices are his November 5 account, without a market-wide inventory survey or quantified residual-value model.
- **Bischof says Theory builds research agents to improve the investment process, and judges success by decision speed and human time recovered rather than research volume.** [47:28; 50:48; 57:19; 57:27; 59:33] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/ai-am-is-this-the-ai-we-want-alarming-acceleration-ai-election-bets-gpu-trading-ai-native-vc/)). He describes thousands of agent jobs, quarterly preregistered AI experiments by staff, and agents linking and enriching investors’ research. He proposes measuring how quickly a team goes from identifying an opportunity to an informed decision, and whether investors spend less time extracting routine website information and more time speaking with founders, customers and other knowledgeable people. His examples of 30% less website work or 20% less form filling are success criteria, not measured firmwide results; similarly, 10–100x effectiveness is an ambition. He says humans retain judgment over what to research and when to stop, and explicitly rejects generated page count as a success metric. This is a first-person workflow account, not demonstrated investment alpha or a completed fund-return evaluation.

Figma adds measured adoption to the product architecture covered on October 7, but credit consumption is not revenue and management reports do not establish causal retention lift. Zipline separates an operating network from contracted expansion and manufacturing targets. DeepFinance is evidence about evaluated model-and-harness configurations, not autonomous dealmaking or investment returns. Figma is also a listed sponsor of the show; its interview claims remain attributed and are checked against primary releases.

### [AI:AM: Is THIS the AI We Want? Alarming Acceleration, AI Election Bets, GPU Trading & AI-Native VC](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/ai-am-is-this-the-ai-we-want-alarming-acceleration-ai-election-bets-gpu-trading-ai-native-vc/)

The Cognitive Revolution · Relevance 4/5

This November 7 compilation draws on November 5–6 conversations with Thoughtful researcher Brian Zhou, Luxor GPU trader Mike San Miguel, and Theory Ventures head of AI Bryan Bischof. The strongest material concerns agent persistence, the gap between forecasting and trading, GPU contract economics, deployment bottlenecks, and how a venture firm judges its research agents. The publisher transcript mislabels San Miguel’s answers as Nathan Labenz; attribution here follows the explicit guest introductions and sign-off. The episode discloses cloned-voice narration.

**Why it matters:** These operating accounts expose constraints that capability scores and headline compute prices miss: an agent can satisfy a checklist without doing the intended research, a good forecast can fail in execution, and available hardware can lack a usable site. Theory provides a concrete adoption criterion: faster decisions and more time with people, rather than more generated research. Broad takeoff predictions and repeated Anthropic filing commentary add less incremental evidence.
