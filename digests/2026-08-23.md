### [How to close $100K+ enterprise deals, step by step | Jen Abel](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-close-100k-1m-deals-step-by)

**Lenny's Podcast | relevance 3/5 | [local summary](../episodes/2026-08-23/how-to-close-100k-enterprise-deals-step-by-step-jen-abel-3593dedb1bf3257293e2/summary.md)**

Jen Abel presents enterprise sales as a roughly 15-step orchestration of the customer's buying process rather than the familiar five-stage CRM funnel. For $100,000 to $1 million deals, she recommends engaging the executive sponsor and N-minus-one simultaneously, delaying the demo until the seller has enough internal intelligence to tailor it, and using a small, tightly scripted pilot whose success criteria and post-pilot procurement path are agreed in advance. Her sharpest economic guidance is to align contract value with sales-cycle and implementation cost: a 90-day motion can support a $100,000 deal, while a nine-month motion should command roughly $250,000 to $300,000, and a forward-deployed engineer is difficult to justify at the lower contract value. She also offers practitioner benchmarks of a 25-35% qualified-opportunity win rate and roughly 80% conversion after a properly qualified pilot. These are concrete operating heuristics from an experienced enterprise seller, not audited cohort data.

**Why it matters:** For AI application companies entering enterprise markets, model capability alone may not determine adoption or margins. Abel's framework makes executive sponsorship, buyer-specific narrative, pilot design, procurement timing, and implementation labor explicit components of the business model. It also provides useful tests for forecasts and unit economics: a high headline win rate may indicate underpricing, a long free proof of concept can consume momentum and services capacity, and an FDE-heavy motion requires materially higher ACV. The numerical benchmarks should be treated as hypotheses to validate against a company's own segmented pipeline because the episode supplies no sample, time period, or underlying customer data.
