### [What will computer chips look like in 2045?](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/intel-tour)

Dwarkesh Podcast · Relevance 5/5

Dwarkesh's Intel fab tour connects AI supply constraints to concrete manufacturing decisions. CTO Pushkar Ranade estimates that the existing global tool stock could support roughly doubling Intel's leading-edge wafer output, not a tenfold expansion; that is a conditional estimate, not a ceiling on future compute growth. Engineers explain why backside power changes High-NA EUV economics, why customer-ready design kits and restrained process innovation matter, and how hybrid bonding, ruthenium wiring and stacked transistors could extend scaling. The strongest evidence concerns present integration bottlenecks. The 2045 architectures are research possibilities, not a committed product roadmap.

**Why it matters:** The episode shifts attention from fab construction budgets to qualified equipment, customer design infrastructure and integration yield. Those determine how quickly capital becomes usable AI silicon. It also explains why greater bandwidth and lower communication energy can matter as much as more transistors. Intel's competitive claims remain management claims: the interview supplies neither comparable foundry yields nor customer economics proving parity with TSMC. Ranade refers to mid-2026 during the interview, so later supplier disclosures are necessary context for an October publication.

### [Why AI Compute Comes in Rectangles — Erik Bernhardsson, Modal CEO](https://www.latent.space/p/modal-d)

Latent Space · Relevance 5/5

Modal CEO Erik Bernhardsson describes a company whose elastic software interface increasingly sits on long-term compute commitments. He reports sourcing from almost 25 clouds, often through three-plus-year contracts with upfront payments, while sandboxes have reached roughly a third of revenue and recently kept growing around 30% monthly. The technical counterpart is increasingly granular execution: he estimates an eight-second median sandbox lifetime and wants fast snapshots and forks to separate persistent agents from temporary compute. His account also reveals two costs of serving AI developers: agents keep generating obsolete API syntax, and Modal has expanded its forward-deployed engineering team from about four to 24. These are first-party operating claims, not audited results; the rapid sandbox growth is not a forward projection.

**Why it matters:** Modal offers a useful test of whether software differentiation can sustain attractive economics on top of increasingly capital-intensive GPU supply. Pooling demand can improve utilization, but it leaves the provider carrying commitment and price risk. Sandboxes supply a second substantial product line, while very short executions make startup overhead, snapshotting and scheduling central to the business. The interview also shows how agent adoption changes API maintenance and customer support rather than simply eliminating engineering work.

### [The New Apex Predator: CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz on Adversaries Armed With AI Agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PST8yesYOSo)

No Priors · Relevance 4/5

George Kurtz describes CrowdStrike's effort to combine specialized security models, customer-environment simulations and agent monitoring. The most consequential numbers are his approximately $0.03 versus $10 remediation-cost comparison and a reported shift toward selective use of frontier models. Neither establishes independently measured customer economics. His account of SafeMind is broader than its initial commercial code-review service, while published AIDR documentation places concrete limits on agent visibility and enforcement.

**Why it matters:** The interview makes the incumbent security vendor's AI strategy concrete: use proprietary incident data and existing distribution to sell specialized models and controls over enterprise agents. The relevant tests are comparable remediation quality, actual service availability, coverage of deployed agents and total operating cost. Growing agent activity does not automatically imply a proportional expansion of billable seats or eliminate identity-based attacks.

### [Matthew Prince on PHP2Shell, iPhone Duo Early Reactions, Meta Unveils New Class C Stock | Matthew Prince, Kevin Weil, MC Lader, Dillon Baxter, Richard Starson, Brian Manning](https://share.transistor.fm/s/3dd855a5)

TBPN · Relevance 4/5

TBPN's October 22 episode provides six useful findings across Warp Sciences, Cognition, Xona and Cloudflare. Kevin Weil describes a $215 million effort to build a US robotic chemistry contractor whose experiments can supply scientific AI training data. MC Lader outlines Cognition's move into 14 industry verticals through small teams led by former founders. Brian Manning explains Xona's receiver-first distribution strategy and spacecraft redesign intended to reduce launch cost per satellite. **Why it matters:** These interviews locate bottlenecks outside raw model capability. Warp needs reliable physical experiments and competitive chemistry economics; Cognition needs industry knowledge and implementation capacity; Xona needs receiver adoption and repeatable satellite production. Cloudflare's account adds new attack-traffic telemetry to yesterday's PHP coverage while explicitly preserving the need to patch. Its payments thesis is commercially relevant because Cloudflare is already testing the infrastructure, but a dramatic traffic forecast does not establish future transaction volume or revenue.
