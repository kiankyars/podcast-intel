## TL;DR

- **Boswell estimates PJM could have avoided $12 billion of the $63 billion spent across its last four capacity auctions: $7 billion in 2025-26 and $5 billion in 2026-27, both from crediting existing winter capability more accurately.** [07:39; 09:44; 11:50] ([SemiAnalysis Weekly](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--026---PJMs-12B-Modeling-Mistake-Is-Hitting-Ratepayers-Again-Datacenter--Energy--Robert-Boswell--Jordan-Nanos-e3nkns1)). He says PJM effectively applies summer capability year-round and extrapolates historical cold-weather outages even after mandated winterization, understating cold-air output and reliability. Because the supply curve is near vertical, his modeled quantity correction produces an unusually large reduction in the uniform clearing price; the estimate is not independently audited in the episode.
- **Boswell argues PJM is manufacturing part of its scarcity by running auctions only one to two years ahead while its interconnection process is slow, leaving too little time for new generation to qualify and shifting the supply curve left.** [05:15; 08:57] ([SemiAnalysis Weekly](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--026---PJMs-12B-Modeling-Mistake-Is-Hitting-Ratepayers-Again-Datacenter--Energy--Robert-Boswell--Jordan-Nanos-e3nkns1)). He says the auctions are intended to provide roughly three years of warning, which he already considers tight, but recent delays compressed the interval further. New plants therefore cannot arrive in response to the capacity signal even as forecast demand rises.
- **Boswell explains that PJM's capacity auction can magnify small modeling errors because every cleared generator, including fully paid-off existing plants, receives the marginal systemwide price rather than its own bid.** [03:48; 14:41] ([SemiAnalysis Weekly](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--026---PJMs-12B-Modeling-Mistake-Is-Hitting-Ratepayers-Again-Datacenter--Energy--Robert-Boswell--Jordan-Nanos-e3nkns1)). Boswell explains that the auction pays for the ability to generate, not delivered electricity, and gives the example of a resource bidding $5 per MW-day still receiving $300 per MW-day if the last required unit sets that price. A small quantity change at the steep end of supply can therefore reprice a very large installed base.

## Episodes

### [Ep. 026 - PJM's $12B Modeling Mistake Is Hitting Ratepayers Again (Datacenter, Energy) | Robert Boswell, Jordan Nanos](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/jordan-nanos/episodes/Ep--026---PJMs-12B-Modeling-Mistake-Is-Hitting-Ratepayers-Again-Datacenter--Energy--Robert-Boswell--Jordan-Nanos-e3nkns1)

**SemiAnalysis Weekly | relevance 5/5**

Robert Boswell argues that PJM's recent capacity-price shock is not simply the physical consequence of AI data-center demand. His model attributes $12 billion of avoidable cost across the 2025-26 and 2026-27 auctions to PJM undervaluing existing winter capacity while procuring against a supply curve made unusually steep by short auction lead times and interconnection delays. The same issue now matters for an emergency procurement: PJM sees a 6.8 GW shortfall for 2028-29, while Boswell estimates seasonal capability and winterization reduce the need to roughly 3 GW. The proposed structure could commit capacity through 2042-43 without a guaranteed data-center counterparty, potentially transferring a liability Boswell sizes at up to $21 billion to existing ratepayers. These are consequential, mechanistically explained estimates from SemiAnalysis, but the episode does not independently validate the model or the auction assumptions.

**Why it matters:** PJM covers 66 million people and the core U.S. data-center corridor, so its auction design affects both household power bills and the pace and location of AI infrastructure deployment. If Boswell's estimates hold, a material portion of the price increase commonly attributed to data-center load instead reflects reliability accounting, procurement timing, interconnection bottlenecks, and liability allocation. That changes the investment and policy question from whether demand must be suppressed to whether PJM can credit existing capacity accurately, enable new supply soon enough, and make the customers creating long-term demand bear the corresponding commitment risk.

### [From Restoring Sight to Reimagining the Brain, with Max Hodak](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7HXqMepjvy8)

**No Priors | relevance 4/5**

Science CEO Max Hodak gives a first-party update on Prima's transition from retinal-implant trial to commercial product: European marketing approval arrived in July, initial sales are expected within weeks, and a next-generation version is entering animal studies with a hoped-for human study next year. He also says Science is practically aligning animal neural recordings with internal AI-model representations, while arguing that consumer brain-to-AI interfaces may not escape the brain's roughly 10-bit-per-second cognitive bottleneck.

**Why it matters:** In Hodak's account, the episode separates two BCI investment cases. Prima has reached a European regulatory milestone; Hodak cites vision-restoration comparables as pricing and reimbursement precedents and describes an iterative engineering roadmap, while high-bandwidth consumer thought interfaces may face a cognition-and-attention constraint. Science's claimed representational-alignment work is a concrete example of AI models serving as neuroscience tools, but the episode gives no named model, method, or independently validated result.
