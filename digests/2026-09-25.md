## Episodes

### [Runway’s WorldPrompt and the Engineering of Real-Time Worlds](https://www.latent.space/p/runway)

Latent Space · Relevance 4/5

Runway co-founder and co-CEO Anastasis Germanidis explains the engineering path from video diffusion to interactive world models: autoregressive conversion, step distillation and action conditioning. He reports real-time 24-fps character generation, but separates its roughly 30-minute duration from open-ended worlds that last only a few minutes. He describes embodiment-specific robotics work built on general video pretraining, early pixel-generated interfaces and a need to simulate failures rather than only visually plausible successes. The interview is a technical account of Runway's research and deployed character model, with broader robotics transfer, neural interfaces and model unification still requiring validation.

**Why it matters:** Runway's approach reuses a costly general video backbone across creative tools, interactive characters and robotics simulation, potentially spreading pretraining costs across products. The interview makes the remaining barriers concrete: low-latency serving, accumulated errors, representation of failed actions, embodiment-specific adaptation and the cost of rendering interfaces with continuous inference. Attractive video output is insufficient evidence of a reliable simulator or an economical software replacement.

### [Who Feeds the GPUs? Inside AI's Hidden $30B Layer | Renen Hallak, VAST Data](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/Who-Feeds-the-GPUs--Inside-AIs-Hidden-30B-Layer--Renen-Hallak--VAST-Data-e3palb8)

The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck · Relevance 4/5

VAST Data CEO Renen Hallak describes rapid expansion in customers' storage plans, an existing multi-exabyte cluster, strong customer retention, and a profitable software model. The most consequential product development is DataEnclave, intended to bring proprietary models into enterprise-controlled environments while protecting both weights and data. VAST's primary announcement clarifies that it is a preview with shipment planned for Q1 2027. Customer demand, deployment scale, retention, and profitability remain executive-reported; the interview does not quantify revenue, bookings, backlog, or audited margins.

**Why it matters:** The interview strengthens the case for a software supplier benefiting from AI infrastructure growth without owning the underlying compute fleet. It also identifies a potential expansion from storage into model distribution and governance. Capacity plans must convert into funded orders, while DataEnclave must reach shipment and customer deployment before its promised market expansion can be counted as realized business.
