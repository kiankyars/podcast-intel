# 2026-11-24

This edition covers episodes available by the scheduled November 24 check. It was assembled on November 25.

## TL;DR

- **Worldcraft used about 250 agents to improve one shared generator against thousands of real locations.** Simonian says the generator combines smaller models and rules. It uses OpenStreetMap building classifications to furnish interiors, so an office may get cubicles even when the real layout is unknown. Serving the world posed a separate problem. The server crashed almost immediately after launching with a 100-player cap. Simonian then worked with experienced Minecraft operators and estimates the rebuilt system can handle thousands of players. He gives no measured peak concurrency. [TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/ca7b9f75) [01:45:13; 01:45:38; 01:46:56; 01:47:23; 01:47:36; 01:48:53; 01:49:08]
- **Linq is planning regional infrastructure expansion to cut latency for voice agents.** Potter says Linq owns and operates hardware, started in Alabama and has about 90% of its customers on the West Coast. Its new wideband calling codec expands the audio frequencies available to an agent. Bringing infrastructure closer to customers addresses a different part of the conversation experience, the delay on each turn. The interview gives no before-and-after latency measurements. Linq also [announced a $90 million Series B](https://linqapp.com/blog/linq-raises-90m-welcomes-ben-horowitz-to-the-board). [TBPN](https://share.transistor.fm/s/ca7b9f75) [01:56:16; 01:56:41; 01:56:57; 01:57:02; 01:57:11; 01:57:15; 01:57:26]
- **Linq sees a checkout advantage for agents offered by businesses that already hold a customer's address, preferences and payment method.** Potter names DoorDash as a customer and contrasts its stored information with the onboarding a general-purpose agent faces on a first purchase. He says a conversation carried over Linq can capture a user's request and identifies reliable verification that a human approved a particular charge as an open problem. ## More findings

- **Supertake's AI can propose a portfolio for a thesis even when the available instruments offer a poor way to express it.** Murr describes connecting brokerage accounts and building a portfolio conversationally. His US soccer example illustrates the limitation. A media-rights holder can make most of its money elsewhere, so its shares can rise even if soccer viewership falls. Supertake lacked short positions at interview time. Murr presents contracts tied to a specific sport's viewership as a possible future solution and says the business model is still undecided.
