## TL;DR

- **Cacioppo says Vanta is absorbing token costs and accepting lower margins while roughly 25% of customers use its agent workflow; reaching 75% is a goal, with pricing to be resolved later.** [09:40; 10:40; 10:59; 14:39] ([Invest Like the Best](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQaM5oPlUYs)). She argues that marked-up token pricing invites customers to compare an application directly with its underlying model. Vanta is prioritizing usage and expects better retention as agents do more work, but she supplies no measured retention uplift or margin bridge. At 14:39 she also distinguishes charging for completed outputs from true outcomes and says a menu of many billable tasks creates cognitive overhead. The 25% is her approximate current adoption figure; 75% and the commercial payoff are aspirations. Vanta's [September guide](https://help.vanta.com/en/articles/16397022-create-schedule-and-manage-custom-agents) documents scheduled agents with creator permissions and approval gates, but does not verify adoption or retention figures.
- **Conrad reports meaningful resale recovery from a still-small order book: customers recover about 25% of spend, while only 5% of SF Compute capacity is on the book.** [05:45; 06:21] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/sfcompute2)). The 5% figure is his interview snapshot. SF Compute's September 9 [realized-discount announcement](https://sfcompute.com/news/realized-discount) also reports average resale proceeds equal to 25% of on-platform compute purchases. It does not publish cohort size or a distribution of outcomes. This is recovery of unused capacity costs, not a universal reduction in the price of an equivalent workload or evidence of deep market-wide liquidity.
- **Lieberman estimates that only about 5% of roughly 100 enterprises Tenex is talking to have production agents doing non-engineering work, despite about 90% having rolled out a general-purpose LLM.** [19:45; 20:21; 20:57] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/tenex)). He places training and enablement at roughly 60% and meaningful coding-agent rollout at roughly 50%, with the largest drop occurring before non-engineering production deployment. This is a directional account of Tenex's commercial conversations, with approximate definitions and a selected sample, not a representative enterprise adoption survey. It supports demand for deployment services more directly than a claim that enterprises lack access to capable models.

[Current documentation](https://docs.sfcompute.com/preview/networking/infiniband-partitions) marks the feature public preview, requires instances to share one physical fabric, and says moving an instance to another partition requires terminating and recreating it. This makes cluster configuration programmable; it does not establish interruption-free repartitioning or connectivity across separate fabrics.
- **Rives expects biological AI to progress through repeated experiment-and-feedback cycles, but says reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards needs laboratory verification infrastructure that software domains largely avoid.** [43:08] ([The Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/a-world-model-of-life-biohub-s-alex-rives-on-virtual-cells-measurement-the-1-8b-data-push/)). At 43:08 he describes a system proposing a question, performing an experiment, learning from the result and proposing a better experiment. He contrasts that ambition with rapid computational checks in coding and mathematics. No working end-to-end system, iteration throughput, reward reliability or measured improvement curve is presented. The implication is an engineering dependency on experimental feedback, not demonstrated recursive scientific acceleration.

He identifies paying for non-coding work as the bigger design problem. Hess estimates output roughly doubled this year but says the company revisits story-point criteria at least quarterly. No stable workload benchmark or quality-adjusted productivity series is supplied. Her rationale for emphasizing quality is that Vanta's decision-and-action workloads consume fewer tokens than coding workloads. This is a description of Vanta's evaluation process and relative usage, not a published reliability benchmark or a quantified cross-industry cost comparison. - **Cacioppo questions whether expensive forward-deployed engineering can be sustained on five- and six-figure contracts, even if the title helps attract exceptional talent.** [34:36; 34:43; 35:15; 35:30] ([Invest Like the Best](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQaM5oPlUYs)). This is her assessment of service-delivery economics, without customer-level costs or margins. It does not establish that all forward-deployed teams lose money: the relevant test is engineering effort and reuse relative to contract value and renewal revenue.

## Episodes

### [Christina Cacioppo - Building an Enduring Software Company in the Age of AI - [Invest Like the Best, EP.496]](https://colossus.com/episode/becoming-a-category-winner/)

Invest Like the Best · Relevance 4/5

Vanta CEO Christina Cacioppo describes a deliberate trade-off between current margins and agent adoption: the company is absorbing token costs while trying to move more customers into workflows where agents prepare and execute work with permission. She reports roughly 25% adoption of that workflow and identifies domain-specific evaluations, codebase modularization and organizational habits as practical constraints. Her account also connects growing compensation for exceptional employees with competition from AI companies, and outlines expansion from compliance into automated security work for existing customers. The operational figures are management estimates; retention gains and broader security automation remain ambitions. Timestamps refer to the [official video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQaM5oPlUYs), whose edit differs from the podcast audio.

**Why it matters:** Vanta offers a concrete example of an established software company financing an agent transition from its existing business. Adoption, inference cost and pricing must be tested together: a lower current margin can fund a better product, but the interview does not establish the eventual retention or monetization payoff. Its accumulated integrations and customer relationships may help distribute security automation, while domain evaluation, engineering context and buyer approval processes still constrain deployment.

### [The Programmable Data Center — Evan Conrad, SF Compute](https://www.latent.space/p/sfcompute2)

Latent Space · Relevance 4/5

Evan Conrad describes SF Compute's move from GPU subleasing into operating clusters and settling trades in usable compute. The most useful evidence is an early resale metric, the constraints preventing GPU-hours from becoming fully interchangeable, and self-service InfiniBand partitions with narrower capabilities than the interview's 'on the fly' phrasing suggests. His financing and market-neutrality arguments are strategic theses, not proof that competing venues cannot succeed.

**Why it matters:** A compute exchange's value depends on whether customers can recover unused commitments and receive equivalent, reliable capacity. That makes storage, networking, control-plane operations and contractual interruption rights part of market design. Investors and infrastructure buyers should distinguish signed contract value, actual trading liquidity and repeatable customer savings, and evaluate which physical constraints the software truly removes.

### [AI for the Fortune 2000: How Tenex Builds an AI-Native SDLC — Alex Lieberman & CJ Hess](https://www.latent.space/p/tenex)

Latent Space · Relevance 4/5

Tenex's Alex Lieberman and CJ Hess describe a 75-person applied-AI business whose customers are evenly split between Fortune 2000 companies and mid-market firms. Their most useful evidence concerns the gap between buying AI access and deploying production agents, the changing economics of paying engineers for output, and a shared engineering process that stores project decisions alongside code. The operating numbers are management estimates, not audited results: reported output growth is particularly hard to normalize because Tenex recalibrates its story-point definitions.

**Why it matters:** The interview makes the implementation bottleneck concrete: enterprises need agreed processes, access controls and durable project context before more capable agents translate into reliable work. For investors and operators, it also separates three economics that are often conflated: employee output, inference expense and the continuing cost of owning customized software.

### [A World Model of Life: Biohub's Alex Rives on Virtual Cells, Measurement, & the $1.8B Data Push](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/a-world-model-of-life-biohub-s-alex-rives-on-virtual-cells-measurement-the-1-8b-data-push/)

The Cognitive Revolution · Relevance 4/5

Biohub Head of Science Alex Rives describes a research strategy centered on generating measurements that present biological models cannot learn from existing data. The Virtual Biology Initiative aims to connect molecular readouts, imaging and tissue context at very large scale, while investing in instruments that reveal previously inaccessible information. His nearer-term model is a probabilistic predictor of cellular state transitions; a complete physical simulation remains distant. The interview supplies concrete priorities and research forecasts, rather than evidence that virtual cells can already predict human treatment outcomes. The $1.8 billion resource commitment was announced October 7 and includes earlier investments and existing data assets.

**Why it matters:** Biohub is concentrating capital on the experimental data and measurement layer needed for biological foundation models. Rives's account suggests that compute scaling alone leaves a substantial bottleneck: observations must capture relevant cellular context, and proposed interventions eventually require laboratory verification. The useful milestones are released multimodal datasets, out-of-distribution perturbation accuracy and reproducible experimental feedback loops. Clinical benefit and the five-year modeling ambition remain separate, unproven outcomes.
