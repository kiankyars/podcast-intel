# 2026-10-10

## TL;DR

- **Pavlo says an unnamed large bank raised text-to-SQL accuracy from roughly 60% to 99.5% only after years and millions of dollars invested in semantic context, making enterprise data definitions a major part of the product rather than assuming an MCP connection solves correctness.** [24:24;24:39;24:51;25:00;25:08;25:24;26:19;26:32] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/What-Happens-When-Billions-of-AI-Agents-Hit-Your-Database--Andy-Pavlo-e3q4qu7)). He recounts a conversation the previous day: an off-the-shelf system, subsequently refined internally, initially produced the right query and answer around 60% of the time; a richer semantic/context layer eventually produced the reported 99.5%. He explicitly says ordinary downloaded tools are not at that level. This is secondhand evidence with no named bank, evaluation set, coverage denominator or cost breakdown, so it cannot establish general text-to-SQL accuracy. His later MCP discussion separates standardized access to data from inferring its business meaning. The implication is to budget for semantic modeling and workload-specific evaluation, not just model or connector selection.
- **Laskin says Beam consumed more compute in reinforcement learning than pretraining, and that controlling the base model became necessary for Reflection's RL strategy.** [06:00; 06:42; 07:20; 12:25; 12:31; 12:37; 12:50; 12:57; 13:47; 13:55] ([No Priors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=up4sG9RM20M)). Reflection initially intended to apply RL to other labs' open models, then decided to build end to end because Laskin found pretraining and scalable RL tightly coupled. He describes roughly 6,000 GB300s for several weeks of pretraining and more than 10,000 for four weeks of RL. His approximately 12-day pretraining figure describes what improved infrastructure could now achieve, not the original run's elapsed time. The [launch post](https://reflection.ai/blog/introducing-beam) specifies 6,144 GPUs for pretraining under four weeks and 10.5K for four weeks of RL, consistent with his rounded account. He reports continuing gains when training ended; this is an observation over that run, not evidence that scaling cannot saturate. The implication is a large post-training compute and agent-environment budget, rather than inexpensive fine-tuning of a finished base.
- **Beam's claimed 3–4× reasoning efficiency is a generation-compute comparison, not demonstrated 3–4× lower customer bills or latency.** [19:55; 19:59; 20:07; 20:13; 20:27; 20:31; 20:43; 20:49; 21:02; 21:40] ([No Priors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=up4sG9RM20M)). Laskin connects shorter agent solutions to lower cost and faster completion, reports 3–4× efficiency against similar-capability models and roughly 10× against some larger models, and credits a strong base plus large-scale RL. The [published methodology](https://reflection.ai/blog/introducing-beam) estimates forward-pass FLOPs as twice active parameters times generated tokens, including reasoning and answers; it excludes prompt prefill, context-dependent attention and serving overhead. Its headline 3–4× comparator is GLM-5.2, and the company acknowledges stronger open models on raw capability. This supports a promising efficiency hypothesis, not universal frontier leadership or measured production savings. Workload completion rate, hardware utilization and total serving cost still require customer tests; Laskin supplies no comparable deployment bill or wall-clock study here.

## More findings

- **Pavlo reports that LLMs can deliver a useful first database-tuning configuration in about 15 minutes, reaching his rough estimate of 80–85% of the desired tuning result; specialized optimization remains valuable for the remaining performance.** [33:15;33:33;33:50;33:59;34:18;34:31;34:38;34:54;35:19;35:31] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/What-Happens-When-Billions-of-AI-Agents-Hit-Your-Database--Andy-Pavlo-e3q4qu7)). Earlier learned tuners struggled to obtain representative production data and transfer results from staging hardware and workloads. He says LLMs bring documentation and best-practice knowledge that can quickly improve common settings, especially for customers mistakenly assuming cloud defaults were already tuned. The interview does not define the 85% denominator, model, workload or benchmark; it is not an 85% speedup or a measured rate of autonomous DBA replacement. His related [Booster paper](https://db.cs.cmu.edu/papers/2026/zhang-sigmod2026.pdf) uses historical tuning artifacts, LLM suggestions and beam search to assist existing tuners on PostgreSQL analytical workloads. That supports a combined approach, but does not independently establish the interview's exact 15-minute/85% shorthand.
- **Pavlo forecasts a potential 10–100× increase in database query volume from continuously running agents, while saying he has not yet established that agent queries require fundamentally different database engines.** [04:42;05:46;06:22;08:53;09:02;11:04;12:51;13:43;13:52;14:01;14:08;14:55;01:07:37] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/What-Happens-When-Billions-of-AI-Agents-Hit-Your-Database--Andy-Pavlo-e3q4qu7)). The mechanism is many concurrent agents per person, mixed reads and writes, and additional agent telemetry. He reports seeing increasing volume at ClickHouse and hearing similar reports elsewhere, but supplies no measured growth series or horizon for 10–100×; he says that level has not arrived. He is unsure whether agents generate simpler exploratory queries or more complex joins than human workflows. His concrete near-term prescription is isolated copy-on-write development branches plus database permissions, which scale beyond approving each action. [Neon's official 80% statistic](https://neon.com/about-us) counts databases deployed by automated agents: it is a provisioning measure, not evidence of equivalent production query or revenue growth. [Neon's branching description](https://neon.com/blog/branching-as-the-new-standard-for-relational-databases) confirms that branches share storage until changes occur.
- **Pavlo says ClickHouse is exploring GPU query acceleration, but frames compatibility, data movement and cost as unresolved research questions; Sirius illustrates adding GPU execution beneath existing database interfaces rather than replacing the whole database.** [01:03:49;01:04:42;01:04:53;01:05:03;01:05:24;01:05:30;01:05:41;01:06:04;01:06:13] ([The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/What-Happens-When-Billions-of-AI-Agents-Hit-Your-Database--Andy-Pavlo-e3q4qu7)). He attributes earlier GPU-database difficulties to fitting data in GPU memory and PCIe transfer costs, then describes Sirius as an execution layer for existing systems. For ClickHouse, translating data layouts and managing shared memory remain exploratory; no shipped integration or customer savings are announced. The [Sirius repository](https://github.com/sirius-db/sirius) documents DuckDB support, StarRocks as forthcoming, CPU fallback for unsupported operators and tiered GPU/host/disk execution, so the historical all-data-in-GPU limitation should not be treated as a current universal constraint. Its [research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.04701) reports workload-specific TPC-H results, not universal economics. The consequential question is cost per completed query including transfers and fallbacks, rather than GPU arithmetic throughput alone.
- **Reflection intends open weights and deployment services to create demand for an inference business spanning the software between enterprise applications and bare metal.** [22:51; 23:00; 23:09; 23:15; 24:15; 24:25; 24:35; 24:43; 24:49; 24:56; 30:22; 30:30; 30:42; 31:21] ([No Priors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=up4sG9RM20M)). Laskin says token purchases bundle model, harness, inference software, cluster management and GPUs. Enterprise ownership still needs those components; Reflection supplies the surrounding tools and helps identify valuable workflows that drive recurring inference demand. He says selling inference alone has not worked in his experience, and gives an on-premises Dell infrastructure scenario rather than a named customer's measured migration result. The [enterprise offering](https://reflection.ai/enterprise-solutions) likewise lists infrastructure, agent software and forward-deployed implementation. Availability needs qualification: [developer docs](https://developers.reflection.ai/introduction) describe a waitlisted API beta, while the [launch post](https://reflection.ai/blog/introducing-beam) promises Apache 2.0 weights later in October. Public self-hosting should therefore not be treated as already delivered. No revenue, margin, utilization or service-cost figures establish this business model's economics yet.
- **Laskin expects most enterprise open-model usage to start with customized agent systems, with model fine-tuning following later; he also reports incomplete generalization between agent harnesses.** [18:10; 18:22; 18:29; 19:14; 19:19; 19:22; 19:32; 27:16; 27:25; 27:29; 28:00; 28:14; 28:24; 28:44; 29:11] ([No Priors](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=up4sG9RM20M)). He says capability does not transfer cleanly even across Terminal Bench versions and harnesses, although suitable data can make adaptation fast. His customer recipe starts with a useful task evaluation, then generates synthetic training data approximating that task; it is not automatically training on the customer's private records. For traditional enterprises he predicts adapting a harness around an unchanged open model before fine-tuning, because existing systems lack the native data-collection loops of AI-first products. He contrasts this with an unnamed inference providers' greater-than-90% customization statistic, which he explicitly limits to a customer mix dominated by AI-native companies; it is not a measured enterprise-wide rate. The practical implication is to test the actual workflow and harness before attributing a benchmark score to a deployable application. The adoption sequence remains his forecast, supported by customer conversations rather than a representative survey.
- **DeepMind’s experience with cell-by-gene data points to a measurement bottleneck for virtual cells; scaling readily available biological datasets alone is not a demonstrated route to the goal.** [00:01:00; 00:02:32; 00:04:54] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/biohub-deepmind)). At 00:04:54, Kohli says that after substantial work on cell-by-gene data, DeepMind concluded the data was insufficient for its virtual-cell ambition. At 00:01:00 and 00:02:32, Candido stresses finding a task-relevant scaling law and co-designing data generation with modelers; he also reports useful protein-language-model training from noisy metagenomic sequences, without specifying a benchmark. Separate context: [Biohub’s October 7 initiative expansion](https://biohub.org/news/virtual-biology-initiative-expansion/) emphasizes coordinated multimodal measurements, shared standards, and data infrastructure. That announcement supplies program context, not validation that a predictive virtual cell has been built.
- **Kohli identifies raw cryo-EM micrographs as a possible route beyond static protein structures, while explicitly saying his attempt still needs more work.** [00:15:28; 00:17:54; 00:19:00] ([Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/biohub-deepmind)). ### [Beam: The Great American Open Model with ReflectionAI Co-Founder and CEO Misha Laskin](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=up4sG9RM20M)

No Priors · Relevance 4/5

Reflection CEO Misha Laskin gives a concrete account of Beam's training economics: roughly 6,000 GB300s for pretraining versus more than 10,000 for four weeks of reinforcement learning. He explains why Reflection moved from adapting other labs' models to training its own base, how it intends to monetize enterprise inference infrastructure and deployment work, and why customer-specific evaluations and harnesses matter despite strong general agent capabilities. Beam's advertised efficiency needs careful interpretation, and public weights are still promised rather than available.

**Why it matters:** The interview makes the open-model business concrete: expensive RL produces a potentially efficient workhorse, but revenue depends on supplying compute, operating software and solving customer workflows. The useful investment distinction is between competitive benchmark capability, estimated generation efficiency and proven deployment economics. Laskin also identifies a deployment labor constraint: enterprises need people who can turn business tasks into evaluations and reliable agent systems.

### [What Happens When Billions of AI Agents Hit Your Database? (Andy Pavlo)](https://podcasters.spotify.com/pod/show/firstmark/episodes/What-Happens-When-Billions-of-AI-Agents-Hit-Your-Database--Andy-Pavlo-e3q4qu7)

The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck · Relevance 4/5

Andy Pavlo, CMU database researcher and recently appointed head of ClickHouse Labs, argues that agents first change database volume, development workflows and operating costs; a fundamentally new database architecture is still an open research question. He reports rapid, useful LLM tuning and widespread disclosed coding-agent contributions, while separating those advances from production correctness. His most striking text-to-SQL example is an unnamed bank that reportedly spent years and millions of dollars building semantic context. GPU acceleration and combining Postgres with analytics offer concrete architectural opportunities, but workload economics, product maturity and organizational ownership still matter.

**Why it matters:** The useful investment and engineering distinction is between agents increasing demand for existing database capabilities and proving a new engine is necessary. Pavlo identifies near-term value in semantic context, isolated database branches, permissions, tuning and integrated developer workflows. His numbers have different evidentiary strength: tuning is a first-party research impression, bank accuracy is secondhand, disclosed AI commits measure project adoption, and 10–100× query growth is a forecast. Pavlo now works for ClickHouse, and host Matt Turck discloses that FirstMark invests in the company.

### [Why AlphaFold Didn't Solve Protein Folding — Pushmeet Kohli, Google DeepMind & Sal Candido, Biohub](https://www.latent.space/p/biohub-deepmind)

Latent Space · Relevance 3/5

Pushmeet Kohli and Sal Candido explain why progress beyond AlphaFold depends on measuring the right biology: existing cell-by-gene data was insufficient for DeepMind’s virtual-cell ambition, static structure prediction does not recover protein dynamics, and useful scientific models need calibrated uncertainty. This is a technically useful account of research constraints, with no new model benchmark or demonstrated drug-development speedup.

**Why it matters:** The actionable constraint for AI biology is the information captured by experiments, not simply the volume of data or compute. Model builders and funders should ask which missing measurements enable the intended prediction, how uncertainty is validated, and which discovery stage actually improves. The panel offers concrete failure experience and research directions rather than evidence of an end-to-end clinical breakthrough.
